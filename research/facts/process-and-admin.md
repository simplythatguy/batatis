# Process and administration of alternative solutions in Alberta

## Plain-language summary
An Alberta building alternative solution uses evidence to show that a proposed departure from the usual code recipe still meets its required performance. The usual recipe is an "acceptable solution" in Division B. The comparison covers the objectives and functional statements attributed to the applicable acceptable solutions. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.2.1.1.(1) and Sentence 1.2.1.1.(2) [PDF p.38].]

Alberta administers this route through written variances. An Administrator or a safety codes officer, abbreviated SCO, may issue a variance if satisfied that it provides approximately equivalent or greater safety performance for persons and property. The power is discretionary. A completed form does not create an entitlement to approval. [Safety Codes Act, s.38(1), file p.31; SCC FAQ, file p.1.]

The applicant develops the case and supplies the evidence. The SCO assesses it. Registered professionals remain responsible for professional design and analysis. [SCC 2019 notice, file pp.1-2.]

The SCC form puts applicant and site details in Sections A and B, the code comparison in Section C, and the supporting-document register in Section D. The detailed code analysis and any operating, maintenance and commissioning requirements are attachments to that form. [SCC form, file pp.1-2; NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739].]

A local variance can cover one site or multiple sites within the issuer's jurisdiction. Local filing policies can be narrower. A variance issued by the Provincial Building Administrator under the code applies throughout Alberta. [SCC 2019 notice, file p.2; NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.2.1.2. [PDF p.729].]

A variance can carry conditions. Permit, inspection and continuing maintenance obligations still matter. [Safety Codes Act, s.38(2), file p.31; NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.2.10.1. [PDF p.735]; Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739].]

An SCO refusal must be written. The applicant can appeal to the Safety Codes Council within 30 days after receiving the refusal, with the current $500 filing fee. An Administrator's variance decision is final under the Act and has no Council appeal. [Safety Codes Act, s.38(5)-(7), file pp.31-32; SCC appeals page, accessed 2026-10-18, S08.]

The tool's role is to identify missing facts, documents and valid citations. It cannot decide that the proposed design has equivalent safety performance. [Challenge scope, file pp.1, 3.]

## When this topic applies

### Concrete trigger signals

- An owner, designer, contractor or manufacturer asks how to submit an Alberta building alternative solution, obtain a variance, find the correct issuer, or complete the SCC form.

- The proposed design departs from an identified Division B acceptable solution and needs the performance comparison required by NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Clause 1.2.1.1.(1)(b) [PDF p.38].
- A submission needs a code-analysis attachment, named design responsibility, operating conditions, commissioning requirements, or coordination between several designers under NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739].

- The request concerns a site-specific or multiple-site variance, an issued condition, Council registration, an SCO refusal, or a possible appeal. [Safety Codes Act, s.38, file pp.31-32; SCC 2019 notice, file p.2.]
- The applicant has an unfamiliar local form, a permit number is missing, or Calgary and Edmonton submission routes differ. [Calgary guide, file pp.1-3; Edmonton emergency-services guide, file p.3.]

### Near-misses and routing boundaries

- A design already satisfies an applicable acceptable solution or a permitted exception may need ordinary permit documentation rather than a variance. Check that applicability first. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Clause 1.2.1.1.(1)(a) [PDF p.38].]

- A dispute about the meaning of wording may be an interpretation request. The Provincial Building Administrator's written interpretation route is distinct from the variance route. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.2.1.3. [PDF p.729].]
- A zoning setback, land-use approval or development variance needs the planning authority's separate process. This sheet addresses the Safety Codes Act route and does not establish planning permission. A variance can address only a thing, process or activity regulated by that Act. [SCC 2019 notice, file p.1.]

- A barrier-free relaxation has a separate code provision and responsible provincial Administrators. Route the accessibility analysis to [occupancy-and-accessibility](occupancy-and-accessibility.md). [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.2.1.4. [PDF p.730].]
- A manufacturer's evaluation report or certification is evidence with its own scope. It does not by itself identify the local variance or permit route. Route evidence questions to [evidence-and-conformity](evidence-and-conformity.md). [SCC 2019 notice, file p.1; Calgary guide, file p.1.]

- A question about which specific egress, fire-separation, structural or other recipe is affected belongs with that technical topic as well as this procedure. The administration provisions themselves are not a substitute benchmark for Section C.
- An appeal of an order or a permit refusal is a different appeal category. The SCC page lists 35 days for orders and 30 days for written variance refusals. Do not substitute the order deadline for the variance deadline. [SCC appeals page, S08, accessed 2026-10-18.]

### Part 9 and Part 3

Division C Part 2 applies to all buildings covered by the code, so a small building has alternative-solution documentation duties too. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentence 2.1.1.1.(1) [PDF p.729].]

Part 9 generally covers buildings no more than three storeys and no more than 600 m2 in building area in the occupancies enumerated by its application clause, including Group B, Division 4 home-type care. These limits do not mean every building below them is Part 9. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) [PDF p.40].]

The Part 3 and related Parts application clause covers specified occupancies and larger buildings, subject to its stated exception. Classify the actual project before selecting its Division B benchmark. Professional-involvement rules require a separate check. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.3.3.2. [PDF pp.39-40]; Div C, Article 2.4.1.1. [PDF pp.739-740].]

### The six documentation duties

All six items below are from NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739].

| Sentence | Required content | Usual location in the SCC submission |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The person requesting the alternative supplies documentation showing code compliance. | Part 1 and its referenced attachments. |
| 2(a) | Code analysis identifies analytical methods and rationales for the minimum-performance comparison. | Section C summary and the detailed analysis listed in Section D. |
| 2(b) | Special maintenance, operation and component-commissioning requirements needed after construction. | Analysis attachment, operating/maintenance plan, commissioning plan and proposed conditions. |
| 3 | Applicable objectives, functions, acceptable solutions, assumptions, limits, tests, engineering studies and performance parameters. | Section C table and an attachment that explains the proposed departure and comparison. |
| 4 | Qualifications, experience and background of those taking design responsibility. | Section D responsibility register plus a qualifications statement in the analysis. |
| 5 | Enough detail to communicate design intent and support the analysis's validity, accuracy, relevance and precision. | Traceable calculations, drawings, reports, scope limits and named revisions. |
| 6 | One identified coordinator when different people are responsible for different design aspects. | Named coordinator in the analysis or cover sheet, with a responsibility matrix. |

The last column is a proposed content mapping, not wording imposed by the code. Sections A and B identify who and where; they do not discharge the detailed analytical duties. A table of objective codes without an analysis is incomplete.

### Permit application remains a separate record

Permit Regulation, s.20, requires a form and manner acceptable to the permit issuer. Its information includes the use, location, owner's name and mailing address, undertaking, applicant contact and credentials, signature, fee information, building occupancy and value, temporary duration if applicable, and further plans or verification information requested by the issuer. [Permit Regulation, file pp.17-18.]

The SCC form does not contain dedicated fields for all that permit information. An SCC-form completeness result therefore cannot certify the completeness of a permit application. Permit terms may address occupancy, expiry, scope, location, qualifications and inspection before concealment. [Permit Regulation, s.22, file p.19.]

### Who is the authority having jurisdiction?

"Authority having jurisdiction", abbreviated AHJ, is the administering authority for the actual site and discipline. The Safety Codes Act allocates administration through accreditation orders and ASCA. It also provides for accredited agencies to supply services. [Safety Codes Act, s.13, file p.15; ss.30 and 30.1, file p.26; s.33, file p.28.]

| Project situation | Routing evidence and checks |
|---|---|
| Municipality accredited in the building discipline | Start with its permit office or assigned building SCO. It may use its own staff or an accredited service agency. Verify the discipline and accreditation scope rather than assuming all local services are municipal. |
| Unaccredited municipality for the relevant service | The SCC permit locator directs applicants to agencies providing inspection services for the Alberta Safety Codes Authority, abbreviated ASCA. Verify the contracted agency for the location. [S09.] |
| Accredited regional services commission or corporation | Its accreditation order and service scope matter. Do not assume the nearest city's officer has jurisdiction. [Act, s.13, file p.15; s.33, file p.28.] |
| Agency performs the review | Record both the administering organization and reviewing agency. The SCC reviewer form has separate fields for them. [SCC form, file pp.3-4.] |
| Proposal intended to apply across Alberta | Use the Provincial Building Administrator route and demonstrate a province-wide issue. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.2.1.2. [PDF p.729]; Note A-2.2.1.2. [PDF p.743].] |

The challenge describes more than 430 jurisdictions. That is challenge background, not a verified live count of building-discipline authorities. The SCC locator and accreditation search are the practical sources for a specific current destination. [Challenge scope, file p.1; S09.]

### End-to-end record and decision sequence

1. Identify the owner, representative, exact site, proposed use, project stage, applicable edition and responsible permit office.

2. Identify the acceptable solution, the precise departure, applicable exceptions, and whether a variance or another process is needed.
3. Discuss the route, document format, professional involvement and technical scope with the responsible SCO before completing a complex analysis. Calgary expressly starts with this discussion; Edmonton encourages early discussion of uncertain emergency-services assumptions. [Calgary guide, file pp.1-2; Edmonton guide, file pp.3, 21.]

4. Prepare Part 1, the code analysis and the supporting documents. Identify each responsible person and, where needed, one coordinator. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739].]
5. Submit through the local permit/variance process. Record submitted file names, revisions, date, recipient, permit linkage and any local fee requirement. [Calgary guide, file pp.2-3; Permit Regulation, s.20, file pp.17-18.]

6. The SCO reviews, seeks more information if needed, then issues a variance or written refusal. Only the issuer makes the safety-performance decision. [SCC FAQ, file p.1; Act, s.38, file pp.31-32.]
7. For an issued variance, preserve scope, conditions, signature and date. Notify the Administrator of Accreditation through the SCC registry process. The Act places the notification duty on the issuing SCO. [Act, s.38(3), file p.31; SCC 2019 notice, file p.2.]

8. Implement and maintain the issued conditions alongside the permit and inspection duties. Retain the analysis for future ownership, use or design changes. [Act, ss.5 and 10, file pp.13-14; NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Note A-2.3.1. [PDF p.744], explanatory note.]
9. For an SCO refusal, capture receipt/service information and decide whether to revise, resubmit or appeal within the applicable time. An appeal does not create permission to build the refused design. [Act, s.38(6)-(7), file p.32; SCC FAQ, file p.2; SCC appeals page, S08.]

### Scope and registration limits

The SCC 2019 notice allows one variance for multiple sites within an SCO's jurisdiction when the thing, process or activity and conditions are the same. An accredited agency works within the jurisdiction of the accredited organization it serves. This does not make a local decision portable to another jurisdiction. [SCC 2019 notice, file p.2.]

Calgary's November 2023 guide requires a separate request for each permit or site and a separate form for each different issue. This is a local submission rule, even though the provincial variance power is broader. Its standing-posted-variance process is a separate local route described under Precedents. [Calgary guide, file p.2.]

The live SCC Variances page says the registry needs a copy of the signed variance and no longer requires supporting documentation. The downloaded SCC form's header still asks for the entire form and supporting documentation. This is a genuine source conflict. The deciding AHJ needs the complete evidence package regardless of the registry's reduced intake. Preserve separate "submitted for decision" and "sent to registry" records and confirm the current registry instructions before forwarding personal or professional material. [SCC form, file p.1; S07, accessed 2026-10-18.]

## Usual mitigations and evidence

### What the package needs to establish

The analysis must identify the affected acceptable solutions and the performance comparison, assumptions, limits, testing, engineering studies and responsible people. It must also address any special continuing operation, maintenance and commissioning requirements. These are code duties, not optional presentation improvements. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739].]

The Alberta explanatory note cautions that qualitative objectives alone do not establish equivalent performance. The comparison must account for all applicable acceptable solutions, including interactions outside the most obvious provision. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Note A-1.2.1.1.(1)(b) [PDF p.57], explanatory context.]

Useful submission checks include:

- The proposed departure is specific enough to distinguish it from the compliant benchmark.

- Drawings, calculations, product/test evidence and the Section C table refer to the same design and revisions.
- The author identifies the limits of a test report, evaluation or precedent and explains why it is relevant to the actual installation.

- Proposed mitigations are included in the design, the analysis and any operating or maintenance plan on which performance depends.
- Each specialized analysis has an identified competent author and the required professional authentication.

- Assumptions that depend on another organization, such as fire-service operations, have a documented basis rather than an invented capability.

These checks operationalize the documentation duties. They cannot decide that a mitigation is adequate. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739]; Edmonton emergency-services guide, file pp.3, 20-22.]

### Evidence confidence ladder

This is a proposed way to describe evidence status, not a ranking published by SCC or a rule that one category guarantees approval.

| Status | What it establishes | What remains to be assessed |
|---|---|---|
| Applicant assertion or product brochure | What is being claimed. | Independent basis, applicability, limits and code-performance comparison. |
| Prior guide, published example or another jurisdiction's decision | A possible analytical approach or a bounded precedent. | Current edition, issuer, legal reach, exact conditions and relevance to this design. |
| Traceable test, certification or evaluation report | Results or conformity within the report's stated scope. | Exact specimen/product identity, installation, exposure, limitations, edition and applicability to the whole proposed system. |
| Project-specific authenticated analysis by the responsible qualified professional | Named professional responsibility for the specified analysis and design scope. | Whether the analysis demonstrates the necessary performance and satisfies the reviewing authority. |
| Issued written variance for the actual scope | The issuer's decision, conditions and authorized scope. | Permit compliance, implementation, inspection and continuing compliance with conditions. |

SCC's notice specifically calls for due diligence with innovative products or systems without a recognized standard or evaluation-service listing. A professional report or evaluation does not require the SCO to accept a proposal. [SCC 2019 notice, file pp.1-2.]

### Standards and operation evidence

There is no universal "variance test standard" in the administration article. Relevant testing procedures and engineering studies are identified through the technical benchmark and proposed alternative. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentence 2.3.1.1.(3) [PDF p.739].]

One verified example is ASTM F2090, Standard Specification for Window Fall Prevention Devices with Emergency Escape (Egress) Release Mechanisms, named in STANDATA 23-BCV-001, file pp.1, 3. The STANDATA's condition and project applicability still govern. This research did not examine the full proprietary test standard or any manufacturer's test report.

Edmonton's emergency-services guide identifies NFPA 1710 as a reference for fire-service organization and deployment. It is operational context in that guide, not a generic product test or a universal variance prerequisite. [Edmonton guide, file pp.4, 14-15.]

If a proposed system has inspection, testing and maintenance needs beyond those already prescribed for ordinary fire-code systems, the alternative-solution record must describe those needs and who is accountable for them. Edmonton specifically addresses this handover to owners/operators, the fire AHJ and the responsible professionals. [Edmonton guide, file p.22; NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Clause 2.3.1.1.(2)(b) [PDF p.739].]

## Engineering / professional triggers

### Three separate checks

1. Does the building code require a registered professional for this building, design element, system or particular safety concern?

2. Does the proposed service fall within a regulated professional scope, including any applicable statutory exception?
3. Is the person actually authorized and competent for that scope, and are the documents authenticated as required?

The word "variance" alone answers none of these. The alternative-solution documentation clause requires qualifications and design responsibility; it does not say that every variance must be authored by an engineer. The SCC FAQ says hiring an engineer or architect may be beneficial. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentence 2.3.1.1.(4) [PDF p.739]; SCC FAQ, file p.1.]

### Building-wide professional involvement

The baseline exceptions in NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentence 2.4.1.1.(1) [PDF pp.739-740] are subject to the special-safety-concern power in Sentence 2.4.1.1.(4) [PDF p.740]. The following table paraphrases those exceptions. It is not a Part 9 classification table.

| Building type | Baseline exception described in the code |
|---|---|
| Assembly, care, treatment or detention | No more than three storeys; building area limits of 300 m2 at one storey, 150 m2 at two, or 100 m2 at three. |
| Residential | Single-family dwelling, or multiple-family dwelling containing no more than four dwelling units. The clause does not apply the hotel-style area thresholds to this category. |
| Hotel, motel or similar residential use | No more than three storeys; building area limits of 400 m2 at one storey, 200 m2 at two, or 130 m2 at three. |
| Business/personal services, mercantile or industrial | No more than three storeys; building area limits of 500 m2 at one storey, 250 m2 at two, or 165 m2 at three. |
| Farm building | Not for public use. |
| Relocatable industrial camp building | Included in the listed baseline exceptions. |

Secondary suites need not be counted as dwelling units for these exceptions. For other buildings, drawings and specifications require authentication by one or more registered professionals qualified for the applicable combination of architecture and engineering. The AHJ can require professional involvement and field review where project size or complexity raises special safety concerns. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentences 2.4.1.1.(2), 2.4.1.1.(3) and 2.4.1.1.(4) [PDF p.740].]

Do not convert "Part 9" into "no professional needed", or "Part 3" into a complete professional-involvement decision. The classification and professional thresholds use different tests, and specific system/design duties still apply.

### Regulated professional practice

The Engineering and Geoscience Professions Act defines engineering practice to include specified reporting, advice, evaluation, design, plans and technical direction when they require professional application of mathematics, chemistry, physics or related applied subjects for structures, works or processes. Its exclusive-practice provision is subject to express exceptions, including specified building work and building categories. A simplistic "uses calculations, therefore always requires an engineer" rule would omit those exceptions. [EGPA, s.1(1)(q), file p.10; s.2, file pp.12-15, S20.]

A fire/smoke performance model, alternative structural design or specialized system analysis therefore triggers a scope and authorization check. The tool can flag that check and request the responsible professional's work. It must not calculate an unsupported equivalence verdict or invent a professional exemption. Architecture has its own statutory scope; this sheet does not reproduce the Architects Act. See [professional-involvement](professional-involvement.md).

The code exceptions above use building area. EGPA's listed multi-storey building exceptions use gross area on each floor. They must not be treated as identical wording or merged into one numeric rule. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.4.1.1. [PDF pp.739-740]; EGPA, s.2(7), file pp.14-15.]

The person coordinating several authors under the alternative-solution documentation clause and the coordinating registered professional under the professional-schedules provisions are related roles with different triggers. One named coordinator in a report does not prove that all required professional schedules have been obtained. [NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentence 2.3.1.1.(6) [PDF p.739]; Article 2.4.2.2. [PDF p.741].]

## Common refusal / "more info" causes

### Source-based refusal categories

The current combined SCC form gives three refusal categories: incorrect information; insufficient/incomplete information to determine performance; and a proposed solution that does not provide the required safety performance. It also provides space for reasons. The separate Written Notice template has those categories plus "Other" and instructions for explaining each. [SCC form, file p.4; Written Notice template, file pp.1, 4, S12.]

| Submission problem | Appropriate completeness finding or next information request |
|---|---|
| Wrong code edition, Division, sentence or attribution | Identify the bad reference and request the correct governing provision and table. The tool can verify citation existence without deciding the design. |
| No precise description of the departure or benchmark | Request the proposed design, relevant drawings and specific acceptable-solution comparison. |
| An objective label is offered as the entire justification | Request analytical methods and rationales tied to the required minimum performance. |
| Required report, drawing, calculation or qualification evidence is absent | Identify the missing item and responsible author. The Written Notice instructions expressly give an absent required engineering report as an example. |
| A report is present but applies to a different product, design, exposure or revision | Request an applicability explanation or correctly scoped evidence. |
| Special operating, maintenance or commissioning needs are omitted | Request those requirements and the responsible party. |
| Several disciplines contributed without a named coordinator | Request the coordinator and a coherent, consistent analysis. |
| Project/site or issuer scope is unclear | Resolve the property, permit, AHJ, reviewing agency and single-site/multiple-site scope. |
| A proposed design is said to be unsafe or unequal | Record this only as an actual reviewer judgment supported by that reviewer's reasons. The application tool cannot turn missing information into a technical failure verdict. |

The documentation requests follow NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739]. They are also reflected in Calgary's guide, file pp.2-3, and the refusal-template instructions, file p.4. They are not measured frequencies of why applicants are refused.

### Written refusal record

The SCO must serve written notice when refusing to issue a variance. A request for more information and a formal written refusal should be recorded as different events; an application status alone cannot establish whether notice was served. [Safety Codes Act, s.38(6), file p.32.]

The separate SCC Written Notice template records issue date, recipient and address, owner on title if different, property identification, variance request details, refusal category and rationale. It also identifies the AHJ, agency where involved, issuing SCO, discipline, designation-of-powers number, contact details and signature. Its instructions call for the requested variance's relevant code references and request date. It provides appeal instructions and a copy destination at SCC. [Written Notice, file pp.1-5, S12.]

The current combined variance form instead records the refusal in Part 2(b), including explicit served-on and service-format fields. Retain the document actually issued, its attachments and service evidence. Do not rewrite the issuer's reasons into a different category. [SCC form, file p.4.]

### Appeals of an SCO variance refusal

- The Act gives the applicant 30 days after receipt under s.38(7). The general notice-of-appeal provision requires Council receipt within 30 days after service of the written refusal. Preserve both service and receipt information rather than assuming the issue date controls. [Act, s.38(7), file p.32; s.51(2), file p.43.]

- The current SCC pages require the signed Notice of Appeal, the written refusal and $500. The current combined form also calls for copies of Part 1 and Part 2(b), grounds and appellant contact information. [S07; S08, accessed 2026-10-18; SCC form, file p.5.]
- Send the appeal to the Council's Coordinator of Appeals through the current Council channel. A discussion with a local reviewer is not filing an appeal. The SCC page lists `appeals@safetycodes.ab.ca` and Suite 500, 10405 Jasper Avenue. [S08.]

- The Tribunal can confirm the refusal or revoke it and issue a variance on conditions it considers appropriate. An appeal is not an automatic approval. [Act, s.52(2)(b.1), file p.43.]
- SCC says the parties submit appeal briefs and that a pending appeal does not itself operate as a stay of a directive. Do not represent an appeal as permission to carry out refused work. [S08.]

- The Act makes an Administrator's variance decision final and not subject to the Council appeal route. The SCC form and Written Notice template expressly flag this limitation. Identify whether the actual decision was made by an SCO or by an Administrator before presenting an appeal option. [Act, s.38(5), file p.31; SCC form, file p.5; Written Notice, file p.3.]
- A Council order has a distinct court-appeal route on a question of law or jurisdiction, generally commenced within 30 days after service, with an extension power in the statute. This is not a second technical review by the application tool. [Act, s.53, file p.44.]

UNVERIFIED: a full implementation of service validity, deadline computation, holiday treatment and extensions under the current Council bylaws and other applicable law. This research verified the statutory 30-day rule and the current fee, but did not validate a legal deadline-calculation algorithm. Preserve the dates and evidence and confirm a disputed deadline with the Council or a legal adviser. A pending request to revise a design should not be assumed to extend the appeal window.
