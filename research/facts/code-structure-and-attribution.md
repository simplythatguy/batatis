## Plain-language summary
Alberta's building code has three Divisions across two volumes. Division A sets scope, compliance rules, objectives and building functions. Division B contains the technical acceptable solutions. Division C contains administrative provisions. Volume 2 continues Division B; it is not a different code. [C1, contents, PDF p.5; Preface, PDF pp.10-12.]

An acceptable solution is the code's specified way to reach its required performance. An alternative solution proposes another way. It must reach at least the minimum performance of the applicable acceptable solutions in the areas identified by their attributed objectives and functions. Those words do not set a numerical target by themselves. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.2.1.1.(1) [PDF p.38].

An attribution is a published link between a technical requirement and its objectives and functional statements. Section C of the Safety Codes Council form asks for the article, objective, function and intent. The first three come from the Alberta code and its tables. Intent comes from the separate national supplement where it applies. [C1, Preface, PDF pp.10-12; S1, PDF p.1.]

An intent statement explains why a requirement exists. It is advisory. The national NBC 2020 Intent Statements supplement is explanatory context, not Alberta law, and does not supply intent for Alberta-only requirements. [C1, Preface, PDF p.11; I1, introduction, PDF p.2.]

A complete Section C identifies the actual departure, the correct edition and Division, the applicable sentence, its conditional attribution sets and the matching intent entry. It also identifies missing evidence rather than filling a blank with a guessed citation. The worked examples below show a Part 3 row, a Part 9 row and an exception with no direct attribution. [S1, PDF p.1; C1, Preface, PDF pp.10-12.]

## Structure reference
### Divisions, volumes and numbering
The combined second-printing PDF has Volume 1 material followed by Volume 2. Its contents place Division A, Division B Parts 1-8, Appendices C and D, Division C and its professional schedules in Volume 1. Volume 2 contains Division B Parts 9-11. Part 2 of Alberta Division B is reserved. [C1, contents, PDF p.5.]

A full citation needs the Division because the same number can occur in more than one Division. The Code's own unspecified cross-references mean the same Division as the referring provision. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.1.4.(1) [PDF p.39].

The numbering hierarchy is Part, Section, Subsection, Article, Sentence, Clause and Subclause. The Preface illustrates this with Part 3 and progressively longer identifiers. Parenthetical Arabic numbers identify sentences, letters identify clauses, and Roman numerals identify subclauses. Read the opening text and the connecting "and" or "or" with every clause. [C1, Preface, PDF p.13.]

A PDF page number means the one-based page of the combined source file, not its printed page label. Printed page labels restart by Division and Part. Keep the body and attribution locations separate. For example, NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.9.10.1.(1) is in Volume 2 [PDF p.873]; its attribution [F10-OS3.7] is in the Part 9 attribution table [PDF p.1126].

### Applicability comes before attribution
NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.1.1.1.(1) [PDF p.37] covers new design and construction, occupancy and changes of occupancy, alterations, additions, demolition, reconstruction, unsafe conditions and specified related work. It also reaches regulated material/equipment replacement and relocation work. Sentence (2) covers site-built and factory-constructed buildings. The exclusions in Sentence (4) begin on that page and continue on PDF p.38. A small building is not automatically exempt.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.1.1.2. [PDF p.38] addresses buildings that legally existed before 1 May 2024. It prevents a decrease in life safety or building performance during listed work and addresses lawful existing conditions and the authority's acceptance powers. An existing-building question needs that analysis before treating every current new-construction provision as a required variance.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.2.1.(1) [PDF p.39] applies Division A Parts 1-3 to all buildings covered by the Code. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.4.1.(1) [PDF p.41] similarly applies Division C Parts 1 and 2.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.1.(1) [PDF p.39] applies Division B Parts 1, 7 and 8 to all covered buildings. A Part 9 classification does not remove those Parts.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.2.(1) [PDF pp.39-40] applies Division B Parts 3, 4, 5 and 6 to the following categories:

- Post-disaster buildings.
- Buildings used for Group A assembly, Group B care/treatment/detention, or Group F Division 1 high-hazard industrial major occupancies, subject to the express Part 9 home-type care category below.
- Buildings exceeding 600 m² in building area or exceeding three storeys in building height, when used for Group C residential, Group D business and personal services, Group E mercantile, or Group F Divisions 2 or 3 industrial major occupancies.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) [PDF p.40] expressly applies Division B Part 9 when all of these conditions hold:

- The building is within the Code's scope.
- It is no more than three storeys in building height.
- Its building area is no more than 600 m².
- Its major occupancy is Group B Division 4 home-type care, Group C, Group D, Group E, or Group F Division 2 or 3.

Exactly 600 m² and exactly three storeys fall within those numerical limits. A two-storey 500 m² assembly building does not become Part 9 merely because it is small. A four-storey residential building does not qualify through this sentence. These are applications of the preceding two verified sentences, not technical classifications of actual projects.

Alberta's home-type care category needs special care. The broad Group B wording in NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.2.(1) [PDF pp.39-40] must be read with the express Group B Division 4 entry in NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) [PDF p.40]. Do not give the blanket answer "all Group B is Part 3." Confirm that the proposed use meets the defined term and any applicable Part 9 conditions; consult the occupancy topic for a real classification.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.3.3.4. [PDF pp.40-41] governs building size determination, including firewalls and qualified separated portions. Ordinary tenant or ownership boundaries do not by themselves establish separate buildings. Classification cannot use total floor area as a substitute for the defined building area.

Part 10 relocatable industrial accommodation and Part 11 exterior acoustic insulation have separate application rules. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.3.3.3. [PDF p.40]. They are Alberta-specific routing checks, not interchangeable alternatives to Part 9.

### Definitions, Notes and standards
NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF pp.42-52] assigns code meanings to italicized terms. Resolve terms such as building area, building height, first storey, grade, major occupancy and home-type care occupancy before classifying a project. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.4.1.1. [PDF p.41] addresses non-defined terms and identifies the Code's objectives, functions, acceptable solutions and alternative solutions.

The following defined terms control several of the routing decisions above:

| Defined term | Paraphrased meaning | Verified source |
|---|---|---|
| Building area | The greatest horizontal building area above grade, measured to outside wall surfaces and, where applicable, a firewall's centre line. | NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.43]. |
| Building height, in storeys | The number of storeys between the first storey's floor and the roof. | NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.43]. |
| First storey | The highest storey whose floor is no more than 2 m above grade. | NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.45]. |
| Grade | Use the lowest of the average finished-ground levels beside each exterior wall; localized depressions need not count in those averages. | NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.46]. |
| Floor area | The space on a storey inside exterior walls and required firewalls, with interior partitions included but exits, vertical service spaces and their enclosures excluded. | NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.46]. |
| Major occupancy | The principal intended use of the building or part, including integral subsidiary uses. | NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.47]. |
| Home-type care occupancy | A single detached housekeeping unit in which residents receive care, potentially including the caregiver's family living space. | NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.47]. |

Notes beginning with A- explain the correspondingly numbered requirement. They are explanatory and are not requirements in their own right. For example, NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Note A-1.2.1.1.(1)(b) [PDF p.57] explains the benchmark comparison, while the compliance obligation is in the body sentence. Cite the governing sentence and use the Note to explain it.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.5.1.1. [PDF p.54] limits referenced documents to their relevant building and attributed performance issues. Its second sentence includes objectives and functions from the referenced NECB, NFC(AE) or NPC when those codes are invoked. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.5.1.2.(1) [PDF p.54] gives the Alberta code precedence over a conflicting referenced document.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.5.1.3.(1) [PDF p.54] directs the reader to the specified editions in Division B. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 1.3.1.1.(1) [PDF p.90] includes amendments and related updates effective to 15 July 2022 unless otherwise specified. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 1.3.1.2.(1) [PDF p.90] directs the reader to Table 1.3.1.2. for the designated editions. Do not silently replace a referenced edition with a newer commercial edition.

### Alberta amendments, errata and printings
The Preface distinguishes a thin margin line for technical changes relative to the national 2015 edition from a heavy margin line for Alberta changes to the national 2020 text. Editorial changes, renumbering and deletions may have no change mark. [C1, Preface, PDF pp.13-14.]

Alberta lists NBC(AE) 2023 second printing, published 17 August 2026, as the current building code. Its historical timeline identifies the first printing as declared in force on 1 May 2024. That is a printing distinction within the 2023 Alberta edition. National NBC 2020 or 2025 publications are explanatory context and cannot replace the adopted Alberta text. [W1, accessed 2026-10-18.]

The Preface distinguishes revisions implemented through a Building Code amendment regulation from the STANDATA categories of variance, interpretation, bulletin and errata. A bulletin or old variance should not be described as a new code edition. Check edition, amendment date, purpose and conditions before using any STANDATA. [C1, Preface, PDF p.14.]

## Benchmark acceptable solutions
This topic's main benchmark is the compliance method, not a single construction detail.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.2.1.1.(1) [PDF p.38] permits compliance through applicable Division B acceptable solutions or an alternative solution reaching their minimum performance in the attributed areas. Sentence (2) directs the attribution lookup to Division B.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 1.1.2.1.(1) [PDF p.89] identifies the attribution sections for the technical Parts. The objectives are not a menu from which an applicant chooses a convenient broad label.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Note A-1.2.1.1.(1)(a) [PDF p.56] explains that compliance with all applicable acceptable solutions already satisfies the linked objectives and functions. A new product is not automatically an alternative solution if it can demonstrate that compliance.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Note A-1.2.1.1.(1)(b) [PDF p.57] explains how to compare an alternative with a compliant design. The objectives and functions describe the areas of performance. The applicable acceptable solutions establish the benchmark. Where several compliant designs are available, the lowest-performing applicable compliant design generally establishes the minimum; a design replacing several sets must meet the applicable overall performance.

NRC's Bergeron paper makes the same distinction between a benchmark approach and a first-principles assessment against qualitative goals. It describes performance "as well as" the acceptable solution, not "well enough." Its discussion of the lowest acceptable benchmark concerns applicable compliant designs and identification with the regulatory official. It does not authorize selecting a weaker provision for a different occupancy, building size or construction condition. [N1, PDF pp.4,11-12, historical explanatory research; not Alberta law.]

Archer's NRC paper places the demonstration burden on the proponent and discusses third-party evaluations, certification and qualified professional review. It explains why an innovative design's asserted adequacy does not replace comparison with code performance. These are historical explanations of the system, not a current Alberta professional-practice rule. [N2, PDF pp.3-4, explanatory context.]

Practical benchmark record:

1. Describe the project and the exact construction or design change.
2. Establish scope, occupancy, building area, building height and the governing Parts.
3. Identify the applicable acceptable solution that the change does not meet, including its conditions and cross-references.
4. Describe a buildable compliant comparison design and the relevant performance measures.
5. Retrieve the sentence-level attribution sets, including table application conditions.
6. Link the matching explanatory intent entry, or record that one is unavailable.
7. Identify the Section D evidence and qualified author responsible for demonstrating the comparison.

Steps 1-7 are a consultant's synthesis of the code method and Section C/D fields. They are not additional code articles or a finding that any proposed alternative is adequate. [S1, PDF pp.1-2; C1, Preface, PDF pp.10-12; N1, PDF pp.11-12.]

### Objective application limits
NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 2.1.1.2. [PDF p.67] limits application as follows:

- OS4 applies only to dwelling units in Part 9 buildings.
- OH3 applies only to dwelling units.
- OH5 applies to the extent defined in the Plumbing Code Regulation, NFC(AE) and Private Sewage Disposal Systems Regulation.
- OA, OA1 and OA2 exclude the listed detached and attached house forms, houses with secondary suites, boarding houses, Group F Division 1 buildings and buildings not intended for daily or full-time occupation.
- OE, OE1 and OE1.1 apply to Part 9 residential buildings, qualifying Part 9 business/personal-service, mercantile and low-hazard industrial occupancies with combined total floor area at most 300 m², and the specified mixtures.

The OE limitation uses combined total floor area for the listed non-residential occupancies. The Part 3/Part 9 boundary uses building area. They are different measurements. For the broader energy-code interface, consult NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Note A-2.1.1.2.(6) [PDF p.75] and [energy-efficiency](energy-efficiency.md).

### Reading the rows and preserving the pairings
Find the article heading, then its sentence, then any clause selector. Read continued columns and pages. Keep each bracket intact and retain every applicable bracket. The table is not a list of independent objectives and functions that can be recombined arbitrarily. The bracket links the stated functions to the stated objectives.

For example, NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.4.2.1.(1) has attribution [F10,F12,F05,F06-OS3.7], [F12,F06-OS1.2] and [F12,F06-OP1.2], all in its Part 3 attribution row [PDF p.346]. OS3.7 applies to all four functions in the first set. It does not follow that F10 has also been attributed to OP1.2 for this sentence.

A Part 9 example is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.9.10.1.(1), attribution [F10-OS3.7] [PDF p.1126]. Do not replace it with the broader heading OS3 or with fire-only OS1.5.

Conditional table text matters. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 4.1.1.3.(3) has attribution [F20-OS2.1] only for structural members where temporary overloading during construction may impair that or another member [PDF p.513]. Keep that condition with the attribution.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.4.3.1.(1) [PDF p.838] demonstrates several conditions in one attribution row. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Table 9.38.1.1. [PDF p.1120] records them as follows:

- NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.4.3.1.(1), [F22-OP2.1,OP2.4] and [F22-OH4] have no further table application qualifier.
- NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.4.3.1.(1), [F22-OS2.3], [F22-OP2.3] and [F22-OH1.1,OH1.2,OH1.3] concern elements forming or supporting environmental separators.
- NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.4.3.1.(1), [F22-OS3.1] applies to floors and elements supporting them.
- NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.4.3.1.(1), [F22-OS3.7] applies to walls, and their supports, containing doors or windows required for emergency escape.

### Unattributed provisions and the root-sentence rule
NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Note A-1.1.2.1.(1) [PDF p.123] explains that modifiers, pointers and clarifying provisions often have no direct attribution and therefore do not appear in the tables. For a modifier or pointer with no attribution, use the attributions of the provision it references. "Root sentence" below is a convenient description of that referenced requirement, not a term defined by the Code.

Use this sequence:

1. Confirm that the provision exists in the Alberta second printing and read its complete text.
2. Search its attribution table for the exact sentence and any clauses. Confirm the omission; do not treat a failed database match as proof of omission.
3. Identify the requirement the exception modifies or points to from the actual text and linked context.
4. Retrieve that requirement's attribution sets and preserve their conditions.
5. In Section C, name the exception and explicitly identify the root requirement as the source of the attribution. A blank direct row does not mean the performance issue disappears.
6. If the referenced requirement is unclear or several roots apply, record an unresolved attribution question. Do not automatically use the preceding sentence, the preceding article or a similar national entry.

Verified example: NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.2.(1) [PDF p.151] modifies the temperature-rise condition for qualifying exterior walls and explicitly references the fire-resistance test requirement. There is no direct row for that exception in the Part 3 attribution table. Its root is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.1.(1), whose attribution sets are [F03-OS1.2], [F04-OS1.3], [F03-OP1.2] and [F04-OP1.3] [PDF p.322]. The worked example keeps the direct omission separate from the root attributions.

Another omission occurs at NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.4.2.1.(2) [PDF p.269]. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Table 3.10.1.1. [PDF p.346] lists sentences (1) and (3), with no direct row for (2), under that article. The national explanatory intent identifies it as a limited single-exit exemption. This omission is not permission to approve any single-exit building. The body conditions and root requirements still govern.

## Intent
### What the supplement contains
The source is the Supplement to the NBC 2020: Intent Statements, first release 2025. It is national explanatory material, not Alberta law. Its introduction says that only national Division B Parts 2-9 have intent statements. Entries identify a provision and may then group objectives, attribution sets and numbered intents. A provision may have several intents, or explanatory intent without a direct attribution set. [I1, PDF pp.1-2.]

A consultant first selects the Alberta requirement. Only then is the identically numbered national intent entry considered. Matching numbers are necessary but not sufficient; compare subject, scope, clauses, attribution conditions and any Alberta amendments. The Alberta code supplies the governing text and attribution. Its Preface expressly says intent statements are unavailable for requirements unique to Alberta. [C1, Preface, PDF p.11.]

A match can span pages. The source's printed page labels and combined PDF pages can differ. For example, the national NBC 2020 Intent Statements entry for 9.9.10.1.(1) [PDF p.1333] shows a printed footer of 203. Use PDF p.1333 for this repository's citation.

### Alberta differences and missing entries
National Part 2 farm-building intents do not establish an Alberta Part 2 acceptable solution. Alberta's Division B Part 2 is reserved. [C1, contents, PDF p.5; I1, introduction and first entries, PDF pp.2-3.]

Alberta Parts 10 and 11 are outside the national supplement's stated Part range. One checked example is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 11.2.1.2.(1) [PDF p.1517]. It has attribution [F110-OH10.1] [PDF p.1523], but the national intent search returned no entry. Record "No published national intent entry identified for this Alberta provision". Do not fabricate an intent from the objective label.

A missing entry is different from an unverified search. "No published entry identified" records what was searched and the source's stated scope. "UNVERIFIED: intent correspondence" means a national entry exists or may exist but its correspondence with the Alberta requirement has not been established. An applicant-written rationale may explain the proposal, but must not be labelled an NRC intent statement.

## Typical alternative solutions
This topic does not provide a generic substitute design. It describes how actual proposals become a complete code comparison.

- An alternative exit arrangement compares the affected escape and response functions with the applicable exit requirements. The worked Part 3 row below is an illustration of that comparison, not an accepted single-stair precedent.
- A window opening control device must reconcile protection from falls with bedroom escape. Alberta STANDATA variance 23-BCV-001 is a real example, with a defined residential Part 9 scope and a restriction concerning adaptable or barrier-free dwelling units. [P1, PDF pp.1-3.]
- A novel fire-rated wall or protection system needs a comparison with the required fire performance and the applicable testing or other permitted rating path. Its root attribution can concern fire spread and collapse, with both person and property objectives. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.1.(1), attributions [F03-OS1.2], [F04-OS1.3], [F03-OP1.2], [F04-OP1.3] [PDF p.322].
- A new envelope or structural component may affect several functions. A change cannot be assessed only as insulation if it also provides required bracing or fire protection. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Note A-1.2.1.1.(1)(b) [PDF p.57] expressly warns that all applicable acceptable solutions must be considered.

The Canadian Wood Council guide is useful explanatory reading on alternative-solution documentation. Its professional-credential discussion expressly names British Columbia's AIBC and EGBC. ## Usual mitigations and evidence
### What the documentation must establish
NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739] requires the requester to provide a code analysis and information about special operation, maintenance and commissioning needs. It requires identification of the applicable acceptable solutions, objectives and functions, the assumptions and limits, supporting tests/studies/performance parameters, and the responsible designers' qualifications. Where several people take responsibility, one person must coordinate preparation.

The SCC form separates Section C's justification from Section D's supporting-document list and responsible individual/professional/organization. A correct citation table cannot demonstrate technical equivalence by itself. [S1, PDF pp.1-2.]

Useful evidence varies with the proposed departure. For the three examples below, the evidence may include:

| Departure | Evidence to identify in Section D | What remains to be judged |
|---|---|---|
| Changed exit arrangement | Plans with route geometry, occupancy assumptions, alternative and compliant comparison designs, evacuation/response analysis, assumptions about warning and suppression, maintenance dependencies. | Whether the submitted comparison establishes the required performance across every affected function. |
| Changed bedroom escape arrangement | Exact window/door and hardware configuration, clear opening and operation information, device compliance records if applicable, any well/obstruction details, drawings showing the actual installation. | Whether escape remains effective and other window safety requirements are addressed. |
| Exterior-wall temperature-rise departure | Identified assembly, relevant fire test or rating evidence, limiting distance and radiation analysis, exposure conditions and scope limits. | Whether the specific departure can achieve the applicable fire performance. |

This table is an evidence checklist synthesized from the verified example requirements and documentation article. It does not prescribe a universally sufficient analysis or authorize a mitigation package.

### Confidence and scope of the evidence
For completeness review, distinguish these evidence classes:

1. A bare assertion or an unrelated approved project establishes little about the actual proposal.
2. Product literature, preliminary calculations and sketches may establish what is proposed, but their scope and assumptions still need checking.
3. A report tied to the exact product, installation, edition and applicable test method can establish measured or evaluated facts within its limits.
4. A qualified professional's comparison addresses the actual project, linked performance areas, assumptions, limitations and dependencies.
5. The authority's issued variance records the decision and its conditions for the specified application. It does not convert the proposal into a universally accepted solution.

This is an evidence classification for this fact sheet, not a code ranking or a rule that a professional opinion overrides tests. Relevance, validity, accuracy and sufficient detail remain necessary. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentences 2.3.1.1.(3) to (5) [PDF p.739].

Archer's paper likewise describes third-party evaluations as advisory to the building official. [N2, PDF p.3, historical explanatory context.]

### Referenced standards actually checked
NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.1.(1) [PDF p.151] invokes CAN/ULC-S101 for fire-endurance testing. The governing edition rule is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 1.3.1.2.(1) [PDF p.90].

The designated edition is CAN/ULC-S101-14. [C1, referenced-documents table, ULC row, PDF p.115.] The choice of a different test or edition needs an explicit basis; a report saying only "fire tested" is incomplete.

STANDATA 23-BCV-001 discusses that method for the window-control/escape interaction and gives a use limitation. The ASTM document itself was not supplied or read here, so this sheet does not claim to verify its testing clauses or product conformity. [P1, PDF pp.1-3.]

## Engineering / professional triggers
A lookup of an existing attribution is different from making a technical comparison. Section C does not establish that the applicant is qualified to design or assess the alternative. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentences 2.3.1.1.(3) to (5) [PDF p.739] require the analysis, the supporting basis and the responsible person's qualifications.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.4.1.1. [PDF pp.739-740] sets the main professional-involvement rules. Its first sentence lists building categories for which that general involvement requirement does not apply, except for the authority's special-safety-concern power. The listed occupancy and size limits differ from the Part 3/Part 9 boundary:

| Listed building category | Limits in the first sentence |
|---|---|
| Assembly, care, treatment or detention | At most three storeys; building area at most 300 m² at one storey, 150 m² at two, or 100 m² at three. |
| Residential dwelling use | A single-family dwelling, or a multiple-family dwelling containing no more than four dwelling units. |
| Hotel, motel or similar residential use | At most three storeys; building area at most 400 m² at one storey, 200 m² at two, or 130 m² at three. |
| Business/personal services, mercantile or industrial | At most three storeys; building area at most 500 m² at one storey, 250 m² at two, or 165 m² at three. |
| Farm building | Not for public use. |
| Relocatable industrial camp building | The specified classification. |

The same article's second sentence says secondary suites need not count as dwelling units for that first-sentence test. Its third sentence requires appropriately qualified registered professionals to authenticate drawings and specifications for other buildings. Its fourth sentence allows the authority to require professional involvement and field reviews because of a project's size, complexity and special safety concerns. Its fifth sentence requires authentication under the relevant professional legislation. These are limited code rules, not a general licence to practise engineering without authorization.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentence 2.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.740] requires a registered professional skilled in the work for design under Division B Part 4. A small Part 9 building does not eliminate that requirement for a design actually carried out under Part 4.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Articles 2.4.1.3. and 2.4.1.4. [PDF p.740] address professional design and installation review for fire suppression and fire alarm systems in the specified class of buildings, including witnessing testing or verification. Changes proposed as compensating fire measures must be routed through those checks where applicable.

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.4.2.1. [PDF p.741] defines when the professional-schedule subsection applies. NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.4.2.2. [PDF p.741] requires the owner to retain the coordinating professional and professionals of record and provide Schedules A and B for those projects. The division between scope, duties and schedules matters; the word "Part 3" alone is not the legal test.

A fire/egress modelling argument, structural calculation, radiation comparison or interpretation of test results requires a separate professional-practice assessment. This sheet does not determine whether a particular person's work constitutes regulated practice. The applicable code triggers above are verified. The complete professional-statute analysis belongs to [professional-involvement](professional-involvement.md), and is not independently established here.

## Common refusal / "more info" causes
The SCC refusal form expressly distinguishes incorrect information, insufficient information and failure to demonstrate the required performance. [S1, PDF p.4.] The following are practical examples of those categories:

- A national edition or superseded printing is used as the governing Alberta source.
- A citation omits the Division or identifies a whole Part instead of the relevant requirement.
- The building's major occupancy, height, building area or applicable existing-building status is unresolved.
- An exception is used even though its limiting conditions are not satisfied.
- A broad objective heading replaces the exact attributed sub-objective.
- Only one convenient attribution set is included, or conditions and clause selectors disappear.
- An unattributed exception is labelled "no safety objective" without examining the provision it references.
- A same-number national intent entry is copied without checking its Alberta correspondence.
- An applicant's rationale is presented as an official intent statement.
- The application states that sprinklers, alarms or another measure "compensate" without a project-specific comparison across the affected performance areas.
- Section D does not identify the supporting documents, their scope and responsible authors, or the analysis omits operating/maintenance dependencies and limits.
- A prior variance for another condition is treated as automatic approval of the proposed project.

The documentation basis is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739]. The relevant attribution and benchmark explanations are NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Note A-1.1.2.1.(1) [PDF p.123] and NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Note A-1.2.1.1.(1)(b) [PDF p.57]. This list identifies completeness questions; it does not predict an SCO's technical decision.

## Worked example
### How a consultant fills Section C
Section C's fields are Applicable Code or Standard, Article(s), Objective, Function and Intent statements. Section D lists supporting documents and responsible authors. These are the fields visually checked in the supplied SCC form and the identical live download. [S1, PDF pp.1-2.]

A consultant's sequence is:

1. Record the governing source as NBC(AE) 2023, second printing. Record any additional code/standard separately rather than using an unexplained "NBC".
2. Record the project facts that establish the governing Parts, including defined building area, height, occupancy and existing-building status.
3. Describe exactly what the proposed alternative changes. Read the whole acceptable solution, its exceptions and related requirements.
4. Put the exact sentence or clause in the article column, even though the form uses the broader label Article(s).
5. Look up the Alberta table row. Transfer all applicable bracket sets and conditions into the objective/function record. Preserve pairings if those fields are displayed separately.
6. For a modifier without a row, record the direct omission and name its verified referenced requirement as the attribution source.
7. Read the corresponding national intent entry as explanatory context. Paraphrase its intent and identify the supplement and page. Explicitly record missing or unresolved correspondence.
8. Explain why each row is included and name the evidence that Section D must supply. A row can be citation-complete while its technical demonstration is still incomplete.

The following are three hypothetical mini-cases. They demonstrate the lookup method. They are not issued variances, completed technical analyses or a substitute for project-specific code review.

### Row A, a Part 3 exit proposal
A proposed four-storey, 450 m² building-area apartment building has a common interior corridor and an innovator proposes one shared exit stair. The project team has not established an applicable dwelling-unit exemption. The height exceeds the Part 9 limit under NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.2.(1) [PDF pp.39-40] and NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) [PDF p.40].

The starting benchmark is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.4.2.1.(1) [PDF p.269], which requires two exits from an occupied floor area subject to its stated exceptions. Read the entire article, including the conditions and dwelling-unit waiver in NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Article 3.4.2.1. [PDF pp.269-270]. The row below deliberately retains emergency-response and property objectives as well as occupant escape.

### Row B, a Part 9 bedroom window proposal
A two-storey detached house with 180 m² building area has an unsprinklered bedroom. Replacement release hardware would require a tool to open its only exterior escape window. The assumed height, area and residential occupancy satisfy the general Part 9 boundary in NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) [PDF p.40].

The departure is from the tool-free operation requirement in NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.9.10.1.(1) [PDF p.873]. If the hardware also changes the clear opening or ability to stay open, include NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.9.10.1.(2) [PDF p.873]. The latter requires at least 0.35 m² unobstructed opening, no dimension under 380 mm, and an opening maintained without extra support. This worked row addresses only the first sentence's identified hardware departure.
