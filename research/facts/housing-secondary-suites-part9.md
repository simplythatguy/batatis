## Plain-language summary

Part 9 is the code route for qualifying small buildings, including many houses and row houses.
For residential use, the ordinary limits are three storeys and 600 m² of building area, not total floor area.
A secondary suite is one self-contained dwelling paired with one other dwelling and common spaces in one real estate entity.
Three suites under one principal dwelling do not fit that definition; three row-house principal dwellings with one suite each can.
The drawings must distinguish separation between houses from the smoke barrier between a house and its suite.
An escape window for a bedroom is different from the larger window used for the multi-storey travel exception.
Edmonton and Calgary have different published alternatives for row houses with secondary suites.
A matching published alternative can simplify an application, but its location, edition, conditions and required paperwork still matter.
This fact sheet supports completeness and citation checks; a safety codes officer decides acceptance and professionals supply technical judgment.

Sources: NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) [PDF p.40]; Div A, Article 1.4.1.2., definition of secondary suite [PDF p.50]; Div A, Note A-1.4.1.2.(1), secondary suite [PDF p.62]; Div B, Articles 9.9.9.1. and 9.9.10.1. [PDF pp.872–873]. Municipal alternatives are detailed under Precedents.

## When this topic applies

### Project signals

- A new detached or semi-detached house, a row-house development, or a basement conversion described as a secondary suite.
- A second kitchen and independent dwelling facilities within an existing house, even if the applicant calls it an in-law space.
- A backyard house, garden suite or garage suite. First identify the separate building and any secondary suite within it.
- A row house with three or more adjacent houses containing secondary suites, especially a request to replace a firewall with a one-hour party-wall separation.
- A low basement ceiling, a shared entrance, an exterior exit stair beside windows, a bedroom window well, or a window opening control device.
- Repeated house designs, factory-built housing modules, or roof trusses that prompt recurring permit-document questions.

The classification gate is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Articles 1.3.3.2. and 1.3.3.3. [PDF pp.39–40], together with Div B, Sentence 9.1.1.1.(1) [PDF p.831]. The suite-definition gate is Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.50]. These are project signals for investigation, not findings that a permit or variance is already justified.

### Near-misses and boundaries

| Description | Routing consequence |
|---|---|

| Three adjacent principal row houses, each with one secondary suite | Possible secondary-suite configuration. Check overall building size, property lines and party-wall construction before selecting this sheet's row-house precedent. |
| Residential building over three storeys or over 600 m² building area | Ordinary Part 3 route; see [egress-and-single-stair](egress-and-single-stair.md) and [fire-separations-and-spatial-separation](fire-separations-and-spatial-separation.md). A municipal residential permit category does not establish Part 9 eligibility. |
| Apartment unit carved into more units, supportive housing or care use | Do not apply house-with-secondary-suite exceptions merely because there are two kitchens. Establish occupancy and building classification first. |
| Window enlargement in a loadbearing foundation | Egress dimensions belong here; structural design belongs in [structural-and-framing](structural-and-framing.md). |
| Shared furnace, exhaust, combustion air or plumbing alterations | Cross-route to [hvac-ventilation-plumbing](hvac-ventilation-plumbing.md). |

### Intake facts that change the answer

Record the municipality, site address, legal parcel boundaries, existing and proposed uses, number of principal dwellings, number and location of secondary suites, building area, building height, above/below relationships, common spaces, shared exits, sprinklers, and whether work is new construction or an alteration.
Record whether the drawings rely on a firewall to divide the building, a fire separation for height determination, or a party wall without that building-division effect. These are different mechanisms under NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.3.3.4. [PDF pp.40–41].

## Benchmark acceptable solutions

All building-code citations below refer to NBC(AE) 2023, second printing, abbreviated `p2`.
PDF page numbers refer to the combined Volume 1 + Volume 2 file, not the printed chapter pagination.
A benchmark is the prescribed construction against which a proposed alternative is compared.
Municipal policies and provincial variances are separate sources of permission; they do not rewrite the printed benchmark.

### Classification and building boundaries

1. **Part 9 eligibility.** For ordinary Group C residential occupancy, both the three-storey maximum and 600 m² building-area maximum must be satisfied. See Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) [PDF p.40] and Div B, Sentence 9.1.1.1.(1) [PDF p.831]. Larger residential buildings enter the Parts 3–6 route under Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.2.(1) [PDF pp.39–40].
2. **Building division.** A firewall can divide a building into separate buildings under Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.4.(1) [PDF p.40]. The one-hour separation permission in Sentence 1.3.3.4.(2) [PDF p.41] is specifically for determining building height, with additional conditions. It is not a general permission to divide building area into smaller houses.
3. **Secondary suite.** The definition requires only one other dwelling in the relevant residential building or portion, common spaces as applicable, and a single real estate entity. Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.50]; explanatory Note A-1.4.1.2.(1) [PDF p.62]. The suite can be above, below or beside the principal dwelling; "basement suite" is not the full scope.
4. **Existing buildings.** Lawful existing conditions, proposed alterations and safety consequences are relevant to the authority's discretion. Adding a suite does not by itself prove that an old condition is lawful or acceptable. Div A, Article 1.1.1.2. [PDF p.38].
5. **Part 3 provisions inside a Part 9 project.** Part 9 explicitly refers some work to Part 3, including certain basements and sprinkler systems. Div B, Sentence 9.10.1.3.(3) [PDF p.875] and Sentence 9.10.1.3.(8) [PDF p.876]. A Part 9 classification does not eliminate these referrals.

| Hallway | Div B, Sentence 9.5.4.1.(1) [PDF p.841]: ordinarily 860 mm; the 710 mm option has both room-use and second-exit conditions. |

| Stair headroom | Div B, Sentences 9.8.2.2.(3)–(4) [PDF p.852]: the house-with-suite route generally uses 1,950 mm, with 1,850 mm under beams and ducts in secondary suites. Preserve the stated spiral-stair exception when applicable. |
| Open sides | Div B, Sentence 9.8.8.1.(1) [PDF p.859]: guards generally protect accessible walking surfaces where the adjacent elevation difference exceeds 600 mm within 1.2 m, subject to the stated exceptions. |
| Openable windows | Div B, Sentences 9.8.8.1.(4)–(5) [PDF p.859]: guard or opening-control protection, ordinarily limiting an unprotected opening to 100 mm, unless the bottom of the openable part is more than 900 mm above the interior floor or less than 1,800 mm above the other-side floor/ground. The strict "more than" and "less than" boundaries matter. |
| Guard height | Div B, Sentences 9.8.8.3.(1)–(3) [PDF p.860]: 1,070 mm general minimum, 900 mm inside dwelling units and houses with suites, and a conditional 900 mm exterior allowance. This allowance depends on the exterior walking-surface height and what the guard serves. |

Window fall protection and emergency escape must work together. The release required by the guard rule can conflict with the escape-window rule. STANDATA 23-BCV-001 and Edmonton B23-02 address that interface; their conditions are recorded below.

### Egress from a suite and escape from a bedroom

- **Exit protection:** Div B, Sentence 9.9.4.2.(2) [PDF p.865] requires a continuous smoke-tight barrier of at least 12.7 mm gypsum board on the specified walls and underside of floor-ceiling framing around exits in a house with a secondary suite. Sentence (5) has a separate open-exterior-passageway exception with two end stairs and at least 50% open exterior sides.
- **Exposed exterior exit stair:** Div B, Sentence 9.9.4.4.(1) [PDF p.865] addresses unprotected openings exposing the only exterior exit stair or ramp from another fire compartment or the other dwelling/common/ancillary space. The opening zone is within 3 m horizontally and less than 10 m below or less than 5 m above. Its prescribed glazing protection is not ordinary window glass.
- **Travel through multiple storeys:** Div B, Sentence 9.9.9.1.(1) [PDF p.872] generally limits travel to one storey up or down to the described exit/egress level. Sentence (2) permits more travel for a dwelling not above or below another suite if the qualifying window is at least 1 m high by 0.55 m wide, with sill at most 1 m above the floor and 7 m above adjacent grade. Sentence (3) provides a separate balcony option.
- **Opposite directions:** Div B, Sentence 9.9.9.2.(1) [PDF p.872] has a house-with-secondary-suite exception to its two-separate-exits rule. This does not waive all other egress requirements.
- **Shared egress:** Div B, Article 9.9.9.3. [PDF pp.872–873] distinguishes ordinary shared-egress facilities from houses with secondary suites. Sentence (2) still requires a second means for the upper dwelling when all listed exterior-passageway conditions are met, including an under-45-minute floor rating, one stair/ramp and height over 1.5 m.
- **Bedroom escape:** Div B, Sentence 9.9.10.1.(1) [PDF p.873] requires each bedroom or combination bedroom to have the described outside window or exterior door unless the suite is sprinklered. Opening must not require keys, tools, special knowledge or removal of sash/hardware, subject to an applicable variance.
- **Clear opening:** Div B, Sentence 9.9.10.1.(2) [PDF p.873] requires at least 0.35 m² with no dimension under 380 mm, and an opening that stays open without extra support. Nominal frame/glass dimensions do not establish the clear opening.
- **Window well:** Div B, Sentences 9.9.10.1.(3)–(5) [PDF p.873] require at least 760 mm clearance in front, suitable sash operation and an openable protective enclosure without keys, tools or special knowledge. Sentence (6) addresses security bars.
- **Do not substitute windows for required exits:** Div B, Sentence 9.9.2.3.(1) [PDF p.864] excludes windows from a required means of egress. A bedroom escape window and a specific window-based travel concession do not establish a general permission to delete an exit.

### Alarms, sound and building services

Smoke alarms are required in each dwelling and relevant ancillary/common spaces under Div B, Sentence 9.10.19.1.(1) [PDF p.909].
They must conform to CAN/ULC-S531. Location and installation, including each storey and sleeping rooms, are addressed by Article 9.10.19.3. [PDF p.909], which cites CAN/ULC-S553.
Article 9.10.19.4. [PDF pp.909–910] governs power, including backup and its exceptions.
Sentence 9.10.19.5.(2) [PDF p.910] requires all smoke alarms in the house with a suite to sound together and permits wireless or hard-wired interconnection.
This is interconnection within the house with its suite, not a finding that every alarm in an entire row must be interconnected.

CO alarms have a different trigger and wording. Div B, Article 9.32.3.9. [PDF pp.1049–1050] applies to residential buildings with fuel-burning equipment or a storage garage, specifies CSA 6.19, and addresses bedroom/service-room/garage adjacency.
Sentence (7) says CO alarms installed in a house with a suite and common spaces are wired so activation of one causes all to sound.
Do not copy the smoke-alarm wireless permission into this CO sentence. Edmonton's January 2025 design guide expressly accepts wireless CO interconnection [W15, guide p.15]; UNVERIFIED: the authority basis for that difference from the printed Alberta sentence was not established. See Open questions.

Sound separation is independent of fire separation. Div B, Sentence 9.11.1.1.(2) [PDF p.912] offers a prescribed absorptive-material/resilient-channel/gypsum construction, STC 43, or ASTC 40 route for a house with a suite.
STC is sound transmission class for the separating assembly; ASTC also accounts for adjoining construction and flanking sound paths.
A gypsum thickness sufficient for the smoke barrier does not alone establish the sound-separation route.

### When the project instead belongs to Part 3

The house-specific Part 9 concessions cannot simply be transplanted into a larger residential building.
Review the Part 3 residential separation, dwelling-unit egress and floor-area exit rules, as well as the construction and fire-safety classification of the actual building.
Relevant benchmarks are Div B, Article 3.3.4.2. [PDF pp.262–263], Article 3.3.4.4. [PDF pp.263–264], and Article 3.4.2.1. [PDF pp.269–270].
These provisions distinguish dwelling-unit egress from required exits serving a floor area.
For example, the general two-exit floor-area rule has specifically conditioned exceptions, not a general "small apartment" exemption.
For an actual Part 3 alternative, use [egress-and-single-stair](egress-and-single-stair.md) and [fire-separations-and-spatial-separation](fire-separations-and-spatial-separation.md) to develop the additional sentence-level benchmark and attributions.
The worked example below remains within Part 9; those Part 3 gateway provisions are not presented as varied provisions in its Section C.

### Edition and wording cautions

The explanatory NBC 2020 Intent Statements entry for 9.10.11.2.(2) uses wording about "fewer than 2 houses" with adjacent suites [PDF pp.1389–1390].
The verified Alberta prescription instead requires the firewall arrangement to produce buildings containing **no more than two adjacent houses with a secondary suite**, NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.2.(2) [PDF pp.889–890].
The intent explanation is useful for purpose; it must not replace the Alberta threshold.

The explanatory NBC 2020 Part 9 Illustrated User's Guide describes national-model secondary-suite floor-area caps of 80 m² and 80% [guide pp.35–36].
The Alberta second-printing Part 9 opening has Section 9.1 followed by Section 9.2 without that national floor-area provision [PDF p.831].
Do not import the national guide's cap as Alberta building-code law. Municipal zoning/subordination requirements still need a separate check.
The same guide's headroom and doorway explanations [guide pp.63, 66] were used only as context for the independently verified Alberta provisions.

As historical context, Edmonton's row-house report Appendix A uses NBC 2015 intent material, as its introduction states [report p.18].
This sheet uses the supplied explanatory NBC 2020 Intent Statements supplement for its own intent citations instead of silently adopting that older appendix.

### Proposals without a verified blanket precedent

Low ceilings below the suite allowances, undersized bedroom windows, a single shared duct system, missing smoke barriers, or an exposed exit stair outside B23-05's conditions require separate analysis.
No blanket approval for these departures was verified in this research.
Lower cost or an existing physical constraint explains why an applicant wants a change; neither demonstrates equivalent performance.
The alternative-solution documentation must address the applicable benchmark, assumptions, evidence and operational conditions under NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739].

### Standing-variance economics and the product implication

Calgary's **New Standing Posted Variance procedure**, dated September 6, 2017 [source p.1], reports 26 builders using zero-lot-line construction and **382 requests reviewed and accepted from January 1 to September 1, 2017**.
Each generated a variance that was written, issued, processed and forwarded to the Safety Codes Council.
The procedure replaced that repeated processing with a published SPV referenced in the application and permit conditions.
It says the process reduces work for applicants and City staff. It does not supply staff hours, dollars saved or a controlled timing comparison.

The brief's description of "382 identical requests" is shorthand for repeated requests in the same zero-lot-line category.
The primary document does not establish that all 382 designs were literally identical, nor that they concerned secondary-suite firewalls.
This historical example illustrates administrative duplication; it is not a 2026 annual volume forecast.

**Product inference from these sources:** a completeness tool should recognize a potentially applicable published alternative before preparing a new site-specific technical case.
The useful output is a match with documented conditions: jurisdiction, current version, applicable configuration, proposed deviation, required drawings/declarations and permit reference.
A partial match remains a partial match. Combining Edmonton's one-hour-wall permission with selected Calgary concessions would create an unverified new proposal.
The tool can identify missing evidence and condition conflicts; it cannot issue the variance or determine technical equivalence.

## Usual mitigations and evidence

### Evidence expected in a reviewable package

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739] requires a code analysis, applicable objectives/functions/acceptable solutions, assumptions and limitations, relevant testing or engineering studies, design-responsibility qualifications, and operational/maintenance information.
It also calls for sufficient detail to assess the validity, accuracy, relevance and precision of the analysis and coordination where multiple people take responsibility.
The following is a completeness checklist derived from those requirements and the municipal sources, not a universal list of additional code duties.

- Site plan with address, legal boundaries, adjacent buildings, entry routes, grade and exterior-stair elevations.
- All-floor plans that label principal dwellings, secondary suites, common spaces and service rooms.
- A wall/floor schedule that distinguishes rated separation, smoke barrier and firewall.
- Party-wall details from footings through roof junctions, including penetrations and concealed spaces.
- Assembly-rating evidence that matches the proposed materials, framing, fastening, joints and penetrations.
- Door thickness/construction, closers and any required labelled closure information.
- Window schedules with actual clear-open dimensions, sill heights, opening action and window-well geometry.
- Smoke and CO alarm locations, power arrangements and interconnection details, with the two systems assessed separately.
- HVAC plans identifying the dwelling served by each supply/return system and treatment of penetrations.
- The exact municipal policy/SPV identifier and dated version, its signed declaration where required, and the application/permit reference.
- For a new alternative, a named qualified author and an explicit comparison to each applicable objective/function pair, including property protection where attributed.
- A list of conditions needed after construction, with who maintains or inspects each relevant component.

### Mitigations are part of a whole proposal

Edmonton B23-01 retains the other house-with-suite requirements; its supporting report relies on compartmentation, doors, alarms and firefighting assumptions [report pp.13–17].
Calgary SPV-006 uses rated exit/suite separations, protected supporting construction, specified closures/penetrations, duct conditions and conditional additional egress or sprinklers [source pp.2–3].
These are different packages. Additional alarms do not automatically compensate for deleting a firewall, and sprinklers are not a universal waiver of window, exit or separation requirements.

A fire-resistance rating is a demonstrated assembly property, not a promise that evacuation is safe for that duration.
Edmonton's report discusses available safe egress time and required safe egress time as separate concepts and warns that its experimental times are not universal house-fire predictions [source pp.8–10].
The product may organize supplied analysis, but must not turn those report values into its own safety determination.

### Evidence confidence ladder

This ladder is an assessment aid, not a ranking enacted in the code.

1. **Applicable current permission plus demonstrated condition match:** strongest administrative evidence that a published path is available for this jurisdiction and configuration. Still requires the actual permit process.
2. **Exact prescribed/tested assembly or recognized calculation route:** strong component evidence when the installed arrangement matches all limitations.
3. **Project-specific authenticated professional analysis:** can address departures, interactions and extrapolation; the authority evaluates its sufficiency.
4. **Relevant research or a municipal report from another configuration:** useful support, with a documented explanation of transferability and limits.
5. **Manufacturer statement, marketing claim, photograph or an unrelated past approval:** insufficient on its own to establish equivalent system performance.

## Engineering / professional triggers

### General building threshold and its exception

NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Sentence 2.4.1.1.(1)(b) [PDF p.739] exempts a single-family dwelling or a multiple-family dwelling with no more than four dwelling units from the general professional-involvement requirement, subject to Sentence (4).
Sentence 2.4.1.1.(2) [PDF p.740] says secondary suites need not be counted as dwelling units for this threshold.
Thus three principal row houses with one qualifying suite each do not automatically become a six-unit trigger under this particular rule.
This counting permission does not remove the separate fire-separation, egress or building-size requirements.

Sentence 2.4.1.1.(3) [PDF p.740] requires authenticated drawings/specifications for buildings outside the listed exceptions.
Sentence 2.4.1.1.(4) [PDF p.740] permits the authority to require professionals and field reviews for special safety concerns arising from size or complexity.
The small-house exception therefore does not establish that every task on that house is outside professional practice.

### Where the completeness tool stops

Selecting a verified article, reproducing its attribution pair and identifying a missing drawing are administrative/citation tasks.
Developing a structural design, choosing a design fire, calculating smoke spread or evacuation tenability, or asserting that a reduced separation gives equivalent safety requires technical judgment and appropriate professional responsibility.
This is a professional-referral signal, not an assertion that all variance applications universally require an engineer.
The code requires the alternative-solution author's qualifications and responsibility to be disclosed under Div C, Sentences 2.3.1.1.(4)–(6) [PDF p.739].
APEGA states that independent engineering practice is reserved to its licensed professionals and permit holders: https://www.apega.ca/enforcement/rights, accessed 2026-10-18.
The project-specific practice boundary and any statutory exception must be resolved by the responsible professional/regulator, not inferred from the number of dwelling units alone.

Where the full professional-involvement regime applies, Div C, Articles 2.4.2.1. and 2.4.2.2. [PDF p.741] address the coordinating professional, retained professionals and Schedules A/B.
Do not demand those whole-project schedules solely because a truss component requires engineering.

## Common refusal / "more info" causes

These are source-derived completeness risks, not a statistical ranking of actual refusal decisions.
The Calgary alternative-solution guide [source pp.2–3], Edmonton permit pages and NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739] support the documentation requirements.

- No proof of the Part 9 building-area/building-height calculation, or incorrectly treating an ordinary party wall as dividing building area.
- Three rental dwellings called "secondary suites" beneath one principal dwelling without satisfying the defined configuration.
- No distinction between an existing lawful condition and new work in an old house.
- A citation copied from the national model, superseded first printing, old guide or municipal paraphrase without checking the Alberta second printing.
- A Section C row containing the whole article's assorted objectives instead of the pairs actually attributed to the affected sentence.
- Listing fire-safety objectives but omitting the adjacent-property protection objective for the row-house firewall issue.
- Missing B23-01 Letter of Construction Intention, or absent SPV-006 references in drawings and permit conditions.
- Using an Edmonton policy in another jurisdiction as though it were already granted there.
- Selecting only convenient parts of SPV-006, such as omitting its exit, supporting-structure, duct or closure conditions.
- Treating a 12.7 mm smoke barrier as a verified one-hour separation or treating a fire-rated wall as a structurally adequate firewall.
- No details for a party wall at the roof, penetrations or concealed spaces.
- Gross window size reported instead of clear openable area, or no allowance for sash interference in the well.
- Bedroom escape window used to claim a required exit has been provided.
- WOCD proposed for an excluded adaptable/barrier-free dwelling, or no evidence that its installed operation meets the applicable conditions.
- Smoke/CO alarm locations or interconnections omitted, or shared ductwork unresolved.
- Professional-involvement analysis based only on the house count despite engineered trusses, structural alterations or special safety concerns.
- Reliance on a manufacturer's assertion, a past permit or an unsourced savings claim without a project-specific condition match.
- New-house application missing authenticated roof-truss documents under Edmonton's effective March 1, 2026 requirement.

For Edmonton secondary-suite alterations, the published intake package includes site/floor plans, sound-separation information, exterior photos and conditionally required elevations and structural engineering documents.
For new houses, the published house-permit page calls for a survey plot plan, all-floor architectural plans, sections/details, applicable grading/site documents and roof-truss documentation.
These are distinct application contexts; the shorter alteration checklist is not a complete new-row-house submission.
Sources W1 and W2, accessed 2026-10-18.

## Precedents

### Edmonton B23-01: row houses with more than two secondary suites

- **Type and version:** municipal policy, July 30, 2024; listed on Edmonton's current house-permit page on 2026-10-18.
- **Scope:** Part 9 row houses with more than two adjacent houses with secondary suites, within the stated house spatial-separation route, on freehold or condominium lots.
- **Departure:** the firewall arrangement in NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.2.(2) [PDF pp.889–890].
- **Permitted package:** party wall with at least one-hour fire resistance, with all other house-with-secondary-suite requirements met. The policy does not add Calgary's separate 45-minute interior-suite package.
- **Submission:** sufficient drawing detail plus the signed Letter of Construction Intention with the building-permit application.
- **Acceptance boundary:** the letter is not a permit. The policy expressly retains exceptional SCO refusal with reasons and requires notification when construction intention changes.
- **Source:** B23-01 [source pp.1–2], W4.

The supporting **Alternative Solution for Secondary Suites in Rowhouses**, revision 02 dated July 31, 2024, is a City-authored engineering report prepared to inform municipal policy.
Page 3 restricts other uses of that report. Its technical rationale is evidence of how Edmonton supported its policy, not a reusable engineering approval for another project or jurisdiction.
It considers life safety and adjacent-property protection, uses research on compartmentation and protected floors, and discusses response/intervention assumptions [source pp.7–17].
Its conclusion supports one-hour party-wall separation under its stated conditions; this sheet does not independently validate that engineering conclusion.
Source W5.

### Calgary SPV-006: firewall in a rowhouse with secondary suites

- **Type and version:** Standing Posted Variance approved June 19, 2025, presently listed by Calgary.
- **Scope and changes:** the document names the row-house firewall sentence and the fire-damper sentence; it is a complete conditional package, not a one-line substitution.
- **Exit and dwelling separation:** a fire separation with at least 45-minute resistance, with its rating concession where the building is sprinklered.
- **Rated construction:** the cited rating-determination route or the complete deemed construction described by the SPV. Supporting loadbearing walls, columns and arches must have the prescribed supporting-assembly rating.
- **Shared egress:** the SPV adds its second/separate-means requirements and specified sprinkler/balcony/window options. A qualifying SPV window refers to the larger travel-limit dimensions, not merely a bedroom escape opening.
- **Ducts:** its specified noncombustible, minimum-melting-point and single-fire-compartment conditions govern the fire-damper concession; HVAC drawings must show the stated information.
- **Closures and penetrations:** the specified closure/solid-core-door route, self-closing devices and sealed penetrations remain required.
- **Submission/permit:** SPV-006 and compliance with its construction requirements must be referenced in the submitted permit documents; the permit conditions reference SPV-006 for that address.
- **Deviation:** the SPV cannot be modified. A different design follows the prescription or a site-specific variance, with professional involvement expressly required for the site-specific firewall departure.
- **Sources:** SPV-006 [source pp.1–4], W10. Its diagrams on p.5 were not independently assessed as design details.

The verified underlying fire-damper provision is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.13.13.(1) [PDF p.893]; its pairings are in Table 9.38.1.1. [PDF p.1129].
Do not attribute Calgary's duct concession to Edmonton B23-01.

### STANDATA 23-BCV-001 and Edmonton B23-02: window opening control devices

STANDATA 23-BCV-001, April 2024 [source pp.1–3], applies across Alberta to Part 9 residential buildings.
It allows a window opening control device meeting ASTM F2090 in the stated fall-protection/bedroom-escape application.
It excludes adaptable and barrier-free dwelling units.
Its paired benchmark is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.8.8.1.(4) [PDF p.859] and Sentence 9.9.10.1.(1) [PDF p.873].
Source W8.

Edmonton B23-02, December 23, 2024 [source pp.1–2], explains its local acceptance and inspection approach, including windows associated with the travel-limit provision.
It says certification/listing of the individual device is not required, but verification of its operation by the authority remains necessary.
Its checklist includes limited opening before release, either two independent single actions or one dual action, visible release mechanisms for egress windows, automatic reset and owner operating information at inspection.
Source W6.

**Unresolved interface:** B23-02 uses a 102 mm ASTM-derived opening figure, while the printed Alberta guard requirement says 100 mm.
Record which permission and device configuration is relied upon; do not silently replace the provincial code dimension with 102 mm everywhere.
Full ASTM testing requirements and a particular device's conformity were not independently verified.

### Edmonton B23-05: openings near exterior exit stairs and ramps

This is a **municipal interpretation**, version 1, June 10, 2026, not a province-wide amendment.
Its policy statement treats stairs/ramps no more than 1.5 m above, or no more than 600 mm below, adjacent ground as outside the opening-protection application described there [source p.1].
For a below-grade passageway, its two-remote-stair discussion preserves smoke/fire separation and protected doors; the two escape directions must be sufficiently independent [source pp.2, 9–12].
The document describes 9 m as a remoteness target, with qualifications; it is not a universal measured minimum imposed by Part 9.
For cases outside the interpretation, its prescribed protection or specific-variance route remains relevant.
Source W7.

**Source errors to avoid copying:** B23-05 p.2 points to Div A Clause 1.2.1.1.(1)(a) for alternative solutions and to the wrong division for its documentation section (the bulletin prints "Division B" before the section numbered 2.3).
The correct references are Div A Clause 1.2.1.1.(1)(b) [NBC(AE) 2023 p2 PDF p.38] and Div C Article 2.3.1.1. [PDF p.739].
These citation errors do not authorize rewriting or expanding the policy; they require accurate references in a new application.

### Edmonton B23-03 and STANDATA 23-BCI-015R1: roof trusses

B23-03, last revised February 4, 2026 [source p.1], puts authenticated roof-truss assemblies into Edmonton's new-house application package effective March 1, 2026.
It distinguishes the assembly from individual profiles. If interconnection information is not covered by other authenticated documentation, it must be covered on the authenticated layout.
The bulletin also states a local exception from that submission requirement for standard detached garages unless they have living space, attic storage or a loft.
That wording must not be treated as a general provincial exemption from engineering design.
The bulletin's opening text abbreviates the STANDATA identifier inconsistently; its discussion and the actual provincial document identify **23-BCI-015R1**.
Sources W9 and L6.

The provincial interpretation [source pp.1–3] requires engineering design/authentication for wood trusses and truss assemblies, including relevant interconnections, and allows permit-condition timing as described there.
Edmonton's at-application timing is a separate, stricter local submission arrangement.

### STANDATA 23-BCV-002: off-site constructed housing

This May 2024 provincial variance [source pp.1–2] is relevant to modular/factory-built production housing, not a general secondary-suite relaxation.
Its alternatives include the specified accredited CSA A660 steel-module certification, professional verification with authenticated documentation, or authority inspections at the place of work.
Where off-site inspection is infeasible, its note permits mutually agreed critical on-site checks established at plan examination.
It excludes relocatable industrial camp buildings and preserves service-installation assurance requirements.
The underlying administrative provision is NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div C, Article 2.5.1.1. [PDF p.742].
Source W11.

## Worked example

### Hypothetical intake: three row houses with three secondary suites

This is a teaching case, not an approved design or submitted application.

| Intake field | Example information |
|---|---|
| Location | Edmonton; municipal address, permit number, legal descriptions and owner authorization must be supplied for a real application. |
| Form | Three attached principal row houses, H1, H2 and H3, each with one basement secondary suite, S1, S2 and S3. Six dwellings in total. |
| Ownership arrangement | Each principal dwelling and its suite form one real estate entity. Party walls lie on the boundaries between the row-house parcels. |
| Size/use | Entire row is assumed to be 240 m² building area and two storeys in building height; residential use only. Those measurements require drawing confirmation. |
| Construction | Unsprinklered wood-frame proposal; one-hour party-wall separations between houses. |
| Other work | Suite smoke barriers, doors, alarms, sound separation, independent duct systems, bedroom escape windows and exits are proposed to meet their applicable requirements. Their detailed evidence remains part of the submission. |
| Requested departure | Retain one-hour party walls instead of introducing the firewall division required for this arrangement. |

The phrase "three secondary suites" is resolved before code selection.
This case has one qualifying suite in each of three houses; it does not place three secondary suites within one house.
Definition source: NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.50].

### Classification and the prescribed comparison

The assumed overall size and use fit Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) [PDF p.40].
The entire 240 m² row is considered for this check; the proposed one-hour walls are not asserted to divide it into separate buildings.

Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.2.(1) [PDF p.889] offers the one-hour party-wall exception, but Sentence (2) [PDF pp.889–890] restricts it for this multi-house configuration.
The prescribed arrangement must create buildings containing no more than two adjacent houses with secondary suites.
Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.3.(1) [PDF p.890] refers actual firewall construction to Part 3.
For the stated residential-only case, Sentence 3.1.10.2.(2) [PDF p.162] supplies the minimum two-hour, noncombustible firewall benchmark, together with the relevant construction and structural-stability provisions.
The proposal is therefore a real departure from the printed prescription, not merely a different name for a compliant firewall.

### Precedent match before a new variance
