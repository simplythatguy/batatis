## Plain-language summary
Construction type is one of the first facts that sets how tall and how large a Part 3 building can be. The code sorts structure into three categories: combustible, noncombustible, and encapsulated mass timber construction (EMTC). Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.43] and [PDF p.44].

Combustible construction is any structure that does not meet the noncombustible or EMTC definitions. Noncombustible construction uses noncombustible materials for structural members and other assemblies. EMTC uses mass timber elements, such as cross-laminated timber, that are protected by materials with an encapsulation rating.

For a Group C residential building in Part 3, combustible construction is limited to six storeys, 18 m in height, and sprinklered throughout. Area limits fall from 9 000 m² at one storey to 1 500 m² at six storeys. Div B, Article 3.2.2.51. [PDF p.189].

A combustible residential building built outside that route may not have an occupied floor more than 11 m above the lowest exit level. Div B, Article 3.2.1.7. [PDF p.175].

Residential buildings up to 12 storeys, 42 m high, and 6 000 m² in area are permitted when they are sprinklered throughout and built of EMTC or noncombustible construction, alone or combined. Div B, Article 3.2.2.48. [PDF p.188]. Group D business buildings have the same 12-storey route with a 7 200 m² area limit. Div B, Article 3.2.2.57. [PDF p.191].

EMTC carries its own conditions. Structural mass timber needs an encapsulation rating of at least 50 min on exposed surfaces, exposed mass timber is capped by area, and cladding and finishes have their own combustibility limits. Div B, Article 3.1.6.4. [PDF p.146], Article 3.1.6.9. [PDF p.148] and Article 3.1.6.14. [PDF p.150].

Interior finishes have flame-spread limits. The general limit is 150. Exits are limited to 25 whether or not the building is sprinklered. Public corridor walls and ceilings are limited to 75 and 25 unless the floor area is sprinklered. Div B, Article 3.1.13.2. and Table 3.1.13.2. [PDF p.166]; Div B, Article 3.1.13.6. [PDF pp.167 to 168].

Part 9 applies to buildings of three storeys or less and 600 m² or less in building area, used for major occupancies such as residential, business and mercantile. Its interior-surface flame-spread limit is 150. Div A, Article 1.3.3.3. [PDF p.40]; Div B, Article 9.10.17.1. [PDF p.906].

A design outside these rules, such as a CLT firewall, combustible soffits, or a building taller than the EMTC limit, needs an alternative solution accepted by the authority having jurisdiction. This sheet states what the code says and where. It does not decide whether a design is safe.

## When this topic applies
Trigger signals. Any one of these warrants this sheet.
- Wood is the structure or a major structural element, described as glulam, CLT, NLT, "mass timber", "EMTC", "encapsulated", or a hybrid of mass timber with concrete or steel, in a Part 3 building.
- A Group C or Group D building of four or more storeys, or any Part 3 building that may exceed a 3.2.2 limit. Examples are a podium over 1 500 m² at six storeys, a combustible building over 18 m, or a combustible building over six storeys. Div B, Article 3.2.2.51. [PDF p.189].
- A residential floor more than 11 m above the lowest exit level in combustible construction. Div B, Article 3.2.1.7. [PDF p.175].
- A sloping site or stepped building where the grade and the first storey decide the storey count and height. Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF pp.43 to 46].
- Exposed wood, cedar planks, or coated wood in lobbies, corridors, exits, or ceilings. Div B, Table 3.1.13.2. [PDF p.166]; Div B, Article 3.1.6.14. [PDF p.150].
- Combustible cladding, soffits, canopies, marquees, or wood exterior wall assemblies on a building that must be noncombustible or EMTC. Div B, Articles 3.1.4.8. [PDF p.134], 3.1.5.5. [PDF p.137], 3.1.5.24. [PDF p.144], 3.1.6.9. [PDF p.148] and 3.1.6.10. [PDF p.149].
- Firewalls or supports for fire separations made of wood or mass timber. Div B, Articles 3.1.10.2. [PDF p.162], 3.1.7.5. [PDF p.152] and 3.1.8.2. [PDF p.152]; Div B, Article 9.10.9.10. [PDF p.885].

- A request to show the fire-resistance rating of a mass timber member by char calculation or modelling. Appendix D, Subsection D-2.11, Method B covers this calculation (PDF pages 710 and 711).
- Fire-retardant-treated (FRT) wood, FRT wood roof decks, or a fire-retardant treatment claim. Div B, Articles 3.1.4.5. [PDF p.133] and 3.1.14.1. [PDF p.169].

## Benchmark acceptable solutions
Which part of the code applies comes first. Part 3 applies to Group A, B and F1 buildings, and to Group C, D, E, F2 and F3 buildings that exceed 600 m² in building area or three storeys in building height. Part 9 applies to Group B Division 4, C, D, E, F2 and F3 buildings that are three storeys or less and 600 m² or less. Div A, Article 1.3.3.2. [PDF p.40]; Div A, Article 1.3.3.3. [PDF p.40].

Building height and area are measured for the whole building. The height and area of the entire building set the fire-safety requirements for each major occupancy. Div B, Article 3.2.2.5. [PDF p.176]. Building height is the number of storeys between the roof and the floor of the first storey. Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.43]. The first storey is the uppermost storey with its floor level not more than 2 m above grade. Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.45]. Grade is the lowest of the average levels of finished ground adjoining each exterior wall. Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.46]. Height in metres for Div B, Article 3.2.2.48. is measured from the first storey floor to the uppermost floor level, not counting a rooftop enclosure. Div B, Article 3.2.2.48. [PDF p.188]; Div B, Article 3.2.1.1. [PDF p.173].

Group C residential (Part 3). Every route in Articles 3.2.2.47. to 3.2.2.55. requires sprinklers throughout, subject to the exceptions written into those articles. Articles 3.2.2.50., 3.2.2.53. and 3.2.2.54. are "Reserved". Div B, Articles 3.2.2.50. and 3.2.2.53. [PDF p.189 to 190].

Materials, EMTC and finishes. These articles carry the rules that apply inside any of the routes above.
- Combustible construction may be built of combustible materials, with or without noncombustible components. Div B, Sentence 3.1.4.1.(1) [PDF p.132].
- FRT wood must be pressure impregnated under CAN/CSA-O80 and have a flame-spread rating of no more than 25. Div B, Sentence 3.1.4.5.(1) [PDF p.133].
- Heavy timber may replace combustible construction where no rating above 45 min is required. Div B, Articles 3.1.4.6. and 3.1.4.7. [PDF pp.133 to 134].
- Noncombustible construction uses noncombustible materials, with the exceptions in Articles 3.1.5.2. to 3.1.5.24. and Article 3.2.2.16. Div B, Sentence 3.1.5.1.(1) [PDF p.135]. A material may instead meet the CAN/ULC-S135 limits: average total heat release of no more than 3 MJ/m² and smoke extinction area of no more than 1.0 m² at 50 kW/m². Div B, Sentences 3.1.5.1.(2) to (4) [PDF p.135].
- Combustible cladding is permitted in a noncombustible building that is three storeys or less or sprinklered throughout, if the wall passes CAN/ULC-S134. Flaming may spread no more than 5 m above the opening, and heat flux at 3.5 m above the opening may not exceed 35 kW/m². Div B, Sentence 3.1.5.5.(1) [PDF p.137].
- Combustible interior finishes in a noncombustible building must be no thicker than 25 mm, except thin finishes of 1 mm. Walls may reach a flame-spread rating of 150, and ceilings 25, with 10% of the ceiling area allowed at 150. Div B, Sentences 3.1.5.12.(1) to (4) [PDF p.139].
- Subsection 3.1.6 governs EMTC. Materials conform to Subsection 3.1.5 except where Subsection 3.1.6 provides otherwise. Div B, Articles 3.1.6.1. and 3.1.6.2. [PDF p.145].
- Structural mass timber must be in heavy solid masses with no concealed spaces, except as Sentence 3.1.6.3.(4) allows, and must meet the minimum dimensions in Table 3.1.6.3. Div B, Sentences 3.1.6.3.(2) and (4) [PDF pp.145 to 146]. Table 3.1.6.3. is not transcribed here. Cross-laminated timber adhesives must meet ANSI/APA PRG 320. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.3.(3) [PDF p.145].
- Exposed surfaces of structural mass timber must be protected from adjacent spaces, including concealed spaces, by a material with an encapsulation rating of at least 50 min. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.4.(1) [PDF p.146]. Gypsum board, gypsum concrete, noncombustible materials and materials meeting Sentences 3.1.5.1.(2) to (4) qualify. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.4.(2) [PDF p.146].
- Exposed mass timber is allowed inside a suite within limits. Beams, columns and arches may be exposed up to 10% of the perimeter wall area at a flame-spread rating of 150 or less. Walls facing one direction may be exposed if the aggregate exposed area is no more than 35% of the perimeter wall area. Ceilings may be exposed up to 10% of ceiling area at 150 or less, or up to 25% at 75 or less where the suite has no exposed mass timber walls. Div B, Sentences 3.1.6.4.(3) to (6) [PDF p.146].
- Deemed encapsulation. A 38 mm gypsum-concrete or concrete topping on the upper side, or two layers of 12.7 mm Type X gypsum board fastened as Sentence 3.1.6.6.(2) specifies, is deemed to give 50 min. Div B, Sentences 3.1.6.6.(1) and (2) [PDF pp.147 to 148]. Other encapsulation ratings come from CAN/ULC-S146 tests. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.5.(1) [PDF p.147].
- EMTC exterior cladding must be noncombustible, except for listed combustible options. Those options include combustible cladding that runs over no more than four storeys, covers up to 10% of each storey's wall, is no more than 1.2 m wide, has a flame-spread rating of 75 or less, and is separated from other combustible cladding by the spacing stated in the sentence. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.9.(1) and Sentence 3.1.6.9.(2)(a) [PDF pp.148 to 149]. Up to 100% of the first storey may be combustible cladding if it is within 15 m of a street or access route. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.9.(2)(c) [PDF p.149]. The permitted area drops to 5% where the fire department takes longer than 10 min to arrive in 10% or more of its calls. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.9.(3) [PDF p.149]. FRT wood cladding must be weathered before fire testing. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.9.(6) [PDF p.149].
- EMTC interior finishes. Combustible ceiling finishes of 25 mm or less must have a flame-spread rating of 25 or less. Up to 10% of the ceiling area in each fire compartment may reach 150. Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.14.(3) [PDF p.150].
- Only materials permitted in noncombustible construction may be exposed in EMTC concealed spaces, except a concealed space 25 mm deep or less behind a protective material. Div B, Sentences 3.1.6.16.(1) and (2) [PDF p.151].
- Interior finishes. Walls and ceilings may not exceed a flame-spread rating of 150, subject to Table 3.1.13.2. Div B, Sentence 3.1.13.2.(1) [PDF p.166]. Table 3.1.13.2. sets 25 for exits, lobbies, covered vehicular passageways and vertical service spaces, sprinklered or not. It sets 150 sprinklered and 75 unsprinklered for Group A1 and Group B. Div B, Table 3.1.13.2. [PDF p.166].
- Public corridors. Walls of a public corridor may not exceed 75, or 150 where the floor area is sprinklered throughout. Ceilings may not exceed 25, or 150 where the floor area is sprinklered throughout. Div B, Sentences 3.1.13.6.(1), (3), (5) and (6) [PDF pp.167 to 168].
- EMTC buildings. The flame-spread ratings of Subsection 3.1.6 apply in addition to Subsection 3.1.13. The exit cut-through rule applies to the cut surfaces of exit finishes, except for doors, specified structural mass timber, heavy timber and FRT wood. Div B, Sentence 3.1.13.12.(1) [PDF p.169].
- Firewalls. A firewall must be a noncombustible fire separation with a fire-resistance rating of 2 h. Where the separated floor areas include Group E, F1 or F2, the rating is 4 h, with an upper portion of 2 h allowed. The rating is provided by masonry or concrete, except for a firewall of 2 h or less that meets Sentence 3.1.10.2.(4). Div B, Sentences 3.1.10.2.(1) to (4) [PDF p.162].
- Supports. A noncombustible assembly with a fire-resistance rating must be supported by noncombustible construction. In an EMTC building, that support may be EMTC. Div B, Sentences 3.1.7.5.(3) and (4) [PDF p.152]. Combustible construction that abuts or supports a noncombustible fire separation must not cause its collapse. Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.2.(1) [PDF p.152].

Part 9 (houses and small buildings). These articles govern the Part 9 counterpart.
- Part 9 flame-spread ratings are determined by the Part 3 test methods or by Appendix D. Div B, Sentence 9.10.3.2.(1) [PDF p.878].
- Where noncombustible construction is required, combustible elements are limited under Subsection 3.1.5. Div B, Sentence 9.10.6.1.(1) [PDF p.880].
- A noncombustible assembly with a fire-resistance rating must be supported by noncombustible construction. Div B, Sentence 9.10.8.4.(1) [PDF p.881].
- Combustible construction that abuts or supports a noncombustible fire separation must not collapse onto it. Div B, Sentence 9.10.9.10.(1) [PDF p.885].
- Interior wall and ceiling surfaces may not exceed a flame-spread rating of 150. Doors may reach 200, and doors inside dwelling units other than garage doors are exempt. Div B, Sentences 9.10.17.1.(1) to (3) [PDF p.906].
- In an unsprinklered public corridor, at least 90% of the wall surface must reach 75, or at least 90% of the upper half must reach 25. Div B, Sentence 9.10.17.5.(1) [PDF p.906].

## Typical alternative solutions
The CWC guide lists typical subjects for alternative solutions on wood buildings. Its numbering follows BC Building Code 2018 and some BC 2012 references, and it warns that BC 2024 changes may affect some subjects (CWC guide, page 9). Each subject below is matched to the AE 2023 provision with the same content. The match is by subject, not by article number.

Combustible residential beyond the storey limit, including a sloping site (guide, page 9). The guide gives six storeys as the combustible residential limit. It says a sloping site can push a building over six storeys, because the lowest average grade sets the first storey. In AE 2023 the combustible route in Div B, Article 3.2.2.51. is also capped at 18 m (Div B, Sentence 3.2.2.51.(1) [PDF p.189]). Outside that article, a combustible residential building may not have an occupied floor more than 11 m above the lowest exit level (Div B, Article 3.2.1.7. [PDF p.175]). The guide's six-storey statement is therefore incomplete for Alberta.

Combustible area over the limit (guide, page 10). The guide's example is a six-storey wood-frame podium over 1 500 m², and it cites BC Division B Article 3.2.2.50. In AE 2023 the same 1 500 m² limit for six storeys is in Div B, Sentence 3.2.2.51.(1) [PDF p.189]. Div B, Article 3.2.2.50. is Reserved [PDF p.189]. The article numbers differ, so the mapping rests on content.

A CLT firewall where a noncombustible firewall is prescribed (guide, pages 10 and 22). The guide allows a CLT firewall through an alternative solution, and it cites the BC rule for noncombustible firewalls. In AE 2023, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.2.(2) requires a fire separation of noncombustible construction with a 2 h rating [PDF p.162]. Noncombustible construction is defined by noncombustible materials for its members and assemblies (Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.48]), and CLT is a combustible material. Div B, Sentences 3.1.10.2.(3) and (4) add a masonry or concrete rule for the rating, with an exception for ratings of 2 h or less [PDF p.162]. A CLT firewall is therefore outside the acceptable solutions. The guide's case on page 22 is a seven-storey Group C project whose area is over the 1 500 m² limit for a single six-storey combustible building.

Combustible soffits in noncombustible buildings (guide, page 12). The guide says that only materials deemed noncombustible may be used for soffits in a noncombustible building, and that an alternative solution can cover a combustible soffit. In AE 2023 the general rule is Div B, Sentence 3.1.5.1.(1) [PDF p.135]. The only soffit relief written into the code is for decorative fire-retardant-treated wood on the fascias and soffits of marquees and canopies, in Div B, Sentence 3.1.5.24.(1) [PDF p.144]. A general combustible soffit therefore needs an alternative solution.

Exposed mass timber canopies (guide, page 12). Under Div B, Sentence 3.1.5.24.(1) [PDF p.144], only fire-retardant-treated wood that is conditioned under ASTM D2898 and tested to CAN/ULC-S102 may be used on marquee and canopy fascias and soffits. Exposed mass timber canopies therefore need an alternative solution. The guide says that solution should consider the explanatory notes for the provision (guide, page 12).

Treatment alternatives to pressure-impregnated FRT wood (guide, page 13). Div B, Sentence 3.1.4.5.(1) [PDF p.133] requires pressure impregnation under CAN/CSA-O80 and a flame-spread rating of 25 or less. An alternative treatment therefore needs an alternative solution. The guide suggests such products can serve as ceiling finishes under Div B, Sentence 3.1.5.12.(4) [PDF p.139]. That sentence also refers to FRT wood, so it does not admit an untreated product by itself.

EMTC beyond 12 storeys, or exposed mass timber beyond the encapsulation limits (guide, page 13). The guide gives 12 storeys as the EMTC limit, and it recommends early talks with the authority for taller buildings. In AE 2023, Div B, Sentence 3.2.2.48.(1) [PDF p.188] and Div B, Sentence 3.2.2.57.(1) [PDF p.191] set 12 storeys and 42 m. The exposed-surface limits are in Div B, Sentences 3.1.6.4.(3) to (6) [PDF p.146]. Anything beyond those values needs an alternative solution.

Fire-resistance ratings for mass timber by calculation (guide, page 15). The guide lists char analysis, engineering calculations or modelling, and a combination of passive and active measures as ways to justify a rating. The AE 2023 method for mass timber members is Appendix D, Subsection D-2.11, Method B, which refers to a calculation in Annex B (Appendix D, PDF pages 710 and 711). The numeric char rates were not verified in this pass.

Coated cedar ceilings (guide, pages 27 and 28). The guide's example is 25 mm Pacific yellow cedar planks with a flame-spread rating of 50 under CAN/ULC-S102, coated with a product that the manufacturer reports at 20 on red oak. In an EMTC building, Div B, Sentence 3.1.6.14.(3) [PDF p.150] limits combustible ceiling finishes of 25 mm or less to a flame-spread rating of 25, with up to 10% of the ceiling area in each fire compartment allowed at 150. A rating of 50 therefore fits only the 10% allowance, unless an alternative solution accepts the coating data. Corridor ceilings follow Div B, Sentences 3.1.13.6.(5) and (6) [PDF p.168], which set 25, or 150 where the floor area is sprinklered. The red oak figure is the guide's report of the manufacturer's data, and it is not verified here.

Site-specific regulation for a taller timber building (guide, page 13). The guide describes Brock Commons at UBC as a site-specific regulation used in place of an alternative solution to exceed the height and storey limits at the time of construction. The Ubyssey article dated 2016-09-28 describes the same route as a site-specific regulation, which it also calls a special permit, enabled by the B.C. Building Act passed in 2015. It names an 18-storey building and the six-storey wood-frame limit, and it names no fire-protection measures (Ubyssey, https://ubyssey.ca/features/permitting-of-brock-commons/, accessed 2026-10-18). Alberta's routes for the same problem are an alternative solution or a variance, as the 19-BCV-014 history shows (see Precedents).

Evidence ladder for this topic, strongest first. The evidence-and-conformity sheet owns the full ladder.

This case is illustrative. It is not a real project, and its numbers are chosen to show the route.

Case. A seven-storey apartment building, Group C, on a sloping site. Sprinklered throughout. Building area 1 100 m². Building height 21 m, measured from the first storey floor to the uppermost floor level. The team wants a mass timber frame.

Step 1, which Part applies. Group C residential over three storeys is a Part 3 building (Div A, Article 1.3.3.2. [PDF p.40]).

Step 2, storeys and height. Building height is the number of storeys between the roof and the first storey floor (Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.43]). The first storey is the uppermost storey with its floor not more than 2 m above grade (Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.45]). Grade is the lowest of the average finished-ground levels next to the exterior walls (Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.46]). On a sloping site, that lowest average sets the reference for the first storey. In this case the count gives seven storeys.

Step 3, which route. Div B, Sentence 3.2.2.51.(1) caps the combustible route at six storeys [PDF p.189], so that route is out. Div B, Article 3.2.2.49. stops at six storeys and requires noncombustible construction [PDF pp.188 to 189]. Div B, Sentence 3.2.2.48.(2) allows EMTC or noncombustible construction for up to 12 storeys, 42 m and 6 000 m² when sprinklered throughout (Div B, Sentence 3.2.2.48.(1) [PDF p.188]). The case meets Article 3.2.2.48: seven storeys, 21 m, 1 100 m², and sprinklered.

Step 4, the wood. Mass timber at this height means EMTC under Subsection 3.1.6, with the flame-spread rules in Article 3.1.13.12. Combustible construction is defined to exclude EMTC (Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.43]). So the 11 m rule in Div B, Article 3.2.1.7. [PDF p.175] does not attach to this EMTC building.
