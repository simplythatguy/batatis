## Plain-language summary
A fire separation is a wall, floor, or ceiling assembly that slows fire from crossing into another space (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.45]). A firewall is a fire separation of noncombustible construction that divides a building or separates two buildings (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.45]). A firewall divides a building into separate buildings for the Code's building-size purposes (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Sentence 1.3.3.4.(1) [PDF p.40]). A fire-resistance rating is the time an assembly holds up in a standard fire test.

Spatial separation limits fire spread from one building to the next. The limit depends on the limiting distance, which is the distance from the exposing wall to the property line, a street or lane, or an imaginary line between two buildings on one property (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.47]). Closer walls allow fewer unprotected openings, need more fire resistance, and need more restrictive cladding.

For a house, the glazing table allows 0% glazed area below a limiting distance of 1.2 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Table 9.10.15.4. [PDF p.902]). Below 0.6 m, the wall needs a 45 min rating and specified cladding (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(2) [PDF p.902]). Roof soffits must stay 0.45 m from a property line where the limiting distance is over 0.45 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(9) [PDF p.904]).

Two Alberta routes make zero-lot-line houses possible. Calgary's Standing Posted Variance SPV-001 is a fixed package for paired houses with a registered 1.5 m maintenance easement. Edmonton's policy B14-01 places a deemed limiting-distance line midway between the houses. Both change the construction details and the openings allowed. Neither is a general permission.

Row houses with secondary suites are split by firewalls under the Code (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.2.(2) [PDF p.889]). Edmonton's policy B23-01 accepts a 1 h party wall for qualifying houses. Calgary's SPV-006 accepts a package of other protections in place of the firewall.

This sheet cites NBC(AE) 2023 only. Historical context: Edmonton's B14-01 is written against the superseded ABC 2014 edition, so its sentence references need checking. Standing posted variances and policies change over time. Confirm the current version with the authority having jurisdiction before relying on one.

## When this topic applies
Concrete trigger signals:

- A wall, eave, or balcony within 1.2 m of a property line, a zero or reduced side yard, or a request to build to the line.
- Windows, doors, glass block, wired glass, or other openings in a wall that faces another building, especially where the limiting distance is under 1.2 m.
- Roof soffits, eaves, or combustible projections that cross or approach a property line or sit within 1.2 m of another house's wall.
- A party wall between dwelling units, houses, or secondary suites, including row houses with more than two adjacent houses with suites.
- A penetration of a rated wall, floor, or ceiling: a duct, pipe, outlet box, cable, or service equipment. Also a joint between a floor and an exterior wall, or between a rated separation and another assembly.
- A firewall, new or altered, including its parapet height, openings in it, or framing that bears on it (including mass-timber firewalls).
- Clear, sprinkler-protected, or other glazing in a fire-rated wall or in a fire separation (STANDATA 23-BCV-004).
- An interior opening larger than the Sentence 3.1.8.6.(1) and (2) limits, or several large openings in one fire separation.
- A request that depends on limiting distance, exposing building face area, the glazed or unprotected opening area, or a fire department response-time claim.
- Suites in multi-unit residential buildings that need a 45 min or 1 h separation (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.9.16.(1) [PDF p.886] and Sentence 9.10.9.16.(3) [PDF p.886]).

Near-misses that belong to another topic:

- Exit routes, exit stairs, travel distance, and exit width belong to egress-and-single-stair. An exit that passes through a fire separation touches this topic only for the separation itself.
- Sprinkler design, fire alarms, and standpipes belong to firefighting-alarms-sprinklers. Sprinklers come into this topic only as a condition of a rated-glazing or opening rule.
- Cladding and insulation performance on its own (flame spread, foamed plastic insulation outside a fire separation, thermal values) belongs to building-envelope-insulation-cladding. Cladding that a limiting distance rule requires comes here.
- Combustible construction ratings and mass-timber floor or roof assemblies belong to combustible-construction-and-mass-timber. A mass-timber firewall or curtain-wall joint comes here.
- Roof truss design, framing connections, and structural loads belong to structural-and-framing. Sentence 3.1.10.1.(1) is the only bridge.
- Window opening control devices (STANDATA 23-BCV-001; Edmonton B23-02) serve Sentence 9.8.8.1.(4), which requires openable windows in residential buildings to have a guard or a tool-release mechanism that limits any clear opening to 100 mm (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.8.8.1.(4) [PDF p.859]). That rule addresses falls, not fire spread, so it belongs to the housing or occupancy topic.
- Backwater valves and plumbing (Calgary P15-002, SPV-007) belong to hvac-ventilation-plumbing.
- Duct and damper design as an HVAC matter belongs to hvac-ventilation-plumbing. A duct through a rated wall comes here (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.13.13.(1) [PDF p.893]).
- Glazing area for energy performance or daylight belongs to energy-efficiency.
- Fire spread from construction sites during construction or demolition belongs to process-and-admin or the firefighting topic, not here.

## Benchmark acceptable solutions

### Which subsection governs: Part 9 house rules or Part 9 other buildings or Part 3
Part 9 splits its spatial separation rules by the layout of dwelling units, not by size alone. Subsection 9.10.15 applies to buildings that contain only dwelling units with no dwelling unit above another, and to houses with a secondary suite including their common spaces (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.1.(1) [PDF p.900]). Subsection 9.10.14 applies to the other buildings to which Part 9 applies (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.14.1.(1) [PDF p.894]), and it does not apply to detached carports that serve one dwelling unit or one house with a secondary suite (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.14.1.(2) [PDF p.894]). Buildings outside Part 9 follow Part 3.

Part 9 refers to Part 3 in several places. Sentence 9.10.15.4.(1) lets a house conform to Subsection 3.2.3 instead of Table 9.10.15.4 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(1) [PDF p.901]). Article 9.10.8.11. lets floors, roofs, loadbearing walls, columns and arches conform to Section 3.2 instead of Subsection 9.10.8 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Article 9.10.8.11. [PDF p.882]).

### Fire-resistance ratings and exposure direction (Part 3)
A rating comes from a standard fire test under CAN/ULC-S101 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.1.(1) [PDF p.151]). Sentence 3.1.7.1.(2) lets an assembly take a rating from Appendix D instead (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.1.(2) [PDF p.151]).

Appendix D, titled Fire-Performance Ratings, begins on Division B PDF page 677. The intent of Sentence 3.1.7.1.(2) is to treat Appendix D ratings as equivalent to test ratings (see Intent).

The exposure direction depends on the element. Floor, roof and ceiling assemblies are exposed on the underside (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.3.(1) [PDF p.151]). Firewalls and interior vertical fire separations are exposed on each side (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.3.(2) [PDF p.151]). Exterior walls are exposed from inside the building (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.3.(3) [PDF p.151]). Sentence 3.1.7.2.(1) removes the temperature-rise limit for an exterior wall with a limiting distance of 1.2 m or more, provided the radiation correction in Sentence 3.2.3.1.(9) is made (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.2.(1) [PDF p.151]). A rating above the required minimum creates no duty to exceed the minimum elsewhere (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.7.4.(1) [PDF p.151]).

### Continuity, firestops and closures (Part 3)
A fire separation that must be rated is built as a continuous element in line with Article 3.1.8.3. (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.1.(1) [PDF p.152]). Openings in a fire separation are protected by closures, shafts or other means under Articles 3.1.8.4. to 3.1.8.19. and Subsections 3.1.9. and 3.2.8. (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.1.(2) [PDF p.152]).

Where a rated fire separation abuts another fire separation, a floor, a ceiling or a roof, the joint needs a firestop with an FT rating at least equal to the rating of the abutting separation, tested to CAN/ULC-S115 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.3.(2) [PDF p.152] and Sentence 3.1.8.3.(3) [PDF p.152]). A horizontal joint between a floor and an exterior wall needs a firestop tested to ASTM E2307, the perimeter fire barrier test (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.3.(4) [PDF p.152]). Sentence 3.1.8.3.(5) exempts gypsum board joints that meet its conditions (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.3.(5) [PDF p.152]).

Penetrations of a rated fire separation are sealed with a firestop tested to CAN/ULC-S115 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.9.1.(1) [PDF p.159]). Sprinklers and fire dampers may penetrate without meeting that firestop rule when their conditions are met (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.9.1.(4) [PDF p.159] and Sentence 3.1.9.1.(5) [PDF p.159]).

Closure ratings come from test standards, such as CAN/ULC-S104 for doors and CAN/ULC-S106 for windows and glass block (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.4.(1) [PDF p.153]). The closure rating must match Table 3.1.8.4. for the required rating of the separation (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.4.(2) [PDF p.153]). Two closures of equal rating on opposite sides of one opening are deemed to add their ratings, except for dampers, window assemblies and glass block (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.5.(1) [PDF p.153]). Closures in a required fire separation are installed to NFPA 80 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.5.(2) [PDF p.153]).

Maximum opening size in an interior fire separation depends on sprinklers. An opening may be up to 11 m² with no dimension over 3.7 m if a fire compartment on either side is not sprinklered (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.6.(1) [PDF p.154]). It may be up to 22 m² with no dimension over 6 m if both sides are sprinklered (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.6.(2) [PDF p.154]).

### Firewalls (Part 3, applied through Part 9)
A firewall is a type of fire separation of noncombustible construction that subdivides a building or separates adjoining buildings (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.45]). A firewall that separates floor areas with a Group E or Group F, Division 1 or 2 major occupancy needs a 4 h rating, except that the upper portion may be 2 h where it separates other occupancies (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.2.(1) [PDF p.162]). Other major occupancies need a 2 h rating (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.2.(2) [PDF p.162]). The firewall rating must come from masonry or concrete, except for closures (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.2.(3) [PDF p.162]). A firewall rated 2 h or less may be built of other materials if the assembly is protected from damage and the design follows Article 4.1.5.17. (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.2.(4) [PDF p.162]).

A firewall runs from the ground continuously through all storeys (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.3.(1) [PDF p.162]). It extends above the roof as a parapet of at least 150 mm for a 2 h wall and 900 mm for a 4 h wall (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.4.(1) [PDF p.162]). The parapet is not needed where the two roofs differ in elevation by more than 3 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.4.(2) [PDF p.162]). Openings follow the size limits in Article 3.1.8.6. (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.5.(1) [PDF p.162]). The total width of openings in a firewall within one storey is at most 25% of the firewall length (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.5.(2) [PDF p.162]).

Framing that connects to or sits on a firewall, and has a lower rating than the firewall, must be designed so that its failure in a fire does not collapse the firewall (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.1.(1) [PDF p.161]). Where two separate wall assemblies, each tied to its own frame, form the firewall, that rule does not apply if each assembly meets its conditions (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.1.(2) [PDF p.161]).

Where a firewall is used in Part 9, the Part 3 requirements apply (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.3.(1) [PDF p.890]).

### Spatial separation in Part 3 (Subsection 3.2.3)
The area of unprotected openings in an exposing building face is limited by the limiting distance. The limit comes from Table 3.2.3.1.-B or -C for an unsprinklered fire compartment, or from Table 3.2.3.1.-D or -E for a sprinklered one (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.1.(1) [PDF p.204]). The percentage is taken against the exposing building face area (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.1.(2) [PDF p.204]). I read Table 3.2.3.1.-B from its rendered page (PDF p.207). At a limiting distance of 0 m, every entry in that table reads 0%, so an unsprinklered Part 3 wall on the property line has no unprotected openings allowed by the table alone. The percentage rises as the limiting distance increases.

Smaller rules apply on top of the area limit. Individual openings are capped at short limiting distances (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.1.(5) [PDF p.204]). Openings serving one room or space must be 2 m apart horizontally or vertically (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.1.(6) [PDF p.205]). The limiting distance is halved for input to the tables where the fire department takes more than 10 min to begin suppression in 10% or more of calls and any storey is unsprinklered (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.1.(8) [PDF p.205]). Radiation from a hot unexposed wall surface adds an equivalent area of unprotected opening (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.1.(9) [PDF p.205]). A closure that does not match the wall's protection adds one too (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.1.(10) [PDF p.206]).

The exposing building face is the wall area facing one direction from finished ground level to the uppermost ceiling (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.2.(1) [PDF p.211]). A building divided by fire separations with a rating of at least 45 min may use each fire compartment's own area (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.2.(2) [PDF p.211]).

A party wall must be a firewall (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.4.(1) [PDF p.212]). Openings in a wall with a limiting distance under 1.2 m need closures whose rating matches the wall's required rating (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.5.(1) [PDF p.212]). Wired glass and glass block are not allowed as those closures (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.5.(2) [PDF p.212]).

Construction and cladding of an exposing building face follow Table 3.2.3.7. For Groups A, B, C, D and F, Division 3 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.7.(1) [PDF p.212]), and for Group E and F, Division 1 and 2 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.7.(2) [PDF p.213]). Sentences 3.2.3.7.(3) and (4) waive the noncombustible-cladding requirement in some cases (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.7.(3) [PDF p.213] and Sentence 3.2.3.7.(4) [PDF p.213]).

Foamed plastic insulation in an exterior wall of a building more than 3 storeys must be protected where the maximum permitted area of unprotected openings is more than 10% of the exposing building face (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.8.(1) [PDF p.214]). Structural members within 3 m of the property line are protected from exterior fire exposure (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.9.(1) [PDF p.214]).

Unlimited openings are allowed in two cases. An open-air storage garage with a limiting distance of at least 3 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.10.(1) [PDF p.215]), and a storey at street level with a limiting distance of at least 9 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.10.(2) [PDF p.215]).

### Houses: spatial separation under Subsection 9.10.15
The exposing building face area for a house is the wall area facing one direction from finished ground level to the uppermost ceiling, or per fire compartment or portion where the house table is used (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.2.(1) [PDF p.900]). The limiting distance is halved under the same fire department and sprinkler conditions as Part 3 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.3.(1) [PDF p.900]).

Glazing has three compliance routes. The glazed area may conform to Table 9.10.15.4., to Subsection 3.2.3, or, where the limiting distance is 1.2 m or more, be no more than the limiting distance squared (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(1) [PDF p.901]). Table 9.10.15.4. (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Table 9.10.15.4. [PDF p.902]) gives the maximum aggregate glazing as a percentage of the exposing building face, by limiting distance and face area. I read the table from its rendered page. For a 30 m² face the values are 0% below 1.2 m, 7% at 1.2 m, 9% at 1.5 m, 12% at 2.0 m, 39% at 4.0 m, 88% at 6.0 m and 100% at 8.0 m. For a face over 100 m² the values are 0% below 1.2 m, 7% at 1.2 m, 7% at 1.5 m, 8% at 2.0 m, 12% at 4.0 m, 19% at 6.0 m and 28% at 8.0 m. The table prints a dash where no percentage is given. The printed page does not define the dash, so this sheet does not say what the dash permits (UNVERIFIED).

Where the limiting distance is 2 m or less, an individual glazed opening or group of glazed openings may not exceed 50% of the aggregate, unless the building is sprinklered or the opening is an openable window with an unobstructed opening of 0.35 m² (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(3) [PDF p.901]). Glazed openings serving one room or space keep 2 m spacing (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(4) [PDF p.901]). Glass block, or a sprinklered building whose adjacent rooms are sprinklered, permits up to twice the glazed area (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(7) [PDF p.901]).

Wall construction is set below a limiting distance of 1.2 m. Where the limiting distance is less than 0.6 m, the face and any wall above it that encloses an attic or roof space need a 45 min fire-resistance rating and one of three cladding routes: metal or noncombustible cladding under Section 9.20, 9.27 or 9.28; cladding that meets Clause (2)(b), which requires conformance with Subsection 9.27.12, no furring over gypsum sheathing at least 12.7 mm thick or over masonry, a flame-spread rating of 25 or less, and a thickness of 2 mm or less; or a wall assembly tested to CAN/ULC-S134 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(2) [PDF p.902]). From 0.6 m to less than 1.2 m, the same 45 min rating applies with a wider set of cladding routes, including Subsections 9.27.6. to 9.27.10. and 9.27.11. (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(3) [PDF p.902]). Sentences (2) and (3) set the construction only below 1.2 m, so they do not set a rating for a house face at 1.2 m or more.

Combustible projections more than 1 m above grade that could expose an adjacent building to fire spread may not sit within 1.2 m of a property line, or within 2.4 m of another building's combustible projection on the same property (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(5) [PDF p.903]). Sentence 9.10.15.5.(6) exempts buildings with one or two dwelling units and some detached garages (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(6) [PDF p.903]).

Roof soffits are controlled by distance from the property line. Where the limiting distance is 0.45 m or less, no soffit may project above the exposing building face (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(8) [PDF p.904]). Where it is more than 0.45 m, the soffit face may not come closer than 0.45 m to the property line (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(9) [PDF p.904]). A soffit may project to the property line where it faces a public way (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(10) [PDF p.904]). Where a soffit projects to less than 1.2 m from the property line, it has no openings and is protected by sheet steel, unvented aluminum or gypsum at the thicknesses the sentence gives (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(11) [PDF p.904]).

Heavy timber and steel columns are exempt from Sentence (1) at a limiting distance of 3 m or more (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(13) [PDF p.904]).

### Buildings other than houses: Subsection 9.10.14
Subsection 9.10.14 limits unprotected openings, not glazed openings, by three routes: Table 9.10.14.4.-A, Subsection 3.2.3, or a limiting-distance formula (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.14.4.(1) [PDF p.895]). The formula applies where the limiting distance is 1.2 m or more. It gives the limiting distance squared for residential, business and personal services and low-hazard industrial occupancies, and half of that for mercantile and medium-hazard industrial occupancies (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.14.4.(1) [PDF p.895]). An opening under 1.2 m needs a closure other than wired glass or glass block whose rating matches the wall (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.14.4.(2) [PDF p.895]). Construction of the face follows Table 9.10.14.5.-A (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.14.5.(1) [PDF p.897]).

### Party walls, row houses and secondary suites (Part 9)
A party wall on a property line must be a firewall (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.1.(1) [PDF p.889]). Sentence 9.10.11.2.(1) allows a 1 h fire separation instead where the party wall separates two dwelling units with no unit above another, a dwelling unit from a house with a secondary suite, or two houses with secondary suites, all including their common spaces (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.2.(1) [PDF p.889]). Sentence (2) overrides that allowance in a building with more than two houses. The party wall between any two adjacent houses with secondary suites, where it separates them from the rest of the building, must be a firewall that splits the building into separate buildings, each with no more than two adjacent houses with secondary suites (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.2.(2) [PDF p.889]). Any 1 h wall that is used must run from the top of the footings to the underside of the roof deck, and the space above it is tightly filled with mineral wool or noncombustible material (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.11.2.(3) [PDF p.890] and Sentence 9.10.11.2.(4) [PDF p.890]).

Suites in residential occupancies are separated by 45 min fire separations (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.9.16.(1) [PDF p.886]). Dwelling units with two or more storeys are separated from the rest of the building by 1 h (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.9.16.(3) [PDF p.886]). In a house with a secondary suite, a continuous gypsum smoke-tight barrier can replace the 45 min separation for walls and floor-ceiling framing (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.9.16.(4) [PDF p.886]).

### Exterior walls at an angle, soffit protection and closures in Part 9
Where exterior walls meet at an external angle of 135° or less, unprotected openings in different fire compartments, or in different dwelling units, ancillary spaces or common spaces of a house with a secondary suite, must be at least 1.2 m apart horizontally (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.12.3.(1) [PDF p.890]). Where a soffit is within 2.5 m vertically above and 1.2 m laterally from a window or door (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.12.4.(1) [PDF p.890]), and it encloses a common attic or roof space that spans more than two suites and projects beyond the wall, it must have no unprotected openings and must be protected (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.12.4.(2) [PDF p.890] and Sentence 9.10.12.4.(3) [PDF p.891]).

Openings in required fire separations are protected with closures from Table 9.10.13.1., installed to NFPA 80 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.13.1.(1) [PDF p.891]). A duct that penetrates a rated fire separation needs a fire damper under Articles 3.1.8.4. and 3.1.8.10., except where Sentences 9.10.13.13.(2) to (5) apply (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.13.13.(1) [PDF p.893]).

## Typical alternative solutions
The proposals below are the ones that reached an Alberta authority in a written document. Each is a package, and each is stated as a condition on a specific project.

Paired zero-lot-line houses. Calgary's SPV-001 accepts a zero side wall at the property line with a 1.5 m maintenance easement on the neighbour's side. The package sets the exposing-face construction, balcony and soffit rules, interior gypsum, and the openings allowed (Calgary SPV-001, source p.2 to p.4 of 5). Edmonton's B14-01 instead places a deemed limiting-distance line midway between the two houses, provided a registered easement covers both titles (Edmonton B14-01, source p.1). Where the deemed limiting distance is under 1.2 m, windows must be 20-minute labelled window assemblies, 20-minute labelled shutter assemblies, or sprinkler-protected under the Standata the policy cites (Edmonton B14-01, source p.2).

Glazing in rated walls. STANDATA 23-BCV-004 accepts sprinkler-protected glazing in fire-resistant wall assemblies without a CAN/ULC-S101 test, if eight conditions are met (STANDATA 23-BCV-004, source p.1 to p.2 of 3). It is not a closure, and the STANDATA states it does not reduce the number of unprotected openings a wall may have (STANDATA 23-BCV-004, source p.1 of 3).

Row houses with secondary suites. Edmonton's B23-01 accepts a party wall with at least a 1 h fire separation in place of the firewall prescribed by Sentence 9.10.11.2.(2), for houses that conform to the rest of the house-with-suite requirements (Edmonton B23-01, source p.1). Calgary's SPV-006 instead accepts a package. Exits in a house with a secondary suite need a 45 min separation from adjacent floor areas, or none if the building is sprinklered (Calgary SPV-006, source p.2 of 5). Dwelling units and their ancillary and common spaces need a 45 min separation from each other, or none if sprinklered (Calgary SPV-006, source p.2 of 5). Loadbearing walls and columns in the storey below a floor carry the rating of the floor they support (Calgary SPV-006, source p.2 of 5). Closures carry a 20 min rating or are 45 mm solid core wood doors, and penetrations are sealed under Article 9.10.9.6. (Calgary SPV-006, source p.3 of 5). Ducts that penetrate a fire separation need no fire damper under conditions that include a melting point of 760°C or more and duct openings serving only one fire compartment (Calgary SPV-006, source p.3 of 5). Sprinklering is offered as an alternative to a second means of egress (Calgary SPV-006, source p.2 of 5). See housing-secondary-suites-part9 for how these conditions fit together.

Vented soffits. Historical context: STANDATA 19-BCV-017 (November 2020) was written against the superseded NBC(AE) 2019. Its NBC(AE) 2023 counterpart is STANDATA 23-BCV-018 (September 2025). Both accept a tested intumescent-coated vented soffit material where Sentences 9.10.14.5.(12), 9.10.15.5.(11), and 9.10.12.4.(2) and (3) would otherwise require solid, protected soffits (STANDATA 23-BCV-018, source p.1 of 4). The 2023 version requires the material to be tested by a laboratory accredited by the Standards Council of Canada, and its performance must be at least equivalent to 11 mm plywood, as Table 1 of its Appendix A specifies (STANDATA 23-BCV-018, source p.3 of 4).

Exposed faces of single-storey F3 buildings. Calgary's SPV-003 accepts a specified encapsulated wood-frame exposed-wall assembly for qualifying single-storey Group F, Division 3 Part 9 buildings that face dwellings they do not serve (Calgary SPV-003, source p.1 of 6). It rests on an NRC encapsulation test report and does not apply to houses.

Curtain-wall firestop at mass-timber floor edges. The CWC guide's sample alternative solution compares a proposed curtain-wall firestop to a design tested to ASTM E2307 that achieved a 2 h rating at a mass-timber floor edge (CWC-Alternative-Solutions-Guide, source p.31 to p.33). The guide is an industry guide and not an authority's acceptance.

Outside Alberta. The Calgary SPV-006 review states that other Canadian jurisdictions use passive fire protection, with fire separations between dwelling units and exits, and enhanced egress where shared egress is used (Calgary SPV-006, source p.1 of 5). That review does not name the jurisdictions. It is context only.

## Usual mitigations and evidence
Reviewers look for the same things across the packages: the rating of each separation, the continuity of each joint and penetration, the closure type and installation, the cladding and soffit construction, and a registered easement where a house relies on a neighbour's space. Calgary's SPV-001 lists the registration and the soffit and footing limits (Calgary SPV-001, source p.4 and p.5 of 5). Edmonton's B14-01 requires the easement to be registered against both land titles and a Letter of Construction Intention with the permit (Edmonton B14-01, source p.1 to p.2).

The evidence ladder for this topic runs from the least to the most evidence:

1. Code route in Division B, using the tables and sentences. The drawings show compliance, and no test report is needed beyond the rating the sentence names.
2. A standing posted variance or a policy. These are fixed packages. Calgary's SPV-001 and SPV-006 state that they cannot be altered, and that any change needs a site-specific variance (Calgary SPV-001, source p.1 of 5; Calgary SPV-006, source p.1 of 5).

5. Full-scale or research evidence cited as the basis for a package. Calgary's SPV-001 cites an NRC full-scale study of spatial separation (IRC-RR-195, 2005). This sheet could not verify that report (UNVERIFIED).

## Engineering / professional triggers
This sheet does not decide who must sign or seal a drawing. The professional-involvement topic covers that. The points below are the ones that touch fire separations.

- Calgary's SPV-006 states that a site-specific variance to vary Sentence 9.10.11.2.(2) needs professional involvement as part of the application (Calgary SPV-006, source p.1 of 5). The same document says the firewall's design is complex and needs professional involvement (Calgary SPV-006, source p.1 of 5).

- Sentence 3.1.10.1.(1) requires that framing connected to a firewall not collapse the firewall in a fire (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.1.(1) [PDF p.161]). The sentence states the outcome and does not name who designs it. The structural-and-framing topic covers the design work. The tool stops at the requirement.
- A fire-rated closure, glazing, or wall assembly that is not listed or tested for the use needs evidence that a reviewer can check. Whether that evidence needs a professional's seal is set by the professional-involvement topic.
- The tool checks citations and completeness. It does not decide whether a proposed assembly provides equivalent safety. That judgement belongs to the authority and to the licensed professional who signs the design.

- structural-and-framing: framing that bears on a firewall (Sentence 3.1.10.1.(1)) and the support of a rated separation (Sentence 9.10.8.3.(1)) are shared.
- firefighting-alarms-sprinklers: sprinklers change the rules for openings (Sentence 3.1.8.6.(2), Sentence 9.10.15.4.(7)) and for glazing in rated walls (STANDATA 23-BCV-004). The sprinkler design itself is in that topic.
- hvac-ventilation-plumbing: ducts through a rated wall need dampers (Sentence 9.10.13.13.(1) [PDF p.893]). The damper design and duct construction are in that topic.
- energy-efficiency: glazed area for energy performance is a separate calculation. The glazing limit in this topic applies whatever the energy calculation shows.
- process-and-admin: the variance route (standing posted variance, policy, or site-specific variance) and the permit documents are in that topic. This sheet describes what each route changes in the spatial-separation rules.
- evidence-and-conformity: the test standards and the evidence ladder for alternative solutions are covered there. The standards named in this sheet are the ones the Code names in this topic.
- code-structure-and-attribution: how to read the attribution rows and the root-sentence rule.

## Common refusal / "more info" causes
- Windows, doors, or glass block in a house wall within 1.2 m of a property line, with no variance or policy reference. Table 9.10.15.4. allows 0% glazed area there (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Table 9.10.15.4. [PDF p.902]).
- A zero-side wall with openings under Calgary SPV-001, which allows none below 1.2 m (Calgary SPV-001, source p.3 of 5).
- A missing or unregistered maintenance easement, or a neighbour's easement that does not match the package's 1.5 m width (Calgary SPV-001, source p.5 of 5; Edmonton B14-01, source p.1).
- Soffits that project closer than 0.45 m to a property line where the limiting distance is over 0.45 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(9) [PDF p.904]), or a soffit that projects into the easement beyond the package's limit (Calgary SPV-001, source p.4 of 5).
- A limiting distance measured to a line other than the ones the definition names: a property line, the centre line of a street, lane or public thoroughfare, or an imaginary line between two buildings on the same property (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.47]).
- An exposing building face area that combines faces, or omits the uppermost ceiling as the top of the measurement (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.2.(1) [PDF p.900]).
- Wired glass or glass block used as a closure at a limiting distance under 1.2 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.2.3.5.(2) [PDF p.212]; Sentence 9.10.14.4.(2) [PDF p.895] for buildings other than houses).
- Missing rating or firestop evidence for a joint or penetration, or a firestop that is not tested to CAN/ULC-S115 or, for a floor to exterior wall joint, to ASTM E2307 (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.3.(3) [PDF p.152] and Sentence 3.1.8.3.(4) [PDF p.152]).
- Citing the sentence numbers of the superseded ABC 2014 edition as if they were NBC(AE) 2023 numbers, as Edmonton B14-01 does (historical context; see Open questions).
- Treating an explanatory NBC 2020 intent statement as Alberta law. The supplements are explanatory only, so they never override the AE sentence.
- Firewall parapet heights or continuity that do not reach the Sentence 3.1.10.4.(1) heights (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.4.(1) [PDF p.162]) or the continuity required by Sentence 3.1.10.3.(1) (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.10.3.(1) [PDF p.162]).
- Combustible projections more than 1 m above grade within 1.2 m of a property line, without the exemptions in Sentence 9.10.15.5.(6) [PDF p.903] (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(5) [PDF p.903]).

## Worked example
This example is hypothetical. The dimensions are chosen to show each rule. It is not a design, a permit decision, or advice on a real lot.

Intake facts. A two-storey detached house with one dwelling unit and no suite, on a lot in Calgary. The west wall sits on the property line (the zero side). The east wall is set back 1.5 m from the east property line (the easement side). This is the paired layout described in Calgary SPV-001 (source p.1 of 5). The neighbour's house stands across a 1.5 m gap in the same layout. Each exposing face is 6.0 m long and rises 5.0 m from finished grade to the uppermost ceiling, so each exposing building face is 30 m². The applicant proposes a 0.6 m² window on the zero side and a 0.9 m² window on the easement side.

Step 1. Subsection. The house has one dwelling unit and no unit above it, so Subsection 9.10.15 applies (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.1.(1) [PDF p.900]).

Step 2. Limiting distances. The zero-side wall stands on the property line, so its limiting distance is 0 m. The easement-side wall is 1.5 m from its property line. The limiting distance is measured to a line the definition names (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div A, Article 1.4.1.2. [PDF p.47]).

Step 3. Zero side, code routes. Table 9.10.15.4. gives 0% glazed area below a limiting distance of 1.2 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Table 9.10.15.4. [PDF p.902]). The limiting-distance-squared route in Sentence 9.10.15.4.(1) starts at 1.2 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(1) [PDF p.901]). The Subsection 3.2.3 route gives 0% at 0 m in the unsprinklered table, Table 3.2.3.1.-B (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Table 3.2.3.1.-B [PDF p.207]). Result: the 0.6 m² zero-side window fails the code routes. The sprinklered limiting-distance tables in Subsection 3.2.3 (PDF pp.209 to 211) were not transcribed, so this example assumes an unsprinklered house.

Step 4. Easement side, code routes. At 1.5 m, Table 9.10.15.4. allows 9% of a 30 m² face, which is 2.7 m² (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Table 9.10.15.4. [PDF p.902]). The limiting-distance-squared route allows 1.5² = 2.25 m² (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(1) [PDF p.901]). Where the limiting distance is 2 m or less, an individual glazed opening or a group of glazed openings may not exceed 50% of the allowed aggregate, unless the building is sprinklered or the opening is an openable window with an unobstructed opening of 0.35 m² (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(3) [PDF p.901]). That limit is 1.35 m² here, so the 0.9 m² window is within it. The construction rules in Sentence 9.10.15.5.(2) and (3) cover only limiting distances under 1.2 m (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(3) [PDF p.902]). The window passes the code routes.

Step 5. Calgary SPV-001. The package sets the rules for this layout (Calgary SPV-001, source pp.1 to 5 of 5). On the zero side, no glazed or unprotected mechanical opening is permitted below 1.2 m (source p.3 of 5), so the 0.6 m² window is refused. On the easement side at 1.5 m, glazed area is capped at 7% of the face, which is 2.1 m² of 30 m², and no single opening may exceed 3.5%, which is 1.05 m² (source p.3 of 5). The 0.9 m² window passes. The package's 7% is tighter than the table's 9% for this face, so the package sets the limit here. The package also requires a 1.5 m maintenance easement on both titles (source p.4 and p.5 of 5). It sets cladding, sheathing and electrical-box protection (source p.2 and p.3 of 5).

Step 6. Edmonton B14-01, if the lots were in Edmonton. The deemed limiting-distance line lies midway between the two houses (Edmonton B14-01, source p.1). With a 1.5 m gap, that line is 0.75 m from each wall. The policy applies the requirements of one side wall to the facing wall of the adjacent house (source p.1), so both facing walls use the line. Both windows then sit under the 1.2 m threshold. Under the policy, windows there must be 20-minute labelled window assemblies installed to NFPA 80, 20-minute labelled shutter assemblies, or sprinkler-protected windows (source p.2). The policy also requires a 45-minute rated side wall and closed soffits within 1.2 m of the deemed line (source p.1). Its note says closures make the openings "protected openings" and that controls on standard glazed openings are maintained (source p.2). The policy gives no area for protected openings, so the glazed-area limit for them is UNVERIFIED. The policy's soffit reference, "Sentence (10)", uses the superseded ABC 2014 numbering (historical context). In NBC(AE) 2023, Sentence 9.10.15.5.(10) is the public-way exemption (PDF p.904), and the protection rule for soffits within 1.2 m is Sentence 9.10.15.5.(11) (PDF p.904). Confirm the mapping with Edmonton before use.

Step 7. Section C for the Calgary route. The table lists the acceptable-solution provisions this layout must meet or vary, with their attributions. Each intent paraphrase is explanatory, and its NBC 2020 citation is in the Intent section. The objective and function codes come from the code database definitions (see Attributions).

| Article | Objective | Function | Intent (paraphrase; sources in the Intent section) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sentence 9.10.15.4.(1), glazed-area limit [PDF p.901]; attribution [F03-OP3.1] (Table 9.38.1.1. [PDF p.1130]) | OP3.1 | F03 | Limit fire spread from the subject building to an adjacent building during the time responders need |
| Sentence 9.10.15.5.(2), 45 min rating and cladding below 0.6 m [PDF p.902]; attribution [F02,F03-OP3.1] (Table 9.38.1.1. [PDF p.1130]) | OP3.1 | F02, F03 | Limit the probability that an exposing face has insufficient fire resistance |
| Sentences 9.10.15.5.(8) and (9), roof soffit distance from the property line [PDF p.904]; attribution [F03-OP3.1] (Table 9.38.1.1. [PDF p.1130]) | OP3.1 | F03 | Limit spread through roof soffits where buildings are very closely spaced or closely spaced |
| Sentence 3.2.3.5.(1) and (2), closures below 1.2 m, Part 3 route only [PDF p.212]; attribution [F03-OP3.1] (Table 3.10.1.1. [PDF p.336]) | OP3.1 | F03 | Limit spread through unprotected openings, and limit closure materials that do not control radiant spread |

Result by route:

| Route | Zero-side window, 0.6 m² | Easement-side window, 0.9 m² |
|---|---|---|
| Code tables | Not permitted: 0% below 1.2 m | Permitted: at most 2.7 m² by the table, or 2.25 m² by the limiting-distance-squared route; no opening over 1.35 m² |
| Calgary SPV-001 | Not permitted: no glazed openings below 1.2 m | Permitted: at most 2.1 m² in total (7%), and 1.05 m² per opening (3.5%) |
| Edmonton B14-01, deemed line 0.75 m | Permitted only as a 20-minute labelled window closure; area limit UNVERIFIED | The same closure requirement applies, because the easement-side wall also faces the deemed line; area limit UNVERIFIED |

What the example shows:

- The zero-side window fails the code tables and Calgary SPV-001. Only the Edmonton closure route admits it, and that route's area limit is unverified.
- The easement-side window passes both the code tables and SPV-001. At 1.5 m, the package's 7% limit is tighter than the table's 9%.
- The Code does not define a "group" of glazed openings for Sentence 9.10.15.4.(3). The example uses one window to avoid that question (see Open questions).
- The example does not decide equivalent safety, check the cladding, soffit or gypsum details against the package, or decide whether a professional must seal the work. Any change to the layout needs a site-specific variance (Calgary SPV-001, source p.1 of 5). That route needs evidence, and the professional-involvement topic covers when a professional is needed.

## Open questions / uncertainty
1. The dashes in the house glazing table (Table 9.10.15.4., PDF p.902) are not defined on the printed page, so this sheet does not say what a dash permits (UNVERIFIED). The worked example never reaches a limiting distance of 10 m or more, so it does not depend on the dash.
2. The sprinklered limiting-distance tables in Subsection 3.2.3 (PDF pp.209 to 211) and the Part 9 non-house tables in Subsection 9.10.14 (PDF pp.895 to 898) were not transcribed. Values for sprinklered buildings are UNVERIFIED. The worked example assumes an unsprinklered house.
3. The Code does not define a group of glazed openings for Sentence 9.10.15.4.(3) (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 9.10.15.4.(3) [PDF p.901]). Whether two windows on one face form a group changes the answer for a layout like the worked example. The example uses one window, and the point is unresolved.
4. Calgary SPV-001 conflicts with itself on the footing projection: 0.3 m into the easement (source p.4 of 5) and 0.6 m across the property line (source p.5 of 5). The index flagged this too. Calgary clarification is needed.
5. The NRC report IRC-RR-195 (May 19, 2005), cited in SPV-001 (source p.5 of 5), was not found by two web searches on 2026-10-18. The NRC publication listings were not checked directly. UNVERIFIED.
6. Edmonton B14-01 is written against the superseded ABC 2014 edition (historical context). Its current version, the mapping of its sentence references to NBC(AE) 2023, and how the glazed-area limit applies to closure-protected windows are UNVERIFIED. The 2023 counterpart of its sprinkler-glazing reference is STANDATA 23-BCV-004 (source p.1 of 3). Confirm with Edmonton.
7. Sentence 3.1.8.5.(2) applies NFPA 80 to closures in a required fire separation (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.5.(2) [PDF p.153]). Whether that reaches a window used as a closure in an exterior wall under Sentence 3.2.3.5.(1) is not stated. UNVERIFIED.
8. The NBC 2020 intent text, which is explanatory only, does not match the AE 2023 sentences in two places, described in the Intent section. Reviewers should treat the AE 2023 sentence text as the operative rule and should not cite the intent text as law.
9. The CWC guide states that no building code standard evaluates curtain-wall firestop systems (source p.33 of 33). Sentence 3.1.8.3.(4) names ASTM E2307 for floor-to-exterior-wall joints (NBC(AE) 2023 p2, Div B, Sentence 3.1.8.3.(4) [PDF p.152]), so for that joint the Code does name a test. The guide's comparison method is an alternative solution and still needs evidence.
10. The Letter of Construction Intention forms that Edmonton B14-01 and B23-01 require were not read, so who signs them is UNVERIFIED.
11. Some precedents are known only from the index or from partial reads. Edmonton B14-01G, Edmonton B19-03, and the Edmonton row-house alternative solution report are index summaries only. STANDATA 19-BCV-017 was read on page 1 only. Statements that depend on these are labelled.
12. The worked example's dimensions, the 1.5 m gap, and the two-window layout are hypothetical.
