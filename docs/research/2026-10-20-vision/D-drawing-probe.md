## 0. Answer (one paragraph)

Mostly yes, with a hard fallback requirement. In the sample of 11 real applicant plan sets (10 from Calgary SDAB hearing packages, 1 from a Canmore SDAB package; plus the City of Calgary's own 26-page sample set), 9 of 11 have a real text layer holding dimension strings with exact positions on both the site plan and the elevations, so "numbers are selected, never generated" works for about 80% of sets. It does not work for 2 of 11 sets (one set has 11 of its 15 sheets as raster images with zero text, including the site plan and elevations; one is a vector set whose floor-plan, elevation and site-plan annotation text was converted to outlines), and even inside the 9 "good" sets some needed labels are missing from the text layer (in SDAB 2026-0100 the horizontal side-setback labels visible on the site plan are not in the text layer at all, while four look-alike "1.45m" strings in the text are walkway widths). Everything the City itself publishes as "sample drawings" for garages, suites, basements and renovations is low-resolution raster with zero text (8 of 9 City PDFs), and Edmonton's public SDAB agendas contain no applicant drawing sets (only rasterised permit pages of about 775x1000 px and maps). Safest intake for 2 weeks: per-sheet triage, then text-layer candidate extraction with an AI that only labels candidate IDs, then a mandatory human click-to-confirm on the rendered sheet; for raster or outlined sheets show the render and fall back to manual entry or two-point scale calibration, never to model-read numbers. Near-perfect accuracy comes from the confirm step, not from extraction.

## 3. Cross-set measured facts

3.1 Totals over the 11 real applicant sets (151 sheets in the final plan sections): 102 vector, 36 mixed, 13 raster by operator counts. Reclassified by what the text layer can supply (dimension-like strings = metric with unit, imperial ft-in, decimal metres, plus bare-number runs only if 30 or more on a page): 101 sheets usable text (67%), 20 dense vector sheets with almost no dimension text (13%, probably outlined), 17 thin (11%, mostly cover, notes and detail sheets), 13 raster (9%). - Units: mm with no unit symbol (Calgary sample, 0164); metric site plan with imperial building sheets in the same set (Calgary sample, 0098, 0100, 0114, 0148); dual units (`4'-1 1/4" (1.25 m)`, `17' - 6 1/4"` with `(5.33m)`).

I verified the transform for /Rotate 270 by computing the pixel boxes of the four `1.45m` strings on 0100 p238 and cropping the render there; each crop was centred on the string.
- Sheet size: 24x36, 36x24, 36x48, 48x36, 42x30, 34x22, 12x18, 17x11, 11x17 all occur; a 144 dpi render of a 48x36 sheet is 6912 x 5184 px, so vision input must be tiled.

## 5. Recommended intake approach for a 2-week prototype that needs near-perfect accuracy

Principle to adopt (refined): the model never emits a number. Every value is either (a) a text-layer string chosen by candidate ID, (b) a value the user typed or clicked, or (c) a value measured from user-placed points on a calibrated scale. Each value carries its provenance and is shown to the user before use.

1. Triage every page on upload (cheap, deterministic; thresholds are in /tmp/drawing-probe/scripts/analyze_pdf.py and final_counts.py): visible text characters, dimension-string count by unit type, image coverage, path density, page /Rotate and size. Output per sheet: TEXT_OK, PARTIAL (text exists but known labels missing), OUTLINED (dense vectors, almost no text), RASTER.
2. TEXT_OK / PARTIAL sheets: extract text lines/runs with PDFium character boxes (keep strings whole; imperial strings contain spaces), pair stacked dual-unit lines by proximity, normalise units (mm default note, ft-in, dual units), apply the /Rotate transform, render the page in tiles with numbered candidate boxes. The AI sees the render plus the candidate list and returns only {candidate_id, role, confidence} for roles such as side setback (which side), front/rear setback, eave projection, window size, stated limiting distance, required-vs-provided. The user confirms by clicking the highlighted string on the sheet.
3. Always run a "visible but not in the text layer" check on PARTIAL sheets (render plus vision looks for numeric labels near walls and property lines with no candidate; show them as "not selectable, type or confirm"). 0100 shows why: without it the only `1.45m` candidates are walkway labels.
4. OUTLINED / RASTER sheets: show the render; no model-read numbers. Offer manual entry and a two-point calibration (click two ends of a known dimension or use a scale string such as `1:200` with the page size) to measure wall-to-line distance; label results "measured".
5. Cross-sheet consistency: compare the site-plan setback with the limiting distance stated in elevation spatial-separation notes and flag differences; surface "SEE ARCH."-type deferrals as a question to the user (soffit projection) instead of guessing.
6. Ask the user for: the original vector PDF exported from the drafting software (not a scan, photo, printed-to-PDF or City-stamped copy), full-size sheets, the survey-based site plan, all elevations and the floor plans; a statement of units if the plan uses mm with no symbol; the soffit projection when the drawings defer it.

Coverage estimate on this sample (n = 11 real sets; small and not random):
- Text-layer path usable on both key sheets: 9 of 11 sets (82%). Of these, 6 look clean (0098, 0114, 0120, 0142, 0148, 0152 = 55% of all) and 3 have known gaps that need the visual cross-check (0100, 0164, Canmore = 27%).
- Image-based fallback needed for the key sheets: 2 of 11 sets (18%): 0127 raster, 0141 outlined.
- At sheet level (151 sheets): about 67% text-usable, 13% probably outlined, 11% thin, 9% raster. Every set contains at least a few sheets that need the fallback path, so routing must be per sheet, not per upload.
- Needed facts verified end to end as selectable strings: 2 of 12 sets (Calgary sample, 0152). A limiting-distance or spatial-separation statement exists as text on the elevations of 6 sets, with the value adjacent and checked in 4 (0100, 0120, 0148, 0152) and labels with nearby values in 2 (0098, 0142). The remaining sets show dimension strings but I did not verify the individual setback values.
- The City-published examples for garages, suites, basements and renovations (8 of 9 PDFs) are raster; do not use them as test fixtures for the vector path.
- Edmonton: public agenda PDFs offer nothing to test; Edmonton applicants would have to upload their own files.

Two-week fit: the triage, extraction, merge and rotate steps, the tiled render with candidate overlay and the confirm UI are all buildable in two weeks on pypdf/PDFium. The AI labelling step is small because it only picks IDs. The fallback is manual entry plus calibration, which is cheap; do not budget time for general OCR or model-read numbers.

## 6. Reproduce

All code is in /tmp/drawing-probe/scripts (run with `.venv/bin/python -I`, paths as arguments): dl.sh and dl_cookie.sh (downloads into new empty directories, sha256 into manifest.tsv), analyze_pdf.py (per-page operators, images, text, dimension strings, keywords, JSON), aggregate.py, final_counts.py, key_sheets.py, dump_text.py (strings with boxes), page_fonts.py, render_page.py, contact_sheet.py, pdf_outline.py, pdf_links.py, scan_agenda.py. ## 7. Risks and unknowns (inference or not measured)

- Sample bias. All real sets come from appealed or contested development permits (10 Calgary, mostly rowhouses with suites, published for hearings from Oct 2026 to Apr 2027; 1 Canmore); they are municipality-assembled copies, not applicants' original uploads. Raster sheets in 0127 and the 0120 DA-file copy may be City-side artifacts (INFERENCE). About 10 design firms appear; no hand-drawn, homeowner, deck, garage-only or basement-only submissions were found among real applicant files, and the City's own examples for those are raster, so the share of scanned or photographed homeowner drawings is unknown.
- Edmonton and other municipalities: no Edmonton applicant drawing set was obtainable, and Edmonton's plot plans are made by Alberta Land Surveyors (per the City's requirements page), whose CAD output may behave like the Canmore survey plan (almost no text); not tested.
- Proxies are loose. "Dimension-bearing" counts include spot elevations and areas; "TEXT_OK" does not mean the specific side-setback strings are present (0100 proves it). Page classes use my thresholds.
- Vision reading was done by me in one trial per page; there is no error rate. The 0152 blind test is one page. Raster sheets have no ground truth.
- Soffit projection is often not a clean number on the site plan or elevations, so the check will depend on user input or detail sheets more than on extraction.
- IP and privacy: the Calgary sample says it may not be reproduced without permission; SDAB packages hold designers' copyrighted drawings and neighbours' correspondence. Do not copy these into the repo or ship them as fixtures; build permissioned or synthetic fixtures. Nothing from the packages was stored outside /tmp.
- Access fragility: Calgary SDAB links rely on an anonymous session-cookie redirect and packages are probably removed after appeals close; parallel downloads sometimes hit the redirect limit (two of six failed, then worked sequentially).
- Not tested: password-protected or encrypted PDFs (none seen), layered/OCG-driven rendering differences (the Calgary sample has 41 layers including "Property Lines" and "A1-DIM", which could help labelling), Calgary Development Map documents, scanned-and-OCR'd applicant PDFs (only two Canmore pages looked OCR-style).
