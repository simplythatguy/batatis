The reference labels are the average of GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1, not human gold, and the page says the workflows were written by TypeSafe's own team. VENDOR CLAIM.
- From the 10-18 reports (not re-read today): DecideBench 392/400 for Jev versus 389/400 for Luna Decisions (author-generated labels), and an arXiv preprint (2610.06625) finding Jev's calibration advantage over Luna is not universal. Treat as small-sample evidence.
- OpenRouter's "Jev-verified cascade" cookbook (https://openrouter.ai/docs/cookbook/evaluate-and-optimize/jev-verified-cascade.md): on a 50-question set (27 answerable, 23 not covered), Luna drafts, Jev 1.13 checks support, Astra rewrites only on failure. Reported: cascade 0 wrong shipped at $0.012; Astra on all 50 shipped 2 wrong at $0.175; Luna alone 0 wrong at $0.004; one earlier run had Luna alone 2 wrong. Noise is acknowledged (a 2-answer difference "attributable to model and judge noise"). VENDOR CLAIM (partner docs), n=50.

Bottom line for Batatis: nothing public measures Jev on building codes, drawings, or legal entailment of regulatory text. TypeSafe's own cookbooks report almost no accuracy figures (most say "not reported"), so the S1MB table in Section 2 is the best available comparison, and the parent's own small benchmark remains the deciding evidence.

### 1.5 Where Jev should not be used for Batatis

- Arithmetic, dimension sums, areas, travel distances, occupant loads, dates and edition resolution: the 1.13 limitations page says Jev is not a calculator and reads dates as text. Keep these in code.
- Generating the variance narrative: Jev does not generate text.
- Anything that needs the answer to be right with no fallback: the confidence is a distribution statistic (vendor says so), not a case-level correctness probability.
- Canadian data residency: the privacy policy says services are hosted in the United States and no Canadian option is documented.

### 1.6 Section 1 verdict

Jev is a cheap, fast, calibrated-looking selector with a vendor-stated "prototype" image path and US-only hosting; its S1MB English score is mid-table and below free open-weight models. It is useful as an inexpensive screen/verifier, but there is no public evidence it is the best one for Batatis jobs, and for Canadian municipal data it is a non-starter unless TypeSafe confirms a Canadian or private deployment.

## 2. Open-weight and other decision models on S1MB (self-hosting, Canadian residency)

### 2.1 What S1MB is, and what the numbers below are

- S1MB = System One Mosaic Benchmark for decision models that answer Choice / Noul / Score questions. The English snapshot has 137 benchmarks (59 Noul, 57 Choice, 21 Score), 106 subsets, 14,009 cases and 26,269 judgments, built from public NLP datasets, Open-Jev tasks and synthetic sets. Six "generalization" sets were generated and self-checked by GPT-6-Astra with no independent human validation. Scores are "baseline-adjusted": 0 = trivial baseline, 1 = ceiling; 0.5 means closing half of the gap, not 50% correct. The blog warns that its synthetic generalization sets are too simple for fine ranking between leaders, and that Borda and Task Avg can order models differently. Source: https://huggingface.co/blog/hotchpotch/system-one-mosaic-benchmark (accessed 2026-10-20; its snapshot is dated 2026-09-30, so its 23-model leaderboard is stale).

### 2.8 Section 2 recommendation (open decision models)

1. If Batatis wants a Jev-style decision model it can run in Canada, **start with vllm-sr/d3 (27B) for quality and vllm-sr/d3-flash (8B) or d3-mini (4.5B) for volume**: Apache-2.0, vision capable, vLLM-native `/v1/systemone`, best of the open models on the entailment and numeric/spatial groups, and the highest Noul Brier skill (0.650) among the open-weight models without a training-exposure declaration. 3. Do not buy on S1MB rank alone: Darwin v9 and kettei are in-domain; Superion S1 is unreachable. And no S1MB result covers drawings or codes.
4. Cost to self-host is a GPU, not tokens. Illustration only: a third-party aggregator quotes about $2.45 per H100-hour for Denvr (UNVERIFIED; Denvr's own docs list H100 only in Houston, and its Calgary region lists A100 80GB and GH200 96GB with no prices). d3's vendor throughput of 27 req/s on one AMD MI325X implies at most 97,200 requests per GPU-hour, so $2.45 per hour would be about $0.000025 per request if the vendor figure transferred to the chosen GPU and traffic were constant (ESTIMATE; an MI325X is not an A100 or H100, so real throughput will differ). Self-hosting is justified by residency and control, not by price.

### 3.6 Section 3 verdict

1. For reading drawings, public evidence says every model, including the best, is wrong on roughly half of realistic whole-sheet questions (LED-BENCH architecture 49%, ArchPlanVQA 33-38%, DrawingVQA structural 77% with a 95% professional baseline), and symbol counting is worse (doors/windows 0.16-0.39). Treat model drawing-reading as a proposal to be confirmed by geometry extracted from vector PDFs/DWG/IFC or by a person; use tiling plus search-then-reread (+13 points) and crops for text.
2. Gemini 3 Pro was the strongest model on the two benchmarks that tested it against Claude and GPT, and GPT-5.6 Sol was strongest on LED-BENCH, but all of this evidence is one to two generations old. Gemini 4 Argon at $2/$10 (to Nov 30) is the first model to bake-off with GPT-6.1-Sol and Opus 5.5 on a Batatis-specific sheet set; cheap Flash/Luna/Haiku-class models were within a few points of flagships on LED-BENCH architecture, so the bake-off must include them.
3. For code reasoning, closed-book accuracy is roughly 80-90% on multiple choice and below 70% on open questions; answers read well even when wrong. Retrieval plus quote-level verification (Sections 1.3, 5) is required, and no frontier model should be trusted on its own say-so.
4. None of the hosted frontier routes gives Canadian processing; plan for self-hosted open weights where residency is mandatory.

## 4. Embeddings and rerankers for building-code retrieval (brief update)

## 5. Techniques that push accuracy toward "never confidently wrong"

### 5.1 What can honestly be promised

No technique below makes errors impossible. The strongest available statement is of this form: *among the items the system chooses to answer, the error rate is at most epsilon, with confidence 1-delta, provided new items resemble the calibration items* (exchangeability). The price is abstention: items the system declines go to a person. Guarantees are marginal (on average over cases, not for each case or each province), finite-sample, and break under distribution shift (new code edition, new jurisdiction, new drawing style, a changed model). So the design target is "every answered item is covered by a calibrated statistical bound, every unanswered item reaches a person, and a monitored audit sample detects drift", not "the model is never wrong".

### 5.3 Calibration-set size (ESTIMATE: my arithmetic)

- **Demonstrating a low error rate after seeing no errors** (exact binomial, 95% confidence; n >= ln(0.05) / ln(1-epsilon)): epsilon = 5% needs 59 labeled accepted items; 2% needs 149; 1% needs 299; 0.5% needs 598; 0.1% needs 2,995. If errors appear the requirement grows, and the claim holds only for the stratum the items came from.
- **Split conformal at coverage 1-alpha:** minimum n = 1/alpha - 1 (alpha 0.10: 9; 0.05: 19; 0.01: 99); worst-case over-coverage slack 1/(n+1) (about 1.0% at n=100, 0.33% at n=300). The slack is small; what hurts is variance across small calibration draws, so use 200-300 or more per stratum, as TRAQ did (300).
- **Strata multiply the data need.** A guarantee per jurisdiction, edition, discipline (Part 3 fire, Part 9 housing...) or drawing type requires its own calibration set or a pooled set that is demonstrably exchangeable with each. Ten jurisdictions at 300 each is 3,000 labeled cases. Re-calibrate when the code edition, the retrieval model, the prompt, or the LLM version changes.
- **Worked example:** to claim "at most 1% of auto-accepted article-applicability calls are wrong, at 95% confidence" for one jurisdiction, collect at least 299 accepted decisions labeled by an SCO with zero errors; if the system abstains on 40% of items, that means about 500 labeled items per jurisdiction, and one observed error roughly doubles the requirement.

### 5.4 How these combine for Batatis

1. Deterministic gates first (cost about zero): exact quote or article-ID match to the stored, versioned source; edition and jurisdiction match; all arithmetic, unit conversion, dates and geometry in code (Jev's own failure-mode page and FloorplanQA both say LLMs fail here).
2. Cheap calibrated screen: a decision model (d3, Perplexity Decider, Jev) or small checker answers narrow yes/no questions; thresholds from risk-coverage or conformal calibration on Batatis-labeled data, per stratum, with a "no match" outcome. Remember S1MB: relevance judgments are the weakest area (0.29-0.50), so use a low threshold for recall and let later stages filter.
3. Independent verifier on borderline or high-consequence items, from a different vendor or open-weights lineage and given source text (not the draft's reasoning): errors are correlated (60% agreement when both wrong), so independence is partial.
4. Human on all abstentions and on every item where a deterministic gate failed, plus a random audit sample of auto-accepted items to estimate the live error rate (prediction-powered inference) and detect drift.
5. For drawings, base model accuracy on whole sheets is about 35-55% (Section 3.3), so a calibrated "answer only when 99% sure" policy would answer very little; get facts from vector geometry/OCR with deterministic parsing and use models to classify, locate and propose, with person confirmation.

5. **How the image is shown matters more than which flagship reads it.** Overlapping crops gave +13 points and search-then-reread +13, cheap GPT-5.6 Luna was within 2 points of Sol on architecture, and 20 tiles of a 36 x 24 in sheet cost about $0.006 (Luna) to $0.25 (Opus 5.5) in image tokens. Geometry should come from vector/DWG/IFC when available; distances, areas and paths must be computed in code (FloorplanQA: tool use lifted a distance task from 56% to 99%). (VERIFIED + ESTIMATE.)
6. **Closed-book code answers read well and are often wrong.** On the Hong Kong fire code (500 expert questions, no code text supplied) 80-90% of multiple-choice items were right for the best models, but expert-rated open-ended accuracy was only 57-66%, clarity far exceeded accuracy, and thinking mode did not help clearly. Retrieval, quote-level verification and abstention are mandatory; no Oct 2026 model has been tested on the NBC or Alberta regulations. (VERIFIED.)
7. **No hosted frontier route processes data in Canada today.** Anthropic's API offers only `us`/`global`; Claude on Bedrock `ca-central-1`/`ca-west-1` is cross-region only (In-Region "not supported"); Claude on Vertex has no Canadian regional endpoint for current models; OpenAI's Canada option is storage only; Gemini-on-Vertex and Azure Canada status for the newest models is unverified. Canada-resident work means self-hosted open weights, where vision quality is far lower (Qwen3.8-27B 20% on LED-BENCH architecture). Plan two operating modes. (VERIFIED + UNVERIFIED as marked.)
8. **"Never confidently wrong" is a calibrated bound plus abstention, not a model property.** Certifying 1% error at 95% confidence with zero observed errors needs about 299 labeled accepted items per stratum (59 for 5%, 2,995 for 0.1%); conformal methods need at least 1/alpha - 1 calibration items, in practice 200-300 per jurisdiction or edition, recalibrated on any shift; models agree 60% of the time when both are wrong; self-correction without outside evidence fails. Raw model confidence (Jev included) is overconfident out of domain. (VERIFIED papers; ESTIMATE arithmetic.)
9. **Cascades are cheap and the economics are lopsided.** A decision-model entailment check costs about $0.00005 per claim versus $0.007-0.014 for a flagship check; partner and vendor examples show 0 wrong at 7% of the flagship-only cost (n=50), and the literature reports 50-98% cost reductions at matched accuracy. All are small or non-Batatis evidence, so each stage needs its own labeled threshold. (VERIFIED sources, VENDOR CLAIM numbers.)
10. **Price facts to lock into the plan.** Opus 5.5 $4/$20, Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50, GPT-6.1-Sol $2/$10, GPT-6-Luna $0.10/$0.50, Fable 5.1 and GPT-6-Astra $10/$50; Batch -50% and cache reads 0.05-0.1x of input everywhere; Gemini 4 Argon doubles to $4/$20 on 2026-12-01 and Gemini 3.8 Flash doubles on 2027-01-01 (budget the post-increase prices). Variable model cost per 20-sheet case is about $1-4; reviewer minutes dominate cost. (VERIFIED.)
