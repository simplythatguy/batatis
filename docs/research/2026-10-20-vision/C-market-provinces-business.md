## Section 1. Competitors and their 2025-2026 status

### 1.1 Reading of the landscape (what changed since the 2026-10-18 scan)

1. **No competitor found prepares alternative-solution / variance applications.** Every AI product surveyed checks prescriptive rules (zoning, document completeness, Part 9 / IBC provisions). I found no product, funding announcement or municipal pilot that writes alternative-solution justifications or performance-solution reports. A targeted search for such a tool returned only an academic fire-engineering demo (IFETool) and prescriptive plan-check startups (AutoReview.AI, CodeComply.Ai). Label: UNVERIFIED negative (absence of evidence from web search, not proof). Source: WebSearch 2026-10-20, query "AI tool alternative solution OR performance solution building code application ... fire engineering"; vendor pages read are cited per row below.
2. **A Canadian municipal AI pre-check wave happened in 2026** (table 1.3). At least six Canadian municipalities launched or announced AI pre-check tools between March and September 2026 (Surrey, Burlington, Ottawa, Kingston, Toronto, Markham), plus Cambridge (page undated) and Vancouver (2023 partnership announcement). Archistar and Clariti (CivCheck) split them. All are zoning / completeness / Part 9 low-rise checks, voluntary, and none replaces staff review. This confirms municipal appetite for pre-submission checking and also means well-funded vendors already hold relationships with Ontario and BC building departments.
3. **Alberta is behind Ontario and BC on AI pre-check.** Edmonton runs AI auto-review for house development permits (zoning), residential permit auto-reviews (garages, decks, solar) and AI inspection triage, not building-code or alternative-solution review. Calgary: I found no AI plan-review pilot; its service plan update lists industry interest in AI as a trend only. Edmonton page: https://www.edmonton.ca/business_economy/development-permitting-improvements (fetched 2026-10-20, VERIFIED). Calgary: https://www-prd.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/ca/city-manager/documents/2023-2026-service-plans/updates/building-safety.pdf (listed in search results 2026-10-20 as noting AI interest and missing-information delays; the PDF returned 404 on direct fetch, so UNVERIFIED beyond the search snippet).
4. **Capital is real but still small.** Largest private round in AI permitting/plan review is PermitFlow's US$54M Series B (Dec 2025). No vendor discloses ARR. Nothing in this subsector has shown a $100M ARR trajectory yet (ESTIMATE: absence of disclosure, not proof).
5. **Government contract anchor:** Denver approved a five-year, US$4.6M contract for CivCheck (vendor entity ComplyAI) in March 2026; the initial contract is reported at US$1.05M. Denver gets about 1,000 permit applications a month (Propmodo). See 1.4.
6. **Incumbents are bolting AI on, via acquisition:** Accela bought ePermitHub (Apr 21, 2025), Clariti bought CivCheck (Oct 8, 2025), Cloudpermit bought CityReporter (Feb 27, 2026), CivicPlus partnered with CodeComply.Ai (Mar 19, 2026).

#### Important correction to the variance-volume reading (Calgary standing variances)

The City of Calgary's own advisory explains the single most-varied article. Advisory "New Standing Posted Variance procedure", Sept 6, 2017 (repo copy: `reference/text/calgary-new-standing-posted-variance-procedure.jsonl`, VERIFIED): Zero Lot Line (ZLL) houses "do not conform to Sentence 9.10.15.5.(8) ... so an alternative solution is required on each building permit issued"; "26 separate home builders" were building ZLL product; "From Jan. 1 to Sept. 1, 2017, 382 ZLL alternative solutions requests have been reviewed and accepted. The corresponding amount of variances have been written, issued, processed and forwarded to the Safety Codes Council." Calgary then introduced **Standing Posted Variances (SPV)**: the builder cites the SPV on the permit and the permit carries a condition; SPVs "cannot be altered or deviated from" (SPV-003, revised Feb 12, 2025; City of Calgary document, repo copy `calgary-spv-003-construction-of-exposed-building-face.jsonl`, VERIFIED).

Implication (ESTIMATE/inference, not stated by the Council): a large share of the roughly 6,200 building article entries a year is templated, permit-attached variance volume (ZLL soffits, party walls, exposing building face) that already has an official template in Calgary. That volume is a **paperwork-automation and reviewer-triage** market (production homebuilders and plan examiners), not a bespoke-engineering-preparation market. Truly novel alternative solutions (engineered assemblies, single-exit, new products) are probably a small minority of the 6,200; the Council does not collect supporting materials, so the share is unknown. This materially shrinks the Alberta-only "preparation" revenue pool (see 2.5) and pushes the business toward (a) standing-variance workflow for builders and SCOs, (b) pre-check and completeness for all permits, and (c) other provinces where each alternative solution is bespoke and fee-bearing.

### 2.5 Bottom-up market sizing (ESTIMATES, all assumptions shown)

1. **Alberta bespoke alternative-solution preparation pool.** Annual building article entries about 6,200 (3,088 x 2). Assume 5% to 15% are bespoke (not a standing variance): 310 to 930 bespoke variances a year. Assume C$3,000 to C$15,000 of consultant, designer and internal time per bespoke package (assumption; no published rate). Pool = 310 x C$3,000 = C$0.9M up to 930 x C$15,000 = C$14.0M a year. An AI tool that captures 30% to 50% of that pool in fee-equivalent value is about C$0.3M to C$7M a year. **Alberta preparation alone is a single-digit-million-dollar market under these assumptions.**
2. **Alberta standing-variance paperwork pool.** If 5,000 to 5,900 of the 6,200 entries are templated (the complement of the bespoke assumption), and each costs 0.5 to 2 hours of builder or examiner time at C$75 to C$120 an hour (assumption), the labour pool is 5,000 x 0.5 x C$75 = C$0.19M up to 5,900 x 2 x C$120 = C$1.4M a year. Small; value is in cycle time and error reduction rather than labour.
3. **Canadian AHJ-side AI pre-check / plan-review software pool at Denver pricing.** Denver: up to US$4.6M over 5 years = US$0.92M a year for about 12,000 applications a year = about US$77 per application per year (ESTIMATE from sourced contract and volume figures). Apply to 300,000 to 650,000 Canadian applications: US$23M to US$50M a year (about C$31M to C$68M at 1.37 C$/US$, rate assumed). This is the realistic Canadian municipal software pool if every municipality bought at Denver's rate; actual capture would be a fraction.
4. **Seat-based pool, Canada.** Public-sector building officials and plan examiners: assume 25% of the 25,700 inspectors, about 6,400, at an assumed C$3,000 a seat (a discount to CodeComply.Ai's US$3,000, about C$4,100) = C$19M. Design professionals: assume 10% of 330,639 engineers, about 33,000, handle code-driven design at an assumed C$700 a year (below UpCodes Professional's annual US$708, about C$970) = C$23M. Total about C$42M a year.
5. **US seat-based pool.** BLS 153,900 inspectors x US$3,000 = US$462M if every inspector had one seat at CodeComply.Ai's price (upper bound); at 36% local government (about 55,400) = US$166M for public reviewers. Even before any competitor share this is a US$0.2B to US$0.5B inspector-seat pool, not a multi-billion pool. Multi-billion outcomes need (a) applicant-side seats and per-project fees, (b) transaction/take-rate models, and (c) global expansion.

**Conclusion on market size.** The Alberta alternative-solution wedge is a small revenue pool (single-digit C$ millions). Canada-wide AHJ pre-check is tens of C$ millions. The "billion-dollar" case is only plausible as a multi-country permitting-compliance platform. The wedge's value is as a credible entry into the highest-value, least-automated step (performance-based compliance) with regulators who are already open to digital tools.

### 3.2 Licensing of code text for commercial software (summary and risk)

1. **"Free to read" is not "free to embed".** The NRC publishes the National Model Codes and the Alberta Edition as free electronic PDFs, but its Terms and Conditions (last modified 2025-06-24) say users "may not reproduce materials ... for the purposes of commercial redistribution" without "prior written permission from the copyright administrator", and non-commercial reproduction is allowed only without changes and with due diligence on accuracy. VERIFIED: https://nrc.canada.ca/en/corporate/transparency/terms-conditions (fetched 2026-10-20). The NBC(AE) 2023 2nd printing itself says "Commercial reproduction by any means of the NRC's copyright material is prohibited without the written consent of the NRC." VERIFIED (p. xii of the project's local copy, quoted in Alberta row).
2. **NRC copyright notice on the national 2025 code:** "(c) His Majesty the King in Right of Canada, as represented by the National Research Council of Canada, 2025", First Printing, PDF ISBN 978-0-660-76990-5. VERIFIED (PDF page 3, https://nrc-publications.canada.ca/eng/view/ft/?id=adf1ad94-7ea8-4b08-a19f-653ebb7f45f6, first 4 MB read). The NRC record has no licence statement and says licensing questions go to the Codes Canada team (codes@nrc-cnrc.gc.ca). VERIFIED (https://nrc-publications.canada.ca/eng/view/object/?id=adf1ad94-7ea8-4b08-a19f-653ebb7f45f6). Codes Canada's contact page lists "permission to reproduce or licence Codes materials" under general enquiries but describes **no software-developer licence programme and no API or machine-readable feed.** VERIFIED (https://nrc.canada.ca/en/certifications-evaluations-standards/codes-canada/help-using-accessing-codes/contact-codes-canada).
3. **Precedent that licences exist:** BC runs a formal process that ends in a licensing agreement for digital products using the BC Building Code (row above). Ontario's Compendium says NRC material is reproduced "under a licence agreement" and NRC says "Several provincial versions ... are also published through licensing agreements" (search summary). So NRC does license; the terms for a software vendor are not public.
4. **Scale of the exposure for Batatis:** Section C of the variance template needs article text, objectives, functional statements and intent statements. The intent statements come from NRC supplements ("Supplement to the NBC 2020: Intent Statements", an NRC publication), whose terms are the NRC's. A product that stores and displays this text for paying customers is "commercial reproduction". Link-out to the NRC archive, user-supplied text, short quotations, and licensed use are different risk postures. **Fair dealing is not a commercial-software safe harbour** (Copyright Act fair dealing covers research, private study, education, parody, satire, criticism, review and news reporting; I did not research case law, so treat as a question for counsel: UNVERIFIED).
5. **Recommended action (ESTIMATE of effort):** write to Codes@nrc-cnrc.gc.ca now for a written licence covering storage, display, indexing, model use and export for NBC(AE) 2023/NFC(AE) 2023 and the 2020/2025 Intent Statements; in parallel submit BC's Copyright Permission Request Form. ### 3.4 What this means for national coverage speed (ESTIMATES, assumptions shown)

- **Common core.** Every province except Nunavut and NL builds on NBC 2020 (Nunavut NBC 2015; NL NBC 2025). The structure "Division A compliance route (acceptable solution or alternative solution) and Division C section 2.3 documentation" is the same wherever it is adopted, so the engine and checklist are portable. The differences that matter for a pack are: provincial amendments (BC and Quebec heaviest, Ontario trimmed), who may prepare the submission (Manitoba registered professional; Vancouver architect/BC engineer; Toronto qualified designer; Alberta any owner/agent), the form (Alberta single provincial template; Quebec RBQ form; Manitoba reference form; others municipal), the receiving authority (Alberta SCO of accredited municipality/agency; elsewhere the chief building official), and the licensing path.
- **Fastest next markets by structure (judgement):** (1) Alberta (done, one template, 433 authorities). (2) Saskatchewan and Manitoba (NBC 2020 plus light amendments, municipal AHJs, Manitoba has a provincial reference form). (3) British Columbia (clear licensing path, one provincial code but many local-government AHJs with their own forms, and Vancouver separate; strong consultant and mass-timber alternative-solution culture per the CWC/WoodWorks BC guide). (4) Ontario (largest permit volume, municipal forms, MMAH licence, active AI pre-check competition). (5) Atlantic provinces. (6) Quebec (French, RBQ process, strictest copyright). (7) Territories (small volumes).
- **Time to support a province pack (ESTIMATE):** one reviewed pack = provincial amendments + form mapping + licensing + domain-expert review. For BC, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan: roughly 3 to 6 months each with one jurisdiction expert, assuming a licence is obtained (assumption: licences are the critical path, not engineering). Quebec adds French-language review: 6 to 9 months (ESTIMATE). Plan for **simultaneous NBC 2025 editions** in 2027: every pack will need re-release.
- **Big-city exceptions to model:** Vancouver (own Building By-law, AL-1, generic alternative solutions); Toronto (own alternative-solution guide, evaluation form and C$2,368.32 fee); Calgary and Edmonton (accredited municipalities with local forms and standing variances, and Edmonton's separate AI auto-review for zoning); Montreal and four municipalities with RBQ delegation (Chateauguay, Dorval, Dollard-des-Ormeaux, Westmount) plus fire-service involvement; Winnipeg follows the Manitoba code (permit applications on or after Jan 1, 2024; City of Winnipeg wording seen only in a search summary: UNVERIFIED).

### 4.3 Expansion implications (ESTIMATES, judgement)

- A recurring pattern: regulators are digitising themselves (Singapore CORENET X, UK Gateway data, NZ Simpli, Alberta's planned official template and its variance data hub). A startup must be the best private-sector companion, or it will be replaced by a free official tool.

### 5.4 Three revenue models and arithmetic to US$100M ARR (all ESTIMATES; assumptions in brackets)

Currency note: C$1 = US$0.73 (assumed rate, 1.37 C$/US$).

**Model 1. AHJ platform (reviewer completeness view, applicant pre-check, variance registry), sold to authorities having jurisdiction.**
- Pricing anchor: Denver about US$77 per application per year at 12,000 applications (VERIFIED inputs, ESTIMATE ratio). Assume volume discounts for small AHJs.
- Mix [assumptions]: 25 large AHJs at US$900,000 (Denver-like) = US$22.5M; 150 mid-size AHJs at US$250,000 = US$37.5M; 600 small AHJs at US$60,000 = US$36.0M; Canadian AHJs 100 at US$80,000 average = US$8.0M. Total = **US$104M ARR** from 875 authorities.
- Scale check: 775 US authorities is about 3.9% of about 19,900 US permit-issuing places (Census), but weighted to large ones; Canada's 100 is about 4% of roughly 2,400 permit-issuing municipalities (StatCan). Today Archistar says 30+ municipalities in production and Clariti has signed "almost 10" in a year (Apr 2024), so this needs about 29x Archistar's current footprint (875 / 30).
- Canada-only ceiling at the Denver rate: 300,000 to 650,000 applications x US$77 = US$23M to US$50M a year if every application were covered; at a realistic 30% capture, US$7M to US$15M.
- Biggest risks: official tools (the planned Alberta template and guide, Singapore-style government platforms), incumbents bundling AI (Accela, Clariti, CivicPlus), municipal budget cuts (Denver debate over spending on staff), staff adoption (Honolulu survey "almost universally negative"), and the Canadian field already split between Archistar and Clariti.

**Model 2. Applicant-side subscription for builders, design firms and consultants, plus per-package fees.**
- Pricing [assumptions]: C$2,400 per seat per year (about C$200 a month; between UpCodes Professional at US$708 and CodeComply.Ai at US$3,000 per seat); enterprise site licences C$40,000 for production builders; C$150 per completed variance or alternative-solution package.
- Mix [assumptions]: 600 builder/developer enterprise accounts x C$40,000 = C$24.0M; 12,000 design/consulting seats x C$2,400 = C$28.8M; 60,000 packages x C$150 = C$9.0M. Canada subtotal = C$61.8M (about US$45.1M). Add international (Australia/NZ/UK/US) of US$55M [e.g., 16,000 seats x US$3,400] = **about US$100M**.
- Scale check: 12,000 Canadian seats is 3.6% of the 330,639 engineers (Engineers Canada), and 600 enterprise builders is 1.5% of the 40,969 employer residential-building establishments (ISED); both depend on the tool being used daily for general code compliance, not only for rare variances. The Alberta-only variance pool is only C$0.3M-C$7M (Section 2.5), so this model is a bet on becoming the everyday compliance workspace.
- Risk: UpCodes, CodeComply.Ai and Bluebeam Max have broader distribution; the applicant pays only if time saved is visible; Canadian municipalities give some pre-checks free.

**Model 3. Transactions and a review marketplace (per-submission fee plus take rate on third-party peer review and engineering letters).**
- Pricing [assumptions]: platform fee C$1,200 per alternative-solution submission (about half of Toronto's C$2,368.32 municipal fee); 40% of submissions buy third-party peer review at an average C$8,000 with a 15% take rate, i.e., C$1,200 per reviewed submission, or 0.4 x C$1,200 = C$480 per submission on average.
- Canada volume [assumption]: bespoke alternative solutions are 1,500 to 7,500 a year nationally (Alberta 310-930 x 5-8 for the rest of Canada). Revenue = 1,500 x (C$1,200 + C$480) = C$2.5M up to 7,500 x C$1,680 = C$12.6M.
- To reach US$100M with this model the unit has to be the ordinary permit pre-check, not the alternative solution: for example 2.5M submissions a year at US$40 = US$100M, or about 17% of an assumed 15M US permit applications [assumption: no national count exists]. That is a different, much more competitive business (PermitFlow, Greenlite, Archistar).
- Risk: volumes are small; alternates get codified (BC single-exit stairs Rev. 3; CCMC reports as acceptable solutions; Calgary SPVs), shrinking demand per category; Canadian applicants expect free pre-check.

**Cross-model comparison (ESTIMATE).**

| Model | Needs | Share of known pool | Years (judgement) | Dominant uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. AHJ platform | 875 authorities | 3.9% of US permit places, 4% of Canadian municipalities | 8-10 | Free official tools; Archistar/Clariti entrenched |
| 2. Applicant SaaS | 12,000 CA seats + 600 builders + international | 3.6% of Canadian engineers | 7-9 | Is it a daily workspace or a variance-only tool? |
| 3. Marketplace/transactions | 2.5M submissions a year | 17% of an assumed US permit base | 9-12 | Volume of true alternative solutions is small |

An ARR ramp consistent with any of these is roughly C$1M in year 2, then tripling twice and doubling thereafter (Y3 C$3M, Y4 C$9M, Y5 C$18M, Y6 C$36M, Y7 C$72M, Y8 C$144M, about US$105M). That is a top-decile SaaS curve; no company in the sources has disclosed such ARR. Treat "US$100M ARR" as an outer-bound ambition, not a plan.

## (a) One-page business case (ESTIMATES unless marked; sources in Sections 1-5)

**Thesis.** Build the preparation and completeness-review workspace for the part of the building-code system no one automates: alternative and performance solutions, and the standing-variance paperwork around them. Use Alberta's centralised structure (one regulator-run template, 433 authorities, 2,317 SCOs, public variance data) as the beachhead, and make the jurisdiction pack, not the model, the product.

**Evidence of pain and demand.**
- Alberta registered about 3,100 article-level variance entries in H1 2026, nearly half of them (1,501) one Part 9 article (roof soffits within 0.45 m of the limiting distance, i.e., zero-lot-line homes); the Council does not collect supporting material, so first-pass quality is unmeasured. The 2026 Canadian municipal AI pre-check wave (Surrey, Burlington, Ottawa, Kingston, Toronto, Markham, Cambridge) proves buyer appetite, but every deployment is zoning/Part 9 completeness and none touches alternative solutions.
- England's regulator published the clearest proof that application quality drives delay: new-build Gateway 2 approval rose from 39% to 92% and median time fell from 43 to 22 weeks in a year after a dedicated unit.

**Product (wedge to platform).** (1) Applicant: guided Sections A-D with deterministic Section C lookup, engineering-flag and evidence-ladder guidance, Division C s.2.3 documentation checklist. (2) Reviewer: completeness view and registry-ready packet for the SCO. (3) Standing-variance workflow (cite the posted variance, attach conditions) for production builders. (4) Jurisdiction packs with change tracking (NBC 2025 arrives 2027). Completeness, not compliance. For the business-plan criterion, present Alberta volume as "templated volume plus a bespoke tail" and as a beachhead, not as the total market.

**Who pays.** Authorities (per-application or tiered annual licence; Denver anchor about US$77 per application per year); production builders and consultants (about C$2,400 per seat, C$40,000 enterprise, C$150 per package); the Safety Codes Council is a channel and pilot sponsor, not a payer; keep the SCO tier free in Alberta.

**Size.** Alberta bespoke-preparation pool about C$0.9M-C$14M a year before capture (C$0.3M-C$7M captured). Canada AHJ pre-check pool about US$23M-US$50M if all 300,000-650,000 applications were covered. Canada seat pool about C$42M. US inspector-seat pool US$0.2B-US$0.5B. A listed regulator-software vendor serving Australia, New Zealand and the UK (Objective Corp) reports A$18.7M of planning and building ARR (UNVERIFIED primary). **The wedge alone is a single-digit-million business; a US$100M ARR outcome needs the US/Australia/UK and an all-permit pre-check platform, about 8 to 10 years on the curves in 5.4.**

**Competition.** Archistar (30+ municipalities, 8 Canadian), Clariti/CivCheck (Toronto, Denver US$4.6M), Trax (claims 150 Ontario municipalities, code Q&A), UpCodes/CodeComply.Ai (US codes), incumbents Cloudpermit (850 municipalities, Canadian leader claim), Accela, Tyler. None found prepares alternative solutions (unverified negative). The most dangerous competitor may be official: the SSRIA template and guide supported by Build Forward Alberta (announced Oct 7, 2026; no date or price given).

**Moat.** Licensed and reviewed jurisdiction packs; a case and outcome database (needs Council/municipal data access); integrations (POSSE, Cloudpermit, ASCA eSITE); regulator trust and workflow position; a code-change impact feed. Not the model.

**First 18 months.** Request NRC (Codes@nrc-cnrc.gc.ca) and BC written licences now. Offer Build Forward Alberta an integration/pilot. Sign 3-5 Alberta authorities under C$75,000 a year each (Calgary's open-tender threshold) and 2-3 production builders. Apply to ISC (Phase 1 up to C$150,000). Prepare a PIA pack, ISO 27001 plan and the Alberta "automated system" notice text. Alberta Innovates has paused select grant programs since May 29, 2026 (its Digital Traction program shows Closed).

**What must be true / kill criteria.** (1) NRC grants workable written terms within about 6 months, else switch to link-out/user-supplied text architecture. (2) Council or municipal data show at least 300 bespoke variances a year in Alberta, else pivot to standing-variance plus all-permit completeness. (3) One municipal paid contract above C$50,000 within 12 months. (4) If the Council template ships first, integrate it rather than compete.

## (b) The 10 most decision-relevant findings

1. **No competitor prepares alternative-solution applications.** Archistar, Clariti/CivCheck, CodeComply.Ai, UpCodes, Symbium, PermitFlow and Greenlite check prescriptive rules, zoning or completeness; their pages do not mention alternative solutions (UNVERIFIED negative; Section 1).
2. **Canada's municipal AI pre-check wave is already here and split between Archistar and Clariti**: Surrey (Mar 10, 2026), Burlington (May), Ottawa (Jun 1), Kingston (Aug 31), Toronto (Aug 27), Markham (Sep 18), Cambridge, Burnaby, plus Vancouver (2023). Alberta is open: Edmonton automates zoning only; no Calgary pilot found (Section 1.3).
3. **Alberta variance volume is real but templated.** 433 authorities, 2,317 SCOs, about 6,200 building article entries a year; one article (9.10.15.5.(8), zero-lot-line soffits) is about 49% of building entries; Calgary already issues Standing Posted Variances (382 ZLL variances in eight months of 2017). Bespoke volume is unknown and probably a small share (Section 2.0).
4. **The money is small unless you scale across countries.** Alberta preparation pool C$0.9M-C$14M; Canada AHJ pre-check US$23M-US$50M; Objective Corp's planning and building ARR is A$18.7M; no AI plan-review company discloses ARR; the largest rounds are US$54M (PermitFlow) and US$49.5M (GreenLite). A US$100M ARR path needs about 875 authorities or 12,000 Canadian seats plus international (Section 5.4).
5. **Code-text licensing is on the critical path.** NBC(AE) 2023 states commercial reproduction of NRC material needs NRC's written consent; NRC has no public developer licence or API; BC has a formal digital-product licence process; Quebec requires prior authorization for any reproduction; Ontario's compendium reproduces NRC material under licence and commercial reuse of non-legal ontario.ca material needs a King's Printer licence (Section 3.2).
6. **Regulators are building free tools and data.** SSRIA, with Build Forward Alberta, is developing a template and guide, and the Council runs a variance data hub; Singapore built CORENET X; the UK publishes Gateway data; NZ's 30 BCAs share Simpli guidance (Sections 4-5).
7. **Codes are about to churn.** NBC 2025 was released Dec 22, 2025; NL adopted it Jul 12, 2026 (per the CBHCC tracker; not confirmed on a provincial page); the reconciliation agreement implies about Jun 2027 for others; builders asked provinces to pause (Mar 3, 2026); Alberta's timeline is unknown. Every pack needs re-release and a change-impact feature (Section 3.3).
8. **Alternative-solution demand is being codified away for products.** BC single-exit stairs (Rev. 3), BC's Dec 2025 proposal to accept CCMC evaluation reports as acceptable solutions, Ontario Bill 17's CCMC link, ICC-ES entering Canada (May 2025), and First Ministers' end-2026 harmonization commitment all reduce repeat project-level alternative solutions; durable value is workflow and variance intelligence (Sections 3-4).
9. **Price anchors:** Denver about US$77 per application per year (US$4.6M over five years for about 12,000 applications); seats US$468-US$3,000 a year; Ontario alternative-solution fees C$430-C$2,368; Canadian pre-checks for applicants are free in Markham and Burlington, so the authority or the vendor must pay (Section 5.1).
10. **US has a funded buyer; Canada does not.** HUD's automated-permitting grants (up to US$1.5M per city) and a reported US$200M a year Innovation Fund fund exactly this software; no Canadian federal fund for municipal digital permitting was found. Results are mixed (Honolulu review cycles 3.4 to 1.4 but staff feedback negative; Seattle not ready to adopt; Denver first-pass acceptance 37%), so adoption friction is real (Sections 2.3, 5.2).

## (c) Open questions and risks

**Code-text licensing (highest priority).**
- Will NRC license storage, display, indexing, model use and export of NBC(AE)/NFC(AE) and the Intent Statements, on what terms and fee? The NBC(AE) front matter is explicit that commercial reproduction of NRC material needs written consent; no developer programme is published. A refusal would force a link-out or user-supplied-text architecture and weaken the deterministic Section C lookup.
- The Minister of Alberta Municipal Affairs holds copyright in portions of the NBC(AE); the second rights holder's position was not tested.
- BC and Quebec rights holders require separate permission; Ontario's compendium reuse needs the ministry plus NRC. Third-party referenced standards (CSA, ASTM, ULC) are separate copyrights and were not researched.
- Fair dealing for a commercial product is untested here; needs counsel.

**Market and demand.**
- True alternative-solution volume, first-pass rejection rate and time-to-decision in Alberta are unknown; the Council collects neither supporting material nor outcomes. One data request to the Council (distinct variances per year, share standing/templated, returns before issue) would settle the wedge's size. The Data Hub counts articles, not applications, so the 6,200 a year may overstate the number of variance documents.
- Consultant fee levels and expediter pricing in Canada are unpublished; the Alberta value pool rests on assumed C$3,000-C$15,000 per package.
- Applicants may expect free pre-check (Ontario precedent), capping applicant pricing.

**Competition and substitution.**
- Archistar and Clariti could add alternative-solution features; Accela, CivicPlus and Cloudpermit are bolting AI on; general AI assistants can draft variance text; an official template could commoditise the form.
- Regulators building their own tools (Singapore, UK) show the end-state risk.

**Regulatory, liability and trust.**
- Product must stay on the "completeness not compliance" line and flag when engineering is required (APEGA/provincial practice limits not researched).
- Alberta POPA obligations on municipalities: privacy management, PIAs, breach notice, and notification when personal information is used in an automated system. No Alberta statutory data-residency rule was found; municipalities may still ask for Canadian hosting; Quebec Law 25 and NS/BC residency rules are unverified.
- Honolulu's staff survey and Seattle's reluctance show adoption risk among reviewers; SCO buy-in is not assured.

**Code churn and coverage.**
- NBC 2025 adoption timing is unsettled (Alberta unknown; builders ask for a pause); Quebec transition runs to Oct 17, 2027; BC revisions continue (Rev. 7 on Sep 21, 2026).
- Process and licence detail for NB, NL, PEI, YT, NT, NU, SK and NS was thin; Vancouver pages were blocked today.

**Evidence quality notes.** Items labelled UNVERIFIED in the text were seen only in search summaries or aggregators (for example NL's Jul 12, 2026 adoption, MBIE dashboard figures, Objective Corp figures, Nemetschek figures, Engineers Canada and ISED counts, Buildcheck/LightTable rounds, IBC 104.2.3 renumbering, the French Jul 1, 2026 engineering-study duty). The shared search budget ran out before a last round of anchor checks.
