## 1. Key findings

1. **Only Newfoundland and Labrador is on the 2025 national codes, and that rests on the CBHCC tracker.** The tracker lists NBC 2025 and NFC 2025 for NL with enforcement "July 12, 2026"; every other jurisdiction's NBC edition is 2020, except Nunavut (2015). The tracker has no "last updated" date and says "please contact the appropriate provincial or territorial authority". NL's own regulation (NLR 45/12) still names the 2015 editions but says "further editions and amendments to those codes, are adopted 6 months following the date of their publication." I found no gov.nl.ca page stating the July 12, 2026 date. VERIFIED (tracker, regulation text). The NL date on a provincial page is NOT FOUND.
2. **No new 2025 adoption announcement dated after 2026-10-20 exists in anything I could read.** Pending dates: Saskatchewan "proposed for January 1, 2027" (provincial flyer, VERIFIED). Yukon "June 22, 2027" (search snippets quoting yukon.ca, page blocked, UNVERIFIED). Manitoba adopts new editions by statute 18 months after first publication, so NBC 2025 (published 2025-12-22) lands on about 2027-06-22 (ESTIMATE from VERIFIED statute text). Alberta's NPC 2025 on 2027-01-01 comes from a trade article (UNVERIFIED) and I found no Alberta NBC 2025 date. BC and NWT say "within 18 months" in ministry statements seen only as snippets (UNVERIFIED). New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and PEI: NOT FOUND.
3. **BC adopted the CCMC proposal.** Ministerial Order BA 2026 01 (Revision 7, effective September 21, 2026) adds Sentence 1.2.1.1.(4): a CCMC Technical Guide cited in a CCMC Evaluation Report "shall be considered to be an acceptable solution." Sentence 1.2.1.1.(5) does the same for acceptance criteria from programs of bodies accredited under ISO/IEC 17065. VERIFIED (order text).
4. **Division C numbering differs between the national code and BC.** National NBC 2020 and 2025 have only Article 2.3.1.1 "Documentation". BCBC 2024 has Article 2.3.1.1 "Application" (the AHJ "shall accept" a measure on written request by the owner or agent) and Article 2.3.1.2 "Documentation". Manitoba's bulletin cites "2.3.1.1" for documentation, which matches the national numbering. VERIFIED (all three texts read).
5. **Who may prepare an alternative solution differs by place, and BC's code is the loosest.** BCAB 1946 (December 23, 2024): "the Code does not require the involvement of a registered professional in the development of an alternative solution", but the code analysis must give the preparer's qualifications and "The local authority may decline". Vancouver requires a "Professional architect, OR Engineer registered in the Province of BC". Burnaby requires a BC-licensed engineer or architect. Surrey requires the sealed Registered Professional. Manitoba (Form 08, revised October 2025) requires "an engineer or architect licensed to practice in the province of Manitoba". Regina and Saskatoon require an architect or engineer licensed in Saskatchewan. VERIFIED.
6. **Licensing is the critical path and no machine-readable code feed exists.** NRC terms (Date modified 2025-06-24): "you may not reproduce materials on this site, in whole or in part, for the purposes of commercial redistribution without prior written permission from the copyright administrator." NRC's Codes Canada page (modified 2026-10-27) sends "Code licensing" questions to the Codes Canada team and describes no API, XML or software licence programme. The only structured NRC output I found is an Atom feed of Publications Archive catalogue records. BC runs a formal route: "To use the BC Building Code, you must ask for permission", then "a licensing agreement". VERIFIED.
7. **Intent statements are free for 2020 and absent for 2025.** NRC published "Supplement to the NBC 2020: Intent Statements" (3,210 pages, PDF, 2025-08-18) plus NFC, NPC and NECB supplements, English and French. A search of the Archive for "Intent Statements" finds no 2025 supplement. BC does not publish its own and says its BC-specific requirements have no intent statements. Manitoba's Form 08 requires "Intent Statements of the applicable Acceptable Solutions". VERIFIED.
8. **No cross-jurisdiction inventory of provincial variations exists in public.** The 2019 Construction Codes Reconciliation Agreement page commits parties to "identify variations" within six months and to "report progress to RCT annually", not to the public. Per-province sources exist: Saskatchewan's "Construction Codes Amendments, 2020" PDF, Manitoba's regulation Schedule, BC's consolidated code plus Ministerial Orders, and Nova Scotia's regulation. A crosswalk must be built province by province. VERIFIED (negative on RCT, CBHCC and NRC pages).
9. **Manitoba and Saskatchewan have statutory machinery Alberta lacks.** Manitoba's code is M.R. 78/2023 under The Buildings Act, in force January 1, 2024. The Act's s.3(2.1) deems new editions adopted after 18 months, and s.14.5 sends technical permit disputes to The Permit Dispute Resolution Act (ITS BC Guide 09, March 1, 2024). In Saskatchewan the Construction Standards Appeal Board can issue interpretation orders (s.32) and exemption orders (s.33) within 90 days of a complete application, before the permit is issued. Saskatoon's handout nonetheless says a refusal of an alternative solution "is not subject to review or an appeal pursuant to the CCA". VERIFIED.
10. **Winnipeg is fully sourced, including fees.** The City enforces M.R. 78/2023 through "Winnipeg Building By-Law 4555/87". Alternative-solution fees come from the City's open-data fee dataset (Canada Open Government Licence): $131 to $1,300 per request depending on stage (pre-application, application, post-permit) and class (minor or major, new or existing building). VERIFIED. Vancouver is only partly sourced: process, form (AL-1), preparer rule and 4 to 6 week first review from an archived City page; fee NOT FOUND; by-law number 14343 and September 15, 2025 effective date only from search snippets (UNVERIFIED).
11. **Climatic data is a multi-source problem.** NBC 1.1.3.1 says values "established by the authority having jurisdiction" override Appendix C. All 16 priority cities appear in Table C-2 of both NBC 2020 (over 600 locations) and NBC 2025 (over 680 locations, climate-adjusted to a 2.5 degree C warming level, so values and columns differ). Saskatchewan's regulation sets "climate zone 7A" for all buildings in the province for heating degree-day categories. Burnaby publishes its own two-site values (guide revised 2025-01-15). The PCIC Design Value Explorer offers CSV tables for Table C-2 locations and maps for any location. VERIFIED.
12. **Date conflicts resolved.** New Brunswick's Regulation 2025-19 (O.C. 2025-62, filed April 30, 2025) changes NBC "2015" to "2020" and "comes into force on May 1, 2025", so May 1 is the legal date. Nova Scotia's N.S. Reg. 198/2024 adopts NBC 2020 "including all revisions, errata and corrections to the errata ... on or before April 1, 2023", so later NBC 2020 errata do not apply there. VERIFIED.
