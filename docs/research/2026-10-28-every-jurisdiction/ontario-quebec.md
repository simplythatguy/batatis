## 1. Key findings

1. VERIFIED. Ontario's building code is a one-page regulation, O. Reg. 163/24 (consolidation period "October 2, 2026", last amendment O. Reg. 325/26). Section 1 adopts the NBC 2020 First Printing (NRCC-CONST-56435E) as amended by the document entitled "Ontario Amendments to the National Building Code of Canada 2020", dated October 1, 2026 and issued by the Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing. In force 2025-01-01. The 2012 code (O. Reg. 332/12) continues only for permits issued by 2024-12-31, or for drawings substantially complete by then with a permit application on or before 2025-03-31, and only if construction starts within six months.
2. VERIFIED. The date of the Ontario Amendments document has been rewritten eight times since the regulation was made in April 2024 and six times since it took effect (latest O. Reg. 325/26, filed 2026-10-02). The free HTML Compendium on ontario.ca says it reflects amendments "up to and including O. Reg. 242/26", one behind. The free PDF at Publications Ontario is "updated to January 16, 2025" and marked "FOR NON-COMMERCIAL USE". A pipeline that copies the Compendium once will be out of date within months.
3. VERIFIED. The Ontario building alternative-solution rule is Division C Article 2.1.1.1 (Section 2.1), not 2.3.1.1 as in NBC and NBC(AE). The person proposing it gives documentation to "the chief building official or registered code agency" that identifies objectives, functional statements and acceptable solutions and shows the performance level by past performance, tests or other evaluation, plus assumptions, limiting factors and commissioning and maintenance needs. Ontario sets no provincial form, fee or review clock for this route. The Compendium's Building Code Forms page lists only the permit application, Schedules 1 and 2, and the orders, so municipal forms and fees fill that gap.
4. VERIFIED. Ontario has a second, separate route in the Fire Code (O. Reg. 213/07, consolidation 2026-01-01). An alternative solution goes to the Chief Fire Official, "shall bear the signature and seal of a Professional Engineer or Architect, or both", gets a decision within 45 days, and a refusal can be appealed within 30 days. The Fire Code is Ontario's own full code, not a one-page adoption of the National Fire Code.
5. VERIFIED. Ontario keeps objectives, functional statements and attribution tables, but the attributions sit in MMAH Supplementary Standard SA-1, and Ontario adds its own objectives (for example OH6 Privacy, OH7 View to the Outdoors, OR, OC) and its own F-codes. The same F-code means different things in Ontario and NBC(AE) (F101, F130). NOT FOUND: Ontario-specific intent statements. Appendix A calls "application and intent statements" advisory but does not publish them.
6. VERIFIED. Fees and forms, 2026. Toronto: worksheet form 14-0074, "Only a qualified designer may apply", C$2,368.32 effective 2026-01-01. Ottawa: Tier I C$1,080 plus third-party costs, Tier II C$430 (schedule effective 2026-01-01). Mississauga: Form 2966, C$1,225.00 (2026-02-01 to 2027-01-31). Markham: C$3,675 or C$840 first submission, C$315 supplementary (applications after 2025-12-31). Ottawa's web pages block scripts, so its form, tier definitions and portal are UNVERIFIED.
7. VERIFIED. Quebec Construction Code Chapter I incorporates NBC 2020 First Printing, in force 2025-04-17 (decree 437-2025 of 2025-03-19, amended by 238-2026). The previous chapter "as it read before 17 April 2025" may be used when work begins before 2027-10-17, per the RBQ notice of 2026-08-26 (regulations effective 2026-09-10). The 2027-10-17 date is now confirmed. LégisQuébec's consolidated text (current to 2026-07-01) still shows 2026-10-17.
8. VERIFIED. In Quebec the alternative route for buildings under Chapter I is the Régie du bâtiment du Québec (RBQ). Division C 2.3.1.1 of the Quebec edition says alternative solutions "shall be approved by the Régie du bâtiment du Québec ... pursuant to section 127 of the Building Act". Section 127 covers "mesures équivalentes", section 128 covers "mesures différentes" (code cannot reasonably be applied). RBQ forms 1536 and 1536EE and the guide are French only, the service standard is 45 days, and no fee was found. Quebec-domiciled firms must fill RBQ forms in French.
9. VERIFIED. Chapter I exempts many buildings (s.1.04), for example a dwelling building with at most 2 storeys or at most 8 dwellings, business premises up to 2 storeys, mercantile premises up to 300 m2, agricultural and industrial buildings. Municipalities regulate those. Their by-laws pin older editions: Montréal 11-018 (codified 2025-09-02) defines the Code as NBC 2010 (modified Québec), Gatineau 534-2025 pins NBC 2015 (modified Québec), and Québec City's March 2026 guidance still cites NBC 2010.
10. VERIFIED. Reuse is restrictive in both provinces. Ontario regulations may be copied "without seeking permission and without charge" with credit, but other ontario.ca material needs a King's Printer licence for commercial use, and the Compendium's status is not stated. Quebec says "Il est interdit de reproduire, télécharger, stocker, traduire, adapter, publier ou représenter en public les contenus du gouvernement du Québec sans autorisation préalable" (reproducing, downloading, storing, translating, adapting or publishing is forbidden without prior authorization). NRC states that reproducing Quebec's Chapter I "has been authorized by Les Publications du Québec".
11. VERIFIED. Location data. Ontario uses its own SB-1 (climatic Table 2, Part 9 seismic Table 3), not NBC Appendix C. All ten Ontario cities in scope are named in both tables. Quebec uses NBC 2020 Appendix C in the Quebec edition. Montréal (City Hall), Laval, Dorval, Québec, Lévis, Gatineau and Sherbrooke appear by name, but PDF text extraction scrambles the table rows, so values must come from a table-aware extractor. Longueuil was not found by name.
12. VERIFIED (blockers). Ottawa pages (Incapsula), Vaughan's form (Access Denied), Laval's by-law (Cloudflare) and some Windsor and Ottawa document links failed for scripts. Several search results were stale or mirrored: Ottawa's old by-law PDF shows Tier I C$750 and Tier II C$300, and Markham's own page keeps its 2025 table (C$3,500 and C$800) below the 2026 table (C$3,675 and C$840). Search engines also return copies of Markham's fee page on unrelated museum domains. Treat any page without a visible effective date as suspect.

## 5. Hallucination traps

1. Division C section for alternative solutions. Ontario OBC 2024: Section 2.1, Article 2.1.1.1 "Documentation" (Compendium Div C Part 2; Toronto Municipal Code § 363-3.11 cites "section 2.1 of Division C"). NBC(AE) 2023: Section 2.3, Article 2.3.1.1 (local NBC(AE) copy, PDF page 739). Quebec edition of NBC 2020: Section 2.3, Article 2.3.1.1 "Conditions for Approval", which sends the proposal to the RBQ under Building Act s.127. In Ontario, Section 2.3 is the BMEC fee section. Never reuse "2.3.1.1" across provinces.
2. Documentation content differs. NBC(AE) 2.3.1.1 requires a Code analysis, qualifications of the designer and a design-intent level of detail. Ontario 2.1.1.1 asks for identification of objectives, functional statements and acceptable solutions plus evidence and assumptions, with no qualification clause. Montréal 11-018 arts. 48-52 copy the NBC(AE)-style items. Do not apply one template to all.
3. Where attribution lives. Ontario Division A 1.2.1.1(1)(b) points to Supplementary Standard SA-1, not to brackets in Division B. Quebec keeps bracketed attributions in Division B.
4. F-codes collide. Ontario F101 is "To limit unwanted visual exposure." and F130 is "To limit excessive water consumption." In NBC(AE) F101 is "To minimize the risk of contamination of food." and F130 is "To limit unwanted visual surveillance." Ontario has F101-F102, F110-F113, F120-F124, F130-F133, F140, F150 and F151, and has no F90-F100 block. Keep separate lookup tables per jurisdiction.
5. Objectives differ. Ontario lists OS, OH (OH1-OH7), OA, OP (OP1-OP5, "Fire, Structural, Water and Sewage Protection of Buildings"), OR, OE (OE1 Air Quality, OE2 Water and Soil Quality) and OC. The NBC(AE) list shows OS, OH (OH1-OH5), OA, OP (OP1-OP4) and OE (OE1 Resources). Source: Compendium Div A Part 2 and local NBC(AE) pages 68-73.
6. The Ontario regulation is not the code. e-Laws holds a one-page adoption, and ontario.ca says "The full-length version of the Building Code will no longer be available on E-laws." The adopted document's date changed eight times since April 2024.
7. Compendium lag. HTML covers O. Reg. 242/26; the regulation is at 325/26; the PDF stops at 2025-01-16.
8. Old Ontario code. O. Reg. 332/12 (2012 Building Code) applies only under the s.2 transition test. Do not cite it as current.
9. Ontario energy. Use SB-10 and SB-12 (2016 editions) and Part 12, not NECB 2020.
10. Two Ontario authorities. Building alternative solutions go to the CBO. Fire Code alternative solutions go to the Chief Fire Official and need a sealed PEng or architect (Fire Code Div C 1.3.2.2(5)), with a 45-day clock. The Building Code Commission fee (C$212) is for disputes with the CBO, not for an alternative solution application.
11. Product routes. After 2025-07-01 BMEC "shall not exercise its powers" where CCMC has examined or intends to examine the product (Act s.28(6)). Minister's rulings under s.29(1)(c) need consistency with a Commission decision, another province's approval, or an approved national code revision (Div C 2.4.2.1). Div C 2.4.1 "This Sub-section is reserved."
12. By-laws and technical rules. Building Code Act s.35(1): the Act and the code "supersede all municipal by-laws respecting the construction or demolition of buildings". Toronto Chapter 363 and Ottawa 2014-220 are procedural and fee instruments. Do not treat a by-law as a technical source.
13. Stale fee pages. Ottawa's PDF by-law shows C$750 and C$300; the 2026 schedule shows C$1,080 and C$430. Markham's page keeps older tables: C$3,000 and C$800 for applications after 2023-12-31, C$3,500 and C$800 after 2024-12-31, and C$3,675 and C$840 after 2025-12-31. Mississauga's fee period ends 2027-01-31. Read the effective date.
14. Toronto fee location. Chapter 363 § 363-4.5 sends fees to Chapter 441. The 2015-07 worksheet text still says fees follow Chapter 363.
15. Quebec transition date. 2027-10-17, not 2026-10-17, for Chapter I (RBQ notice 2026-08-26). Consolidated LégisQuébec text and the Safety Code transition clause still print 2026-10-17. The test is "travaux ... débuté avant" (work started before), not permit date. The year-of-construction table in Safety Code s.344 (NBC 2015 for buildings built 2022-01-08 to 2025-04-16) is a separate rule for existing buildings.
16. French and English terms. "Mesure équivalente" is Building Act s.127. "Mesure différente" is s.128 and means the code cannot reasonably be applied (mostly existing buildings or constraints). "Solution de rechange" is the NBC term for an alternative solution. In Quebec the NBC alternative solution is approved by the RBQ under s.127. Montréal's page says "mesures différentes ou solutions de rechange" for exempt buildings. The RBQ guide says an equivalence request is "not a product approval".
17. Section 127 wording. French: "La Régie approuve, aux conditions qu'elle détermine, ... lorsqu'elle estime que leur qualité est équivalente" (the Board approves, on conditions, when it finds quality equivalent). The English text says "may approve". A second paragraph adds: "Il en est de même lorsqu'elle estime que la sécurité du public est également assurée" (the same applies when it finds public safety equally protected). The RBQ web page joins both with "et", which reads as cumulative. Quote the statute.
18. Exemption wording. Chapter I s.1.04(3)(f) exempts "un immeuble utilisé comme logement répondant à l'une des caractéristiques suivantes: au plus 2 étages ... ; au plus 8 logements" (a dwelling building meeting either test). Exemption applies to buildings housing only one listed major occupancy and excludes private seniors' residences. Small houses still face Section 9.36 energy rules.
19. RBQ versus city. Non-exempt buildings go to the RBQ only. Exempt buildings go to the municipality under its by-law. Montréal splits three cases (its housing by-law 03-096 and its own by-law go to the borough; a non-exempt building goes to the RBQ); both may be needed. The four delegated municipalities named on the request page (Châteauguay, Dorval, Dollard-des-Ormeaux, Westmount) differ from the three on the supervision page (Châteauguay, Dorval, Westmount).
20. Municipal editions lag the province. Montréal 11-018: NBC 2010 (modified). Gatineau 534-2025: NBC 2015 (modified). Québec City guidance of March 2026: NBC 2010 (modified), and fire by-law R.V.Q. 2241 art. 6: NFC 2010 (modified), while the Safety Code now uses NFC 2020. For exempt buildings the municipal edition governs (inference from the three by-laws read, not a statement in the Building Act).
21. Language. RBQ forms must be filled in French by Quebec-domiciled firms: "Only the companies domiciled outside of Québec and not coming under the conditions of any interprovincial agreement with the provinces of Ontario, New-Brunswick or Newfoundland are authorized to fill out the forms in English."
22. Table extraction. Plain PDF text of NBC 2020 Appendix C Table C-2 in the Quebec edition merges city names and drops row order ("Dorval Laval Montréal (City Hall) 20 -23 -26 ..."). Do not map values to cities from this text.
23. RBQ mailbox. The web page lists rbq.mesures.equivalentes.differentes@rbq.gouv.qc.ca; the 2016-era guide lists mesures.equivalentes.differentes@rbq.gouv.qc.ca. Use the web page's address and confirm.
