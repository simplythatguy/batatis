## 1. Key findings

1. **The universe is 387 rows, not "430+".** The 2025 Municipal Affairs population list (MAPL, the newest on open.alberta.ca, Open Government Licence - Alberta) has 330 municipal-type rows, 8 Métis settlements, 48 First Nations and 1 stakeholder row (Redwood Meadows). I found no source for "430+" [NOT FOUND]. The Safety Codes Council (SCC) counts something else, accredited organizations: 299 municipal ones, 40 joint municipalities, 1 regional services commission and 45 agencies in my 2026-10-29 searches, plus 92 corporations from the earlier A16 inventory [VERIFIED for the first four, UNVERIFIED for 92].
2. **Building authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) by row.** 255 of the 330 municipal-type rows and 3 of the 8 Métis settlements hold their own Building accreditation. 68 municipal-type rows and 5 Métis settlements hold none, so the Alberta Safety Codes Authority (ASCA) oversees them. Six rows are national-park rows where Parks Canada issues the building permit, and Lloydminster works from its own Charter. None of the 48 First Nations is accredited under its own name. Sending an unaccredited row to ASCA combines a verified search result with a verified rule, so it is an INFERENCE at row level [VERIFIED rule: AR 29/2016 s.4(1)].
3. **ASCA areas have no per-municipality assignment.** ASCA's page says it "contracts a number of accredited agencies ... so you can select a service provider that works for you", and fees are the same whichever agency the owner picks [VERIFIED]. Five agencies are named. The "Where to get a Permit" tool is an organization search by type, discipline, city and name. It cannot turn a project address into an agency, and its "Site List" report links send you to a login page [VERIFIED]. Batatis needs one ASCA pack for 73 rows, not 73 packs.
4. **Variance law.** Safety Codes Act s.38(1): "An Administrator or a safety codes officer may issue a written variance". An officer may use only the powers in the officer's designation (ss.31 to 32), and the 2019 Municipal Affairs notice speaks of an officer "designated with a variance power". A refused applicant can appeal an officer's refusal to the Council within 30 days ($500). An Administrator's decision is final [VERIFIED]. Batatis's stored note in `provinces/AB.json` ("a technical administrator or the Safety Codes Council") is out of date. The live SCC page now says "a safety codes officer, or an Administrator".
5. **No route makes one variance valid in every jurisdiction.** What exists: STANDATA variances that say "applicable throughout the Province of Alberta" (16 current for NBC(AE) 2023, 1 for NFC(AE) 2023, 2 for NPC 2020); evaluation reports from the Canadian Construction Materials Centre (CCMC), which NBC(AE) Div A 1.2.2.1.(2) says "may be used in determining compliance", with other bodies needing the Provincial Building Administrator's approval; and one variance for many sites inside one AHJ's area (2019 notice). From 2026-12-01 each variance must still be registered "for a specific location, including those relying on jurisdictional alternative solutions" (IB-2026-10-01) [VERIFIED]. The SCC form has a box for "submitted to the Safety Codes Council for review for jurisdictional use", but I found no public page that explains that route [NOT FOUND]. A manufacturer who picks many jurisdictions needs one dossier and one application per AHJ, with a STANDATA check first.
6. **There is no public variance search.** The Orders and Variances Portal (OVP) is for officers only: "Only SCOs will have access to register variances and orders in OVP". The Act (s.63(4)) and AR 16/2004 s.7(3) let a person ask for a search, issued "if all the applicable fees have been paid" [VERIFIED]. Public precedent data is limited to STANDATA (Open Government Licence - Alberta), SCC's redacted appeal decisions (84 PDF links) and standing variances that some municipalities post (St. Albert's zero lot line variance; Calgary's SPVs).
7. **Codes in force, and why regulation text misleads.** NBC(AE) 2023 2nd printing (NRC file dated 2026-08-17) under Building Code Regulation AR 5/2024, in force 2024-05-01; NFC(AE) 2023 2nd printing; NECB 2020 at Energy Tier 1 only (AR 5/2024 s.3 bars local AHJs from asking for more); NPC 2020 since 2023-04-01; CEC 2024 since 2025-04-01; CSA B149.1:25 since 2026-03-01; Private Sewage Standard of Practice 2021 since 2022-11-01 [VERIFIED from alberta.ca pages and notices]. The King's Printer consolidations still name older editions ("National Fire Code - 2019 Alberta Edition", "National Plumbing Code of Canada 2015", "CSA C22.1-18") "as amended or replaced from time to time". Act s.65(4) makes a replacement edition law on the first day of the month after 12 months from publication unless the Minister orders otherwise. NPC 2025 is scheduled for 2027-01-01. NRC has published the 2025 national NBC (first printing, file header dated 2025-12-22), and I found no Alberta notice for it [NOT FOUND].
8. **Federal and Indigenous land.** The National Parks Building Regulations are repealed (SOR/2024-230). Parks Canada issues building permits in the Jasper townsite, Waterton and Banff National Park outside the Town of Banff, under a mandatory permit condition to meet "any building, fire, plumbing, electrical or energy efficiency code that applies in the province ... or that is published by the National Research Council of Canada or by the Canadian Standards Association". Airport lands: the Calgary Airport Authority names the City of Calgary as AHJ at YYC and Rocky View County at Springbank, and Edmonton Airports' 2026 contractor form names Leduc County as "the primary Authority Having Jurisdiction" at Edmonton International for Alberta Safety Code items. First Nations: Indian Act s.81(1)(h) lets a band council make building by-laws. Tsuut'ina Nation's law adopts the National Building Code and has Superior Safety Codes issue electrical, plumbing and gas permits [VERIFIED]. Department of National Defence bases were not researched.
9. **Location inputs.** Table C-2 lists 147 locations; 22 of them are hamlets or dissolved municipalities (Fort McMurray, Sherwood Park, Lake Louise and others), and only 125 of the 330 municipal rows have an exact-name match, so 205 need a nearest-location choice or an ECCC request. Climate zones that occur in Table C-2 are 6 (27 locations), 7A (101), 7B (16) and 8 (3) [VERIFIED by calculation]. The NRCan seismic tool is a web form; a scripted request returned HTTP 403 and I found no API [NOT FOUND]. The 10-minute fire department response rule is NBC(AE) Div B 3.2.3.1.(8), and I found no municipality that publishes whether it meets it [NOT FOUND]. Div B 1.1.3.1 says climatic and seismic values follow "the values established by the authority having jurisdiction", with Appendix C used only where the AHJ has set none; I found no Alberta AHJ that publishes its own values [NOT FOUND].
10. **Boundaries are solved for Alberta, with the same code as the population list.** The Government of Alberta's public map services return the municipal code (`LG_CODE` equivalent) for cities (19), towns (105), villages (78), summer villages (51), counties and municipal districts (63), improvement districts (7), specialized municipalities and the Special Areas, plus 728 reserve polygons and the Métis settlements. Counts match the MAPL [VERIFIED]. Statistics Canada's 2021 census subdivisions are the national fallback (Open Government Licence - Canada). The Open Database of Addresses (Alberta file, collected January to April 2021) lets Batatis geocode on the user's own machine, and each address point carries a census subdivision ID [VERIFIED].
11. **Local process differs a lot and the links rot.** Among the 20 largest municipalities after Calgary and Edmonton, 5 publish a variance or alternative-solution form or page, 1 states its rule only in a Quality Management Plan, 5 show only a fee line, 1 posts a standing variance, and 8 showed nothing in a search limited to their own site. One "variance" form I read is a zoning form (Wood Buffalo), and two fee schedules list a separate "variance" fee that looks like a zoning fee (INFERENCE). Four links found by search returned HTTP 404 when fetched [VERIFIED].
12. **How to keep the app from guessing.** Keep a closed registry keyed by the municipal code. Give every field a source URL, a retrieval date and a file hash, as the existing jurisdiction packs do. When a field is missing, show "ask the authority" and never fill it from the model. Refresh the SCC search monthly, the STANDATA lists weekly and the population list yearly (section 4).

### 2.2 The 75 municipal-type rows that are not accredited for Building (P2)

Rule, with the proof for each part:

- ASCA is a division of the Council and serves "approximately 300,000 Albertans who live in unaccredited areas" [VERIFIED]. These 75 rows hold 180,458 people in the MAPL, so ASCA's figure also counts people served for disciplines a municipality does not hold (INFERENCE).
- AR 29/2016 s.4(1): "In the areas of Alberta that are not under the administration of an accredited municipality, an accredited regional services commission or an accredited corporation, the Authority shall oversee the provision of permit-related services" for buildings, electrical, gas, plumbing, private sewage and storage tank systems. Fire is not on that list [VERIFIED].
- Variance requests: the 2019 private-sewage notice says that in an unaccredited area "Contact should be made with Alberta Safety Codes Authority for instructions regarding the application process" [VERIFIED, but it is a private-sewage notice]. ASCA has "its own safety codes officers that provide consultative advice/guidance, plans reviews, issue permits, and conduct inspections" [VERIFIED]. No page states who signs a building variance in an ASCA area, so I treat it as ASCA through its own officer or an agency officer acting for it (IB-2026-10-16) [INFERENCE].
- I ran one read-only query of the SCC Accreditation Search for each of the 75 names (organization type and discipline left blank, one request per 1.2 seconds, raw files kept). All 75 results match the A16 snapshot of 2026-10-25. None holds Building under its own name. None is covered by a joint Building accreditation by name. 39 hold Fire only and 36 hold nothing. The full per-row results are in `alberta-unaccredited-routing.json`.
