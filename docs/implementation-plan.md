# Batatis: Implementation Plan

*Building Codes Challenge, Submission 1 (Oct 29, 2026). Sources are cited inline and were retrieved Oct 20, 2026. "Built" means it is in the current build; anything else is marked planned, in progress or a target. Results are reported only where they exist, and are labelled.*

## 1. Summary
Batatis fits into Alberta's current process and doesn't replace any of it. The applicant still submits the SCC variance form (Calgary calls the request a "Request for Specific Variance"), the SCO still decides, and from Dec 1, 2026 the SCO still registers the variance in the Council's portal. What is designed to change:
- the application arrives complete and code-referenced;
- the officer can run a completeness review on any package;
- the officer gets a registration-ready bundle.

The officer screen and the "Not sure" answers are in review (PR #16, one planted sample package for the officer screen; not yet on `dev`); the registration bundle is not built (§6).

**The engine exports the current official SCC Variance Form (PDF dated Oct 6, 2026; in the app (PR #16, in review), once every gap is closed, Check completeness downloads the Council's October 2026 variance form, filled in by the engine, unsigned, officer Part 2 blank, with a notice that it is not a finding that the design complies; Download draft record separately saves a .txt draft record), the form IB-2026-10-01 requires with every portal registration, rather than the older Appendix 1 template in the challenge brief. Every difference is mapped in §3 ("Export target").**

**Path:**
- Oct 29: demo build.
- Nov 10: semi-finals, with the agent-run testing evidence.
- Nov 26: pilot build hosted in a Canadian region.
- Dec 1: **target** of a pilot agreement in principle with one accredited organization (the authority having jurisdiction, per §3).
- Dec 2 – Mar 2: pilot, if agreed, in the program's implementation window, which the program says is "not guaranteed" ([onboarding guide](https://edmontonunlimited.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Built-World-Challenge-Onboarding-Guide.docx.pdf)).

**Real-user rounds begin only if a sponsor engages; none are scheduled, and we have contacted no one.** What *is* scheduled is agent-run evidence: scripted AI-agent walkthroughs before and after each fix (§7) and keyed benchmark runs (§6).

## 2. Why us / approach
**Our approach.** We built Batatis source-first: every value traces to the form, the code database or the applicant's own documents. Reading the regulator's own data and bulletins is how we found the Oct 6 form change and the soffit-article concentration in variances ([SCC Variance Data Hub](https://www.safetycodes.ab.ca/data-hub/variance-data/)). The pattern isn't new: the City of Calgary reviewed and accepted 382 zero-lot-line alternative-solution requests between Jan 1 and Sep 1, 2017, when 26 home builders were building that product ([Calgary advisory, Sep 6, 2017](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/pda/pd/documents/building/variances/advisory-new-standing-posted-variance-procedure.pdf)).

- **Source-traced by design.** A citation must quote the code text word for word or it is dropped. Section C attributions are lookups. Section D professional evidence is never invented. The product never states compliance.
- **We caught the Oct 6, 2026 SCC form change.** The export field map is fingerprinted to that exact file (§3).
- **Officer-side alternative-solution review** is the part of the workflow none of the code-AI products we reviewed advertises (Business Plan, §7). It is our next build focus.

## 3. How it fits current workflows
**Today:**
- **Calgary:** the applicant discusses the need with an SCO, then submits the variance form and supporting documents (digital preferred). The City reviews, may ask for more information, then accepts or rejects. The variance is tied to a building permit ([Calgary guide, approved Nov 23, 2023](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/tools-and-resources/guide-to-applying-for-an-alternative-solution.pdf)).
- **Edmonton:** variance requests go in with the building permit application. Edmonton directs applicants to the SCC variance form (one city page still links the August 2026 version). The City's permit requirements page says "A specific variance may be granted if a proposal for an alternative solution demonstrates at least the minimum level of performance required by NBC(AE) Division B in the areas defined by the objectives and functional statements attributed to the applicable acceptable solution(s)", and it offers policy solutions for conditions that "repeatedly require a variance" ([City of Edmonton, house permit application requirements](https://www.edmonton.ca/residential_neighbourhoods/application-requirements-house-permits)). More than 95% of Edmonton applications are submitted online ([City of Edmonton](https://www.edmonton.ca/business_economy/development-permitting-improvements)). Alternative solutions that affect emergency response are peer-reviewed with Fire Rescue Services ([Edmonton guideline](https://www.edmonton.ca/sites/default/files/public-files/COE-IM-GUIDE-0040-v01-20250415%20Guideline%20for%20Alternative%20Solutions%20Affecting%20Emergency%20Services.pdf)).
- **From Dec 1, 2026, everywhere in Alberta:** variances are registered only through the Council's **Orders and Variances Portal** (OVP), by the SCO. Three recent Council bulletins set the rules Batatis is designed around (all read Oct 20, 2026 from the Council's own PDFs):

**Bulletin and form update runbook (planned; part of the Dec 1 milestone in §6).**
1. **Monitor.** Weekly, and before every release, check the Council's [information bulletins index](https://www.safetycodes.ab.ca/council-information/information-bulletins/) and re-hash the [form PDF](https://ebs.safetycodes.ab.ca/documents/webdocs/PI/Variance%20Template%20final.pdf) against the SHA-256 in the field map. The engineering owner on duty does this; a hash mismatch blocks export of the affected form.
2. **Assess impact.** Classify each new bulletin as no change, a wording change, a new gate, or a form change, and record the clause that drives it.
3. **Update gates.** Add or edit the named gate (or a new field map for a new form version) with tests that plant the new gap, and re-run the completeness evals before release.
4. **Record the version.** Each gate carries its bulletin id and date, each field map its form date and hash, and each case records the gate set and form version it was checked against.

**Export target: the current SCC form, not the brief's Appendix 1 (built), and why.** Brief §2.5(a) asks for an export that matches "the format of the Safety Codes Variance Template Part 1, Sections A-D in Appendix 1" ([challenge scope](https://edmontonunlimited.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Building-Codes-Challenge-with-Appendix-.pdf)). Since the brief was published, the Council has posted a revised form: the **SCC Variance Form for Proposed Alternative Solution, PDF dated Oct 6, 2026** (5 pages, 87 fields; the engine-filled PDF is 7 pages, the 5-page form plus appendix; [SCC form](https://ebs.safetycodes.ab.ca/documents/webdocs/PI/Variance%20Template%20final.pdf)). IB-2026-10-01 says "The revised variance form must be completed and included when the variance is registered through OVP", and from Dec 1, 2026 OVP is "the sole acceptable method for registration". An application on the Appendix 1 template would have to be re-keyed onto the revised form before an officer could register it, so we export the revised form. Its field map, `engine/export/field_maps/scc_2026-10.json` on `dev`, carries the SHA-256 of that exact form file. (The bulletin names the "revised Variance Form" without a date; we take it to be the version at the Council's form URL, last modified Oct 6, 2026.)

**Disclosure:** Batatis fills Part 1, Sections A–D of the Safety Codes Council's current Variance Form for Proposed Alternative Solution (PDF last modified Oct 6, 2026), not the older Appendix 1 template in the challenge brief; the Part 1 fields carry the same names, the differences (Section B checkbox meaning, separate Section C and D rows, the removed owner acknowledgement, privacy wording) are listed in the table below, and Batatis never signs, declares or fills any officer field.

The differences, and how each is handled:

| Appendix 1 (brief) | Oct 6, 2026 SCC form (our export) | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Section A: owner, representative, mailing address, date of request | Same four fields, same field names | Mapped (built) |
| Section B checkbox: "for a Jurisdiction and is not site-specific" | Same field, new meaning: "has been submitted to the Safety Codes Council for review for jurisdictional use" | Mapped to the new meaning (built). Batatis does not yet record "jurisdiction, not site-specific" as a fact: **gap, planned** |
| Section B: permit number, municipal address, Lot, Block, Plan, ATS QTR/SEC/TWP/RGE/MER | Same fields | Mapped (built) |
| Section C: applicable code; 5 rows of article, objective, function, intent | Same, but each row has its own fields | Mapped (built). In the Appendix 1 PDF the 5 rows share one field name per column, so a fillable-form export would repeat row 1 in every row: **an Appendix 1 export would need a rendered overlay, planned, not built** |
| Section C explainer paragraph per article (brief §1.c.iii) | No field on either form | Not on either form. An optional explainer per Section C row is in draft PR #12; how it appears in an export (for example, an attached page) is not decided yet |
| Section D: one combined text field (document and responsible person) | 5 rows, each with document and responsible-person fields | Mapped to the 5 rows (built); an Appendix 1 export would join them into one field (planned) |
| Signatures: owner's acknowledgement checkbox ("Yes") covering publication and an equal-or-better-performance declaration | No checkbox; privacy notice cites POPA s.13(1)(b); printed-name fields added | Printed names mapped (built). The owner's acknowledgement and every signature are left for the owner: Batatis never makes a declaration on the owner's behalf |
| Part 2 officer wording: "equal or better performance" | "approximately equivalent or greater safety performance" (the form's Part 2(a) text; IB-2026-10-01 states the same test: a variance "provides approximately equivalent or greater safety performance") | Part 2 is left blank for the officer on every form (built) |

**Decision:** we do not build an Appendix 1-format export for Submission 1. If the judges require it, it is a second field map plus a rendered overlay for Section C (planned).

**Integration, in three phases:**
1. **Pilot, with nothing to install (in development).** The deliverable is designed to be:
   - the **filled SCC form**;
   - a **portal registration package**: an attachment manifest and a redaction check, so that the registering SCO can upload the form and its supporting documents per location (in progress);
   - an **officer completeness report** with a **drafted information request** that the officer edits and sends.

   The officer will be able to run the review on *any* incoming package, including ones not made with Batatis. A report generated on the applicant side is advisory only.
2. **Next: a planned API.** A documented REST API with webhooks, so a municipal permitting system can push applications and pull completeness results.
3. **Later: a sanctioned OVP integration path.** The Council's bulletin says organizations with high volumes "may work with the Council to develop automated interfaces, integrations, or other approved solutions that facilitate the transfer of required information into OVP while maintaining compliance with Council reporting requirements", with details on integration options and technical requirements to follow ([IB-2026-10-01](https://ebs.safetycodes.ab.ca/documents/webdocs/SR/IB-2026-10-01%20Orders%20and%20Variances%20Registration%20Changes.pdf); [ovp.safetycodes.ab.ca](https://ovp.safetycodes.ab.ca/)). Batatis would build that interface together with a partner authority, under the Council's requirements. After that come connectors to permitting platforms and a jurisdiction-portable schema (the engine's case record already has jurisdiction and authority fields; in the app (PR #16, in review), the user picks a jurisdiction, such as Edmonton, which clears "Reviewing authority missing" (#16 @579cd2c)) for local form add-ons and other provinces.

## 4. Architecture, models, hosting and residency
- **Built today:** a FastAPI engine; a read-only SQLite code database for NBC(AE) 2023 (in force per [Alberta.ca](https://www.alberta.ca/building-codes-and-standards)): scripted rebuild from the official PDF (built); CI rebuild in progress. The database is git-ignored; the PDF and code text are never committed. The 2nd printing is pinned in the codedb README on `dev`; the SHA-256 source manifest is in draft [PR #12](https://github.com/simplythatguy/batatis/pull/12), not yet merged; cases in a SQLite JSON store; a React + Vite front end, currently running on fixture data with the engine not yet connected. **Nothing is hosted yet.**
- **Models in the Oct 29 demo build (OpenAI end to end; the switch is in draft [PR #12](https://github.com/simplythatguy/batatis/pull/12), not yet merged):**
  - **code identification:** BM25 keyword search first (local, no model cost), then an OpenAI rerank, then an OpenAI verifier that keeps a citation only if it quotes the code text word for word;
  - **completeness:** rules only, unless the model-assisted judge in draft PR #11 (variant C) ships;
  - **optional, switchable paths the demo build doesn't use:** Jev screening (TypeSafe, at `api.typesafe.ai`; hosting location not published) and an Anthropic verifier. The current `dev` branch still calls these two until PR #12 merges.

  Configured defaults in draft PR #12: `gpt-4.1-mini` for the rerank and `gpt-4.1` for the verifier (each overridable by environment variable). These are model aliases, not yet pinned: model ids are to be pinned to dated snapshots in PR #12 (in progress), and the exact ids are reported with the keyed benchmark run. Each case records the model ids it used. Measured (keyed OpenAI run on Oct 20, 2026, 20 dev cases; cost and latency over 3 repeats; accuracy from a single run (repeat 1); identification step only (excludes the completeness judge and Section D), recorded in draft [PR #12](https://github.com/simplythatguy/batatis/pull/12)): US$0.019676 = C$0.0277 per case for code identification, 5.7 s median and 11.3 s 95th-percentile latency; the keyless BM25 fallback costs C$0. Code identification: top-1 9/11 and recall@5 9/11 on the model-checked dev cases that have a gold article (in-sample, single run, 9 cases without gold excluded); the BM25 baseline gets 5/11; it asks the applicant on 40% of cases. Completeness-judge cost (PR #11): **to be measured**.
- **Pilot:** cases move to managed Postgres, with encrypted file storage in the same region. Everything runs in one container plus a database, in a Canadian cloud region (e.g. AWS Calgary or Montreal, or Azure Canada Central). The front end is served from the same provider.
- **Residency.** Model calls don't stay in Canada by default.
  - **OpenAI:** its Canada region (`ca.api.openai.com`) offers *storage at rest* in Canada but *not processing* in Canada, and requires approval for Modified Abuse Monitoring or Zero Data Retention ([OpenAI data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)).
  - **Jev:** called at `api.typesafe.ai`; TypeSafe doesn't publish the hosting location or data residency.
  - **Anthropic:** we have not confirmed a Canadian processing option.

  For the pilot we therefore send only code text and non-personal facts to any model, or run the **keyless fallback** (BM25, rule-based completeness, deterministic Section C) fully in-region, with lower identification quality.

## 5. Data handling and privacy
- **Stored today:** document metadata only. PDF ingestion with page-linked quotes is being added for the Nov 26 build.
- **Planned per case:** applicant-entered facts, uploaded documents, extracted values (each either exact from a form field or a verified quote with a page reference), and verdict history for re-upload comparisons.
- **Personal information:** owner and applicant names, contact details, site address, and the professional's name and registration. It is designed to stay in the case record and out of prompts, be encrypted at rest, and have role-based access for applicants and officers.
- **Retention (proposed, to be agreed with each authority):** the life of the application plus a period matching the authority's records schedule, with deletion on request.
- **Model providers:**
  - By default, OpenAI doesn't train on API data and keeps abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days. Zero Data Retention requires OpenAI's prior approval ([OpenAI data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)). **We will request Zero Data Retention** for the provider the pilot build uses.
  - Retention terms for the optional providers (Anthropic, Jev) will be confirmed before either is used with pilot data.
- **Law** (designing for compliance, not certifying it):
  - **Private applicants:** Alberta's **PIPA**, which applies instead of PIPEDA within Alberta. **PIPEDA** still applies to cross-border transfers, such as calls to a model API outside Canada ([OPC](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/r_o_p/prov-pipeda/), [OIPC: PIPA](https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/pipa/)).
  - **Municipalities:** Alberta's **Protection of Privacy Act (POPA)**, in force June 11, 2025, which replaced FOIP's privacy part ([OIPC: POPA](https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/popa/)). Its privacy-management-program duties apply from June 11, 2026. When we serve a municipality, its POPA obligations flow to us by contract, and pilot records belong to the public body.

## 9. Demo cases (General Applicability)
All people, companies and products are fictional, and only code and STANDATA citations are real. Article text is checked against the code database build before the demo. **Status:** the case files are on dev (PR #14). What follows is the behaviour each case key (model-checked) expects; it is how Batatis is designed to respond, not tested tool output. The STANDATA check is not built, identification is command-line only on `dev`, and none of the three cases has been run end to end.
1. **Homebuilder:** a 2-storey, unsprinklered Edmonton infill house with three non-standard features. The case key expects the answer to split them instead of proposing three variance requests:
   - **Heated slab:** the under-slab insulation would be covered by province-wide variance **STANDATA 23-BCV-003** (minimum RSI 1.32), so no new request would be needed; the officer confirms ([STANDATA](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/08ac69ae-5639-46a3-83d2-c52246c78621/resource/3915ba54-96dd-4c54-a9c1-63df5c917836/download/ma-standata-variance-building-23-bcv-003-2024-05.pdf)). **The slab edges would still need detailing:** 23-BCV-003 varies only the under-slab value, and the edge and perimeter insulation rules still apply.
   - **Unvented roof:** **probably NOT covered by 23-BCV-017 as drawn** ([STANDATA](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/08ac69ae-5639-46a3-83d2-c52246c78621/resource/2aa7678c-c476-4523-b0c3-36e71256e11b/download/ma-standata-variance-building-23-bcv-017-2025-09.pdf)). The referenced design report covers slopes greater than 2% (this roof is 2%), and the drawn spray-foam-plus-batt assembly isn't one of its listed assemblies. Batatis is designed to propose a redesign or a site-specific alternative solution, never "fine".
   - **Bedroom window:** a departure. Sentence 9.9.10.1.(2) sets a minimum unobstructed opening of 0.35 m² with no dimension under 380 mm (checked against our code database); the drawn window fails on area. Batatis is designed to offer routes inside the cited sentences first (a larger opening, another bedroom window that meets (2), or the sprinkler exception in 9.9.10.1.(1)), and then to draft Sections A–D. **The window would also need fall protection** (second floor, low sill): a guard or an opening control device, for whichever window is chosen.

   **Model-checked; the "you may not need a variance" check that would apply city interpretations is planned, not built.**
2. **Manufacturer:** a nail-base sheathing panel used in an Edmonton house with vinyl siding fastened to the panel. The departure is Sentence 9.27.5.1.(1), which requires cladding to be fastened to framing, furring or blocking; Sentence 9.27.5.1.(2) is the narrow exception that lets vertically applied vinyl siding be fastened to sheathing only where the sheathing meets minimum lumber, plywood or OSB thicknesses (both checked against our code database; the panel is not on that list). Batatis is designed to point to a voluntary CCMC assessment or recognized evaluation as evidence for the authority having jurisdiction, which decides (CCMC assessment "is not a mandatory requirement", [NRC](https://nrc.canada.ca/en/certifications-evaluations-standards/canadian-construction-materials-centre/ccmc-publications/document.html?id=PAS-001&type=policy)), and to the *project-specific* alternative solution in the permit application, as Calgary describes ([Calgary B23-014](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/pda/pd/documents/building/advisories/b23-014-new-products-materials-and-systems-in-building-construction.pdf)). It would not treat the variance as product approval, which Calgary rules out ([Calgary guide 2023](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/tools-and-resources/guide-to-applying-for-an-alternative-solution.pdf)).
3. **General contractor (Part 3):** a 5-storey mixed-use building in Calgary where 46 pipe penetrations were firestopped with a system outside its tested scope. Sentence 3.1.9.1.(1) requires firestops tested to CAN/ULC-S115:2018 (checked against our code database). Because the penetrations are combustible (plastic) piping in a building more than 3 storeys high, Clause 3.1.9.4.(4)(b) also applies: the firestop's F rating must meet the required fire-resistance rating when tested to CAN/ULC-S115:2018 with a 50 Pa pressure differential across it, higher on the exposed side (checked against our code database). Batatis is designed to point first to remediation with tested systems that carry the 50 Pa test; the industry guideline says engineering judgments should not be used in place of tested systems when those are available. Where no tested system exists, it would flag that **an engineering judgment is required**: issued by the firestop manufacturer's qualified technical staff, or by a registered professional engineer working with the manufacturer ([IFC engineering-judgment guidelines](https://firestop.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Guidelines_Firestop_Engineering_Judgments_Rev2024-02.pdf)). That judgment is then accepted by the registered professional responsible for the Part 3 design. Batatis would list the evidence the judgment must contain, without generating any engineering content.

## 10. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Hallucinated citations | Every citation must quote code-database text and is dropped if the quote doesn't match (built in the verifier). Extracted values will be required to match the source bytes once PDF ingestion lands. The tool never states compliance or safety. |
| Model outage, deprecation or provider change | Model ids are to be pinned to dated snapshots in PR #12 (in progress); each case records the model ids it used. The keyless BM25 fallback auto-commits nothing. The provider switch (in progress, draft PR #12) lets us swap providers after re-running evals. |
| Wrong confident identification outside Part 9 | Auto-commit only in Part 9 with p≥0.75 and at most 3 articles, otherwise ask. **Both values are uncalibrated placeholders with no calibration set, so auto-commit stays off in the UI.** No human-verified subset exists or is scheduled; a future human-verified subset is an option if a reviewer becomes available. Widen to other Parts only when evals clear the error bar. |
| NBC reprints or amendments | The code database is rebuilt by script from official sources, with fingerprint checks and a 30-provision source audit, and versioned by printing. Each case records the version it used. |
| Form or registration-rule changes | Form version Oct 2026 is handled with a fingerprinted field map. The three bulletins in §3 are being encoded (draft PR #11, not merged) as named gates, so that a new bulletin becomes a rule change, not a rewrite; the update runbook is in §3. The portal registration package is in progress (draft PR #11). |
| Eval validity | The 60 dev cases are model-checked, with gold labels derived from the rules under test. No human-verified subset exists or is scheduled; all eval labels are model-checked. Engineer-needed flag (PR #8, on dev): rules_only 55/60 in-sample, 1 under-flag; shipped rules_plus_unknown 50/60 in-sample, 0 under-flags (on the 60 text-only dev cases it answered only yes or ask (0 no), so 0 under-flags there is by design; on the 11 model-constructed ngt cases it answered no 8 times with 0 under-flags (in-sample, model-checked, not human-verified)), 27 unknowns sent to the applicant; held-out 7/8 blind (pre-fix), post-fix 4/8 (no longer blind); model-checked, not human-verified. |
| Code-text copyright | NRC code text is linked or quoted minimally and never redistributed. Written NRC terms are needed before commercial use. |
| Model residency | Prompts without personal data, or the in-region keyless fallback (§4). |
| Official template substitutes for us | Build Forward Alberta and SSRIA are producing a template and guide ([SCC](https://www.safetycodes.ab.ca/a-step-forward-for-alternative-solutions/)). We fill it as a form version rather than compete. |
| Officer adoption | Officers stay the decision-maker and edit every information request. Time saved is measured (§7) before we claim it. |
