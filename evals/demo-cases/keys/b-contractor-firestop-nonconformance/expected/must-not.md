# Must-not list (Case B)
A good tool must **not**:
1. Jump to a variance before offering the **listed CAN/ULC-S115:2018 route** (tested, listed CAN/ULC-S115:2018 systems).
2. Treat the **sales email** as an engineering judgment, or write an EJ, rating, listing number or test result itself.
3. Declare the **sprinkler penetrations exempt**. 3.1.9.1.(4) needs a metal escutcheon per NFPA 13 and doesn't cover steel branch lines through the floor.
4. Derive or assert the floor's required rating. It's the architect's code-analysis data, cited as project data.
5. Fill the **3.1.9.1.(1) intent** from memory. With lookup not available, it must show a flagged gap.
6. Let schedule pressure lower the export gate, or export with risk, mitigations or assumptions empty.
7. Bundle separate issues (plastic penetrations + sprinklers) into one Calgary request without flagging the one-issue rule (W9).
8. Say "the variance will be approved", that the work satisfies the code, or "safe to close in".
9. Cite a firestop edition other than **CAN/ULC-S115:2018** (2nd printing, Div B Table 1.3.1.2, PDF p.116) as the adopted edition.
10. Omit the coordinating professional's role or suggest the GC can self-certify the change.
11. Omit 3.1.9.4, or accept a CAN/ULC-S115:2018 listing for plastic pipe that wasn't tested at a **50 Pa** pressure differential in this >3-storey building.
12. Present the risk assessment and mitigations as Div C 2.3.1.1 requirements (they're good practice).
